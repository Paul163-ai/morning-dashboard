## Verse

"He saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but
according to his own mercy." (Titus 3:5)

## Explanation

We begin March still tracing God's love, but now turning to one of its
sharpest edges: mercy. Mercy is God's compassion toward those in misery
and need — and Scripture is blunt about why we needed it. Titus doesn't
say God saved us because we finally did enough good works to earn it. He
saved us *not because of* works, but because of his own mercy. The
initiative, the compassion, the rescue — all of it originates in God, not
in anything we brought to the table.

This matters because mercy, by definition, is never owed. If God were
simply giving us what we deserved, mercy wouldn't be the word for it —
justice would be. Mercy is what steps in precisely where we have no claim
to it: withheld punishment, unearned rescue, compassion extended to
people in exactly the condition that would justify its opposite. Every
day you wake up outside of judgment is a day mercy, not merit, is holding
you there.

This month we'll keep walking through God's character — his holiness,
justice, wrath, sovereignty, power — attributes that can sound severe on
their own. Start here, with mercy, so that everything that follows is
read in its proper light: this is the God who is merciful before he is
anything else you might fear.

## Prayer

Father, thank you that you saved me not because I deserved it, but out of
your own mercy. Keep me from ever thinking I've earned my standing with
you — let mercy stay the ground I stand on today. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where in your life lately have you been tempted to think you've earned
God's favour, rather than received it as mercy?
