## Verse

"I appeal to you, brothers, to watch out for those who cause divisions
and create obstacles contrary to the doctrine that you have been taught;
avoid them." (Romans 16:17)

## Explanation

In this section we turn to two qualities every church is called to pursue
together, in tension that requires real wisdom to hold well: purity and
unity. We start with purity: the church's calling to guard sound
doctrine and holy living, remaining distinct from false teaching and
unrepentant sin, rather than blending indistinguishably into whatever
beliefs or behaviours happen to be culturally acceptable.

Paul's instruction here is direct: actively watch for, and avoid, those
teaching contrary to sound doctrine. This isn't harsh suspicion toward
every disagreement or every believer who thinks differently on secondary
matters; Scripture elsewhere calls for real patience and charity across
genuine differences. It's specifically aimed at protecting the church's
core doctrinal integrity from teaching that actively contradicts the
gospel itself, the kind of departure Paul elsewhere calls "a different
gospel" (Galatians 1:6–9), serious enough to warrant real concern.

This matters because a church that never guards its purity, in the name
of being welcoming or avoiding conflict, eventually loses its ability to
say anything is actually true or false, right or wrong, indistinguishable,
over time, from any other well-meaning community. Purity isn't
unkindness. It's the church actually remaining what it claims to be.

## Prayer

Lord, give your church discernment to guard sound doctrine without
becoming harsh or suspicious of every disagreement. Help me love truth
enough to notice when it's genuinely being compromised. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you ever seen a church slowly drift from sound doctrine in the name
of being more welcoming? What was lost in that drift?
