## Verse

"Repent and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for
the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy
Spirit." (Acts 2:38)

## Explanation

We begin October where September left off: the church's ordinary,
Spirit-appointed channels of grace. Having spent the close of last month on
the word and prayer, we turn now to the two ordinances Christ himself gave
his church: baptism and the Lord's Supper. Grudem calls these "visible
words": physical, tangible acts that preach the gospel to our eyes and
bodies the way Scripture preaches it to our ears and minds.

Baptism is the first ordinance a believer receives, and it is best
understood as a sign and a seal. A sign points to something beyond itself:
here, to union with Christ in his death and resurrection, and to the
washing away of sin. A seal confirms and authenticates what it signifies,
the way a wax seal confirms a letter is genuine. Baptism does not create
the reality it pictures; it visibly marks a reality (union with Christ,
cleansing from sin) that faith has already laid hold of. That distinction
will matter for nearly everything else we look at this month.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for giving your church visible signs of the gospel,
not just words about it. As I think about baptism this month, deepen my
grasp of what it means to be united to you, washed, and marked as yours.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

When you think about your own baptism (or a baptism you've witnessed), do
you tend to think of it as a sign pointing to something real, or as the
saving act itself? Why does that distinction matter?
