## Verse

"You shall worship no other god, for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is
a jealous God." (Exodus 34:14)

## Explanation

Jealousy sounds like a flaw when we describe people — insecurity, a fear
of being replaced. Applied to God, it means something entirely
different, and Exodus makes it his very name: "Jealous." Human jealousy
usually guards something we're not sure we deserve or might lose
unfairly. God's jealousy guards something he's entirely entitled to and
can never actually lose: the exclusive devotion owed to him as the only
true God. It's closer to a husband's righteous jealousy for his wife's
faithfulness than to petty envy — protective of a relationship, not
insecure about status.

This is the attribute behind the first and second commandments, and it's
threaded through the entire Old Testament narrative of Israel's
repeated idolatry. God's jealousy isn't a minor character trait; it's
central to why idolatry is treated as such serious spiritual adultery
throughout Scripture, not merely bad judgment or misguided preference.

For us, God's jealousy is actually good news correctly understood. A God
indifferent to whether we gave our hearts to lesser things — money,
approval, comfort, other people — would be a God who didn't actually love
us enough to want all of us. His jealousy for our worship is the flip
side of his commitment to our good, since idols always disappoint the
hearts that trust them.

## Prayer

Jealous God, thank you that you want all of my heart, not because you're
insecure, but because you love me too much to share me with idols that
will only fail me. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What competes with God for your undivided devotion right now?
