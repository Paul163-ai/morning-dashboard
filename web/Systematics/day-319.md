## Verse

"Since therefore the children share in flesh and blood, he himself
likewise partook of the same things, that through death he might destroy
the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil, and deliver all
those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery." —
Hebrews 2:14–15

## Explanation

This passage names something most people carry without examining it: a
lifelong, low-grade slavery to the fear of death, shaping decisions,
ambitions, and anxieties in ways rarely traced back to their actual root.
Hebrews' claim is startling — Christ became fully human, sharing our flesh
and blood, specifically so that through his own death he could break this
particular chain, disarming the one who held power over death and
freeing everyone enslaved to fearing it.

This is not a promise that Christians will feel no fear when death draws
near; Christ himself was deeply troubled facing his own (John 12:27). It
is a promise that fear of death no longer has to rule a life, quietly
dictating choices out of self-preservation rather than faithfulness. A
believer who has genuinely grasped that death has been defeated at its
root can take risks for Christ, forgive freely, hold possessions loosely,
and face their own mortality without the slavery Hebrews describes —
not because death has become nothing, but because its power to enslave
has been broken.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for breaking the power of the fear of death by
facing death yourself. Free me from decisions made out of that fear, and
give me courage to live for you unashamed of the risk. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where in your life might fear of death — of loss, of risk, of the
unknown — be quietly shaping decisions more than you've realised?
