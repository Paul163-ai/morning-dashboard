## Verse

"For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and
unrighteousness of men." (Romans 1:18)

## Explanation

Wrath is likely the most uncomfortable attribute we'll cover this month —
and for many readers, the one hardest to reconcile with a God of love.
But Scripture never treats them as competitors. Wrath is God's holy,
settled opposition to sin — not a loss of temper, not an emotional
outburst he later regrets, but the necessary response of a perfectly
good and just God toward what actually harms the people and world he
loves. A God who felt nothing toward cruelty, oppression, and evil
wouldn't be more loving; he would simply be indifferent, and
indifference is not a virtue.

Think of it this way: you'd rightly question the character of a parent
who felt no anger at all watching their child being harmed. That anger,
rightly directed and proportionate, is an expression of love for the one
harmed, not a contradiction of it. God's wrath works the same way — his
holy opposition to sin is inseparable from his love for those sin
damages and destroys.

Romans 1:18 says this wrath is "revealed" — not hidden, not merely
threatened for some distant future, but a present reality against
ongoing rebellion. That should sober us. But it should also point us
forward, because Paul doesn't leave wrath as the final word — tomorrow we
see exactly where it was fully absorbed.

## Prayer

Holy God, thank you that your wrath against sin flows from your love for
what sin destroys. Help me take sin as seriously as you do, without
losing sight of the mercy that meets me too. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does God's wrath toward sin feel to you like a contradiction of his love,
or an expression of it — and why?
