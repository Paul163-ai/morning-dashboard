## Verse

"Therefore, as one trespass led to condemnation for all men, so one act
of righteousness leads to justification and life for all men. For as by
the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners, so by the one
man's obedience the many will be made righteous." (Romans 5:18-19)

## Explanation

We close this short section on original sin exactly where Paul's own
argument was heading all along. The doctrine that Adam's sin brought
condemnation to all he represented is only half of Paul's point: the
half that, on its own, is nothing but bad news. The other half is the
whole reason he raises Adam at all: Christ functions the very same way,
as a representative head, except his "one act of righteousness" brings
justification and life to everyone united to him, exactly as Adam's one
trespass brought condemnation to everyone united to him.

This is sometimes called the doctrine of the "two Adams," and it's one
of the most structurally important parallels in all of Paul's theology.
It means your standing before God was never primarily about your own
private record of good and bad deeds, weighed on some cosmic scale. It's
about whose "one act" you are joined to: Adam's disobedience, or
Christ's obedience. Union with Adam brought a corruption and guilt you
didn't personally choose. Union with Christ brings a righteousness you
didn't personally earn either: received, in both cases, not by merit,
but by representation.

This is the real comfort tucked inside a hard doctrine: the same
mechanism that condemned you in Adam is precisely what saves you,
completely, in Christ.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you that your one act of obedience does for me exactly
what Adam's one act of disobedience did against me, except it saves
rather than condemns. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you tend to think of your standing before God in terms of your own
record, rather than in terms of whose "one act" (Adam's or Christ's)
you are united to?
