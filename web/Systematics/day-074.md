## Verse

"This is good, and it is pleasing in the sight of God our Savior, who
desires all people to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the
truth." (1 Timothy 2:4)

## Explanation

Alongside God's decreed will sits a second sense Scripture uses just as
clearly: God's desired will (also called his revealed or moral will) —
what he commands and delights in, and calls us to align our lives with,
regardless of whether it's ultimately carried out. Paul says God
"desires all people to be saved," yet Scripture is equally clear not
everyone is. That tension isn't a contradiction to be explained away; it
reflects two distinct, biblically grounded senses of "will" operating at
once.

God's revealed will is what fills the commands of Scripture — love your
neighbour, do not steal, believe the gospel. It tells us plainly what
pleases him and what he calls every person to do, without that call
being some kind of insincere performance. When someone rejects the
gospel, they are resisting what genuinely pleases God and what he
genuinely desires for them — his revealed will is real, not a formality.

Distinguishing decreed will from desired will keeps us from two errors:
treating everything that happens as automatically what God delights in
(his decreed will can encompass things he does not delight in, like
sin), and assuming his commands are somehow less than sincere just
because his sovereign plan is broader than any single choice.

## Prayer

Lord, you desire good for me and for all people, and you have made your
will plain in your Word. Help me align my life with what you have
revealed, trusting your wider purposes to you. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a command in Scripture you know reflects God's desired will for
you, but you've been slow to obey?
