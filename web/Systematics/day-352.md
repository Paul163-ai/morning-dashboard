## Verse

"And I saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from
God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband." (Revelation 21:2)

## Explanation

The direction of movement in this vision is easy to miss and important to
notice: the city comes down, from heaven to earth, not the other way
around. The popular image of believers being taken away to a distant
heavenly realm, leaving earth behind for good, is not the picture John
actually gives. God's dwelling place descends and joins the renewed
earth; heaven and earth, long separated by sin, are brought together in
the very next verse — "Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man"
(21:3).

This is the culmination of the whole story Scripture has been telling,
going back to Eden, where God walked with Adam and Eve in the garden
before sin drove a wedge between heaven and earth. The new Jerusalem
descending pictures that original nearness restored, not on a smaller,
Edenic scale but on the scale of an entire renewed creation, an entire
city, structured and described with the language of a bride prepared for
her wedding. The ending Scripture points toward isn't escape upward, but
God coming all the way down to dwell, permanently, with his people on a
renewed earth.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the promise that you are coming to dwell with your
people, not simply taking us away from where we are. Let that shape my
hope toward nearness, not distance. Amen.

## Reflection Question

How does picturing God's dwelling place descending to a renewed earth,
rather than believers ascending away from it, change your mental picture
of "going to heaven"?
