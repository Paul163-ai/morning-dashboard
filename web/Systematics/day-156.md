## Verse

"...who was delivered up for our trespasses and raised for our
justification." (Romans 4:25)

## Explanation

Yesterday we saw the resurrection as the Father's vindication of Christ
himself. Paul's summary here adds something equally important: the
resurrection also secures our justification, our being declared
righteous before God. Notice the careful pairing: delivered up for our
trespasses (the death, dealing with our guilt) and raised for our
justification (the resurrection, securing our righteous standing). The
cross and the resurrection are not two separate, loosely connected
events; they are two inseparable halves of a single saving act.

Why does the resurrection specifically matter for our justification,
rather than the death alone being sufficient? Because a Saviour who
stayed dead would offer no proof his sacrifice had actually been
accepted. The resurrection is the Father's confirmation that Christ's
payment for sin was sufficient and received: if death had continued to
hold him, we would have no assurance the debt was truly paid. His rising
is the receipt, so to speak, proving the transaction complete.

This means our confidence before God rests not only on a completed
death, but on a demonstrated, risen life. Union with a merely dead
saviour secures nothing. Union with a risen one secures everything,
which is precisely the confidence Paul wants his readers, and us, to
have.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, your resurrection is the proof that my justification is
secure. Let me stand today not just on your death, but on your
demonstrated, risen life. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you tend to think about your justification mainly through the cross,
without also resting on the resurrection that confirms it?
