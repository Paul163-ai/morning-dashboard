## Verse

"And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his
glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and
truth." — John 1:14

## Explanation

We turn this month from the doctrine of man to the doctrine of Christ —
and there is no better starting point than this single, staggering
verse. John has just spent thirteen verses establishing that "the Word"
was with God, and was God, present at creation itself (John 1:1-3). Then,
without softening any of that, he says the Word "became flesh." Not
appeared to be human, not temporarily borrowed a body, but actually
became what we are, while remaining fully what he always was.

This is the incarnation: the eternal Son of God taking on a genuine
human nature, so that from a certain point in history onward, the same
person who spoke the universe into being also grew tired, felt hunger,
wept at a friend's grave, and bled. John doesn't treat this as a
diminishment of glory — quite the opposite: "we have seen his glory,"
precisely in the Word made flesh. The incarnation isn't the Son setting
his glory aside to become approachable; it's his glory being displayed
in an entirely new way, "full of grace and truth."

This month traces why this happened, what it means for Christ to be
truly God and truly man, and what he accomplished as a result. Start
here: the almost unbelievable claim that God himself became one of us.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for becoming flesh and dwelling among us. Help me
never treat the incarnation as ordinary — it is the most extraordinary
thing that has ever happened. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What part of "the Word became flesh" do you find hardest to truly grasp,
rather than just accept as a familiar phrase?
