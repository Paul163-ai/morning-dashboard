## Verse

"And they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and the
fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers." — Acts 2:42

## Explanation

The second traditional mark of a true church is the right administration
of the sacraments — baptism and the Lord's Supper — the two ordinances
Christ himself instituted for his church to practice. We'll spend
dedicated time on the meaning of each later this month, but for now,
their presence and proper practice matters as a distinguishing mark in
its own right: a church genuinely obedient to Christ's own commands will
baptise believers and regularly gather at the Lord's table, treating both
with the seriousness and biblical shape Christ established, rather than
neglecting them or distorting their meaning.

Luke's summary of the earliest church in Acts 2:42 places "the breaking
of bread" alongside apostolic teaching, fellowship, and prayer, as one
of the essential, defining practices of that first gathered community —
not an occasional add-on, but a regular, central feature of what it
meant to be the church together from its very earliest days.

This mark works alongside the first: right preaching establishes what
the church believes; right practice of the sacraments embodies and
enacts what it believes, visibly and physically, in the actual gathered
life of the congregation. Together, word and sacrament form the visible,
recognisable shape of genuine church life across history.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for giving your church baptism and the Lord's Supper, not
as optional extras, but as central practices you instituted yourself.
Help me approach both with the seriousness they deserve. Amen.

## Reflection Question

How regularly and thoughtfully do you engage with the Lord's Supper when
your church observes it — as a meaningful practice, or as routine?
