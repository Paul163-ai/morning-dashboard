## Verse

"For the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord
as the waters cover the sea." — Habakkuk 2:14

## Explanation

Postmillennialism holds that Christ returns after ("post") a golden age
in which the gospel's advance gradually transforms whole societies,
bringing about something like the peace and righteousness the millennium
describes through the ordinary spread of the gospel and the church's
faithful influence over history, before Christ returns to a world
substantially won for him. This view leans heavily on promises like the
one above and takes with full seriousness the "already" side of Christ's
present reign — expecting it to grow, not merely hold steady, until it
visibly fills the earth.

Three views, then, all held by careful readers of the same Bible:
Christ returning before a literal earthly reign, Christ already reigning
now in a present, spiritual sense until his direct return, and Christ's
kingdom advancing through history toward a golden age before his return.
Each view takes real biblical texts seriously; each faces real
questions from texts that seem to fit another view more easily. What all
three share, and what matters more than resolving between them, is total
confidence in Christ's ultimate, certain victory over every enemy,
including death itself — the final judgment and eternal state we turn to
next are not in question for any of these views, only the shape of the
road that leads there. As with baptism and church government earlier
this year, this is a place for firm hope and held convictions without
contempt for those who read the same texts and arrived somewhere else.

## Prayer

Lord, whatever the road looks like, I trust the destination: your total
and certain victory. Give me charity toward believers who read these
promises differently than I do. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Having heard all three views in this section, which one do you find most
compelling, and can you name honestly what still troubles you about it?
