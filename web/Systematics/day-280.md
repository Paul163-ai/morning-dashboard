## Verse

"And he took him the same hour of the night and washed his wounds; and he
was baptized at once, he and all his family." — Acts 16:33

## Explanation

Fairness requires we hear the strongest case for infant baptism, held by
many faithful, Bible-loving churches across church history. The argument
runs like this: in the Old Testament, circumcision was administered to
believing Abraham and, on the same day, to his infant son — the sign of
the covenant given not on the basis of the child's own faith, but because
the child belonged to a covenant household. The New Testament, on this
view, doesn't abolish that pattern; several household baptisms (the
Philippian jailer's, Lydia's, Stephanas's) suggest whole families,
presumably including children, received the sign together when the head
of the household believed.

The theological weight behind this view is that baptism, like
circumcision, marks a child's belonging to the visible covenant community
and to the promises of God — a sign held in hope, calling that child to
grow into the personal faith the sign anticipates, much as a birth
certificate names a child before the child can read it. Churches holding
this view do not believe baptism saves infants apart from later faith; they
believe it appropriately marks children of believers as within the sphere
of God's covenant promises and the nurture of the church, the way
circumcision once did.
