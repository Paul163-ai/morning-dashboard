## Verse

"And he put all things under his feet and gave him as head over all
things to the church, which is his body, the fullness of him who fills
all in all." (Ephesians 1:22–23)

## Explanation

We close this long study of the church at the verse that holds every
piece of it together. The church is Christ's own body, filled by the one
who fills everything — which means the church's whole life, everything
we've traced across these last ten sections, flows from a fullness that is
not its own. We began in September with what the church simply is: not a
building but a gathered, Spirit-indwelt people, marked by true preaching,
sacraments, and discipline, held together in purity and unity under
Christ's headship, however that government is structured, however its
membership and eldership are formed. In October we turned to the
ordinary means Christ appointed to strengthen that people — baptism and
the Supper, the gathered worship that rehearses heaven, the gifts the
Spirit distributes for the body's common good. This month, we've traced
where all of it points: outward, into the world, as a people sent the way
Christ himself was sent.

None of it was ever the church working under its own power. Every
doctrine here — nature, government, ordinances, gifts, mission — describes
the shape a body takes when its head is genuinely Christ, filling it,
directing it, and, as tomorrow's reading turns to consider, the very body
that same Christ is coming back for. The doctrine of the church has never
really been a subject on its own; it has been an extended answer to what
it looks like when Christ fills all in all. From here, the year turns
toward the last things — what becomes of that same filled, gathered,
sent people when this age finally ends.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for filling your church — its worship, its gifts,
its mission — with your own presence and power. As I look toward what
comes next, keep my hope fixed on you, the head who fills all in all.
Amen.
