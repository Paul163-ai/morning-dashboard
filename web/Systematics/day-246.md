## Verse

"You also, as living stones, are being built up as a spiritual house, to
be a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God
through Jesus Christ." (1 Peter 2:5)

## Explanation

We turn now to an entirely new section: the doctrine of the church. It's
worth starting with a basic but important correction to how the word is
often used in everyday speech. "Church" in the New Testament never
primarily refers to a building, a denomination, or an institution — it
refers to people: those called out and gathered by God, united to Christ
and to one another. Peter's image is deliberately organic and personal —
"living stones," not dead building material, each individually part of a
larger structure that is, itself, alive.

The Greek word behind "church," ekklesia, simply means an assembly or
gathering of people called out for a purpose. This grounds the church
firmly in relationship, not real estate. You don't primarily "go to
church" the way you'd go to a building; if you belong to Christ, in the
fullest, most important sense, you are the church, together with every
other believer united to him.

This matters practically. A building can be beautiful or plain,
elaborate or simple, without changing anything essential about whether
genuine church is present. What makes a church a church isn't
architecture — it's people, gathered around Christ, being built together
into something alive.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that your church is people, not buildings — living
stones, gathered around you. Help me think of "church" in the days ahead the way
Scripture does: as people I belong to, not just a place I go. Amen.

Do you tend to think of "church" primarily as a building or event, or as
the actual people you're united to in Christ?
