## Verse

"But when Christ had offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins,
he sat down at the right hand of God." — Hebrews 10:12

## Explanation

The ascension is followed by what theologians call Christ's session —
his sitting down at the Father's right hand, the place of highest
honour and authority. Notice what Hebrews pairs this posture with: a
completed sacrifice. Old Testament priests never sat down in the
tabernacle; there were no chairs, because their sacrificial work was
never finished, requiring endless repetition. Christ sits, because his
"single sacrifice for sins," which we studied at length in May, needed
no repetition — it was, as he said from the cross, finished.

The right hand also signifies active reigning authority, not passive
retirement. Christ isn't seated because his work is over in every sense
— he continues to reign, intercede (a theme for later this month), and
govern all things, which we'll see connects directly to his ongoing
kingship. But his sitting specifically marks the atoning work itself as
completed and accepted, in sharp contrast to every priest who came
before him, whose work was never finished enough to allow for rest.

This is a fitting image to hold alongside "it is finished" from last
month: not only declared complete in words, but visibly, posturally
confirmed — a seated king, whose atoning work required, and will ever
require, no further addition.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you sit at the Father's right hand because your sacrifice
truly needed no repetition. Let your finished, seated authority settle
my heart today. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does the image of Christ seated, rather than standing in ongoing
sacrificial labour, deepen your confidence that his atoning work is
truly finished?
