## Verse

"And he said to him, 'Truly, I say to you, today you will be with me in
paradise.'" (Luke 23:43)

## Explanation

The "intermediate state" is the theological term for what happens to a
believer between physical death and final resurrection (a doctrine we'll
study later this year, when we reach the doctrine of the future).
Jesus' promise to the repentant thief on the cross beside him gives one
of the clearest pictures Scripture offers: immediate, conscious
fellowship with Christ, not a gap of unconscious waiting or nonexistence
between death and some far-future reunion. "Today," Jesus says, not
"eventually," not "after a long sleep."

This matters because some traditions have taught "soul sleep": an
unconscious state between death and resurrection. Scripture's more
consistent picture is conscious presence with Christ immediately upon
death, awaiting the fuller, physical reality of bodily resurrection still
to come (which we'll trace later this year under glorification). The
intermediate state is real, genuine, and conscious, but it is also,
importantly, intermediate: not yet the final state, since the full
resurrection body and the new creation still lie ahead.

This should shape how you think of believers who have died before you:
not as absent, sleeping, or in some vague, undefined waiting room, but
genuinely, consciously present with Christ right now, in real, if not
yet complete, joy.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for the promise you gave the thief beside you:
"today, with me." Comfort me with that same promise, both for myself and
for believers I love who have already gone before me. Amen.

## Reflection Question

If you've lost a believing loved one, does picturing them consciously,
presently with Christ right now change how you carry that grief?
