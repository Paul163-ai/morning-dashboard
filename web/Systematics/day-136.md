## Verse

"He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth." (1 Peter
2:22)

## Explanation

Peter writes this in a letter largely addressed to believers facing
unjust suffering, holding up Christ as the pattern to follow. It's
worth noting the specific detail chosen: not just his actions ("he
committed no sin") but his speech ("neither was deceit found in his
mouth"). Sinlessness, in Peter's description, reached into the small,
easily-overlooked corners of daily conduct, not only avoiding dramatic
wrongdoing, but never once, across an entire public ministry under
constant scrutiny from hostile critics, being caught in deceit or
dishonesty.

This is a genuinely remarkable historical claim. Christ's enemies were
actively looking for grounds to discredit him, and found none they
could substantiate, ultimately having to resort to false witnesses who
"did not agree" even with each other (Mark 14:56). A sinless life lived
in complete privacy would be one thing; a sinless life lived under
constant, hostile public examination and still coming through unmarked
is another entirely.

Peter's purpose in raising this isn't merely to admire Christ from a
distance, but to hold him up as the pattern for believers facing their
own unjust suffering (1 Peter 2:21-23), entrusting themselves, as he
did, "to him who judges justly," rather than repaying evil for evil.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, your sinlessness held under public scrutiny, not just
private virtue. Help me follow your pattern when I face unjust
treatment of my own. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where might you be tempted toward small deceits, in speech or
otherwise, that Christ's example calls you to resist?
