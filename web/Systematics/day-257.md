## Verse

"Eager to maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace. There is
one body and one Spirit... one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and
Father of all." — Ephesians 4:3–6

## Explanation

Alongside purity, the church is called to unity — genuine, active,
guarded oneness among believers, grounded not in uniformity of
personality or preference, but in the shared, deep realities Paul lists:
one body, one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God.
Notice the verb Paul chooses: not merely accepting unity as a pleasant
byproduct of shared belief, but being "eager to maintain" it — active,
deliberate effort, because unity, left untended, tends to erode.

This matters because division within the church contradicts something
central to what the gospel itself accomplishes — breaking down walls of
hostility, uniting people across every division that would otherwise
separate them (Ephesians 2:14). A church marked by constant infighting,
factions, and division isn't simply having an unfortunate season; it's
failing to display something the gospel itself is meant to visibly
demonstrate to a watching world (John 17:21).

Held alongside yesterday's mark, purity, this creates real, necessary
tension: guard sound doctrine, and pursue genuine unity, without letting
either concern quietly cancel out the other. That tension, and how to
hold it well, is exactly where we turn tomorrow.

## Prayer

Lord, make me eager to maintain the unity of your Spirit, not passive
about it. Show me where I've contributed to unnecessary division, and
give me grace to pursue genuine oneness with other believers. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a relationship within your church community currently marked
by division or coldness that you could take a step toward repairing?
