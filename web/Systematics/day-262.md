## Verse

"This is why I left you in Crete, so that you might put what remained
into order, and appoint elders in every town as I directed you."
(Titus 1:5)

## Explanation

The second major pattern is presbyterian government (from the Greek
presbyteros, "elder"): leadership held by a plurality of qualified
elders, both within a local church and often connected together across
multiple churches through regional bodies (presbyteries or synods)
that share oversight and accountability beyond any single congregation
acting entirely on its own. Paul's instruction to Titus assumes exactly
this pattern: elders appointed, plural, in every town, forming a
structure of shared, connected oversight.

Proponents point to the consistent New Testament pattern of plural
elder leadership (never a single, unaccountable pastor ruling alone) and
to examples like the Jerusalem council in Acts 15, where representatives
from different churches gathered to resolve a significant doctrinal
dispute together, as a model for connected, cross-congregational
accountability rather than every local church operating in complete
isolation.

This model's particular strength is broader accountability: a single
local church's leadership isn't left entirely to its own judgment,
insulated from correction or support beyond its own walls, but connected
into a wider structure of shared oversight, meant to guard against both
isolated error and unaccountable local power.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the pattern of plural, connected leadership found
throughout the New Testament. Help me value accountability structures,
even ones outside my own local church, as genuine protection. Amen.
