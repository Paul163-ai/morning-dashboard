## Verse

"I am the LORD; that is my name; my glory I give to no other, nor my
praise to carved idols." (Isaiah 42:8)

## Explanation

Yesterday's jealousy was relational — God's guarding of his people's
devotion. Today's verse widens the lens: God's jealousy also extends to
his own glory itself. He will not share the praise due him with anything
else, whether an idol carved from wood or a human achievement dressed up
as self-sufficiency.

This can sound, on first hearing, uncomfortably self-centred — as if God
were insecure about his reputation. But remember what glory actually
means here: the true, accurate recognition of who God really is. For God
to "give his glory to another" wouldn't just dent his ego; it would mean
letting a lie stand as though it were true — crediting an idol, or human
pride, with what only God has actually done. His refusal to do that isn't
vanity; it's simply his commitment to reality, and to justice for
himself as the only one who actually deserves the praise.

This attribute also reframes what our own worship is for. We don't
worship God because he has a need our praise fills — nothing we could
withhold would diminish him. We worship because withholding it would mean
living out of step with what's actually true, giving credit for grace,
provision, and rescue to something — or someone — other than the one who
actually supplied it.

## Prayer

Lord, you alone deserve the glory for every good thing in my life. Forgive
me for the times I've quietly taken credit that belongs to you, and help
me give you the praise that is rightly yours. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where have you recently taken credit — even quietly, even to yourself —
for something that was really God's doing?
