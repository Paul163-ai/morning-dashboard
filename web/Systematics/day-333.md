## Verse

"He must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet." (1
Corinthians 15:25)

## Explanation

Amillennialism ("no millennium," in the sense of no separate future
earthly era) holds that the thousand years of Revelation 20 is not a
literal future period but a symbolic description of the present church
age — the time between Christ's first coming and his return, during which
Satan is already bound in the specific sense of being restrained from
deceiving the nations and preventing the gospel's advance (Revelation
20:3, echoing Christ's own language of binding the strong man in Matthew
12:29), and departed believers already reign with Christ in heaven
(Revelation 20:4), even now.

This view's strength is its consistency with how the rest of Revelation
handles numbers and imagery symbolically throughout, and with the New
Testament's broader pattern of describing Christ's kingdom as already,
genuinely present now, however incompletely, rather than deferred
entirely to a future era (Colossians 1:13, Christ's kingdom already
transferring believers into it). On this reading, we are living, right
now, in the very "thousand years" John describes — a long, indeterminate
period in which the gospel advances despite real opposition, until
Christ's return ends the present age directly and ushers in the eternal
state, with no separate earthly millennium in between.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that your reign is not only future but already, truly
begun. Let me live now in the confidence that you are already putting
your enemies under your feet. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does it change your sense of the present to think you might already be
living inside the very period Revelation 20 describes?
