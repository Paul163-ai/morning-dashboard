## Verse

"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." (Romans 3:23)

## Explanation

Alongside John's definition of sin as lawlessness, Paul offers a
different, complementary image: falling short. The Greek word behind
"sin" throughout the New Testament carries the sense of missing a mark:
an archer's arrow landing short of, or wide of, the target. Applied to
sin, the target is nothing less than "the glory of God," the perfect
reflection of his character we were made to display as his
image-bearers. Sin, in this picture, isn't only breaking a rule; it's
failing to hit the standard of glory we were designed for.

What makes Romans 3:23 so sobering is its universality: "all have
sinned," without exception. This isn't a verse about especially bad
people falling short of an unusually high bar. It's a verse about every
person, including the most respectable and moral, falling short of a
standard that was always going to be God's own perfect glory, not merely
"better than average." No amount of comparing ourselves favourably to
others closes that gap, because the target was never other people to
begin with.

This should end any illusion of self-sufficient goodness before God, and
at the very same time, it's the necessary setup for the gospel: if the
gap is as universal and as real as Paul says, then whatever bridges it
will have to come from outside ourselves entirely.

## Prayer

Lord, I have fallen short of your glory, not just of other people's
standards. Thank you that the gospel doesn't ask me to close that gap
myself. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you been measuring your standing before God by comparison to other
people, rather than by his own perfect glory?
