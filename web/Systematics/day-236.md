## Verse

"For the creation waits with eager longing for the revealing of the sons
of God... that the creation itself will be set free from its bondage to
corruption and obtain the freedom of the glory of the children of God."
(Romans 8:19, 21)

## Explanation

We close this section, and the doctrine of glorification, by widening the
lens beyond individual believers to all of creation itself. Paul says the
created world, groaning under the weight of sin's effects since the fall,
is waiting, eagerly, for something tied directly to believers'
glorification: when God's children are finally revealed in full glory,
creation itself will share in that liberation, freed from its own
bondage to decay.

This means glorification isn't a purely individual, disembodied hope
happening somewhere disconnected from the physical world. It's part of a
much larger renewal: the whole created order, currently subject to
futility and decay because of humanity's fall, finally restored and set
free alongside the people God is restoring. Every part of creation's
groaning (natural disaster, disease, decay, the whole "not the way it's
supposed to be" quality of a fallen world) has an appointed end,
connected directly to the completion of your own salvation.

This should shape a hopeful, rather than dismissive, view of the
physical world you inhabit now. It isn't a disposable stage set on the
way to a purely spiritual destination. It's groaning, waiting, destined
for its own liberation: creation's story and your story, moving toward
the same glorious ending together.

## Prayer

Father, thank you that my glorification isn't disconnected from the
whole created world's own hope. Give me a renewed sense of hope for
creation itself, waiting alongside me for its liberation. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does thinking of creation as "waiting" alongside you change how you view
brokenness in the natural world — illness, disaster, decay?
