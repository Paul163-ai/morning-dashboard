## Verse

"And you will hear of wars and rumors of wars... but the end is not yet.
All these are but the beginning of the birth pains." — Matthew 24:6,8

## Explanation

Jesus does give his disciples signs to watch for — wars, famines,
earthquakes, false messiahs, persecution — but the way he frames them
matters as much as the list itself. These are not a countdown clock,
ticking down to a calculable end; they are birth pains, a labour that
intensifies and recurs without announcing precisely how much longer
remains. A birth pain confirms that the process is underway, not how many
hours are left until delivery.

This framing has protected careful readers of Scripture from two errors
every generation is tempted toward. The first is complacency: treating
wars, disasters, and upheaval as background noise unrelated to Christ's
promised return, when Jesus explicitly connects them. The second, more
common temptation, is treating every war and earthquake as decisive proof
that the end must be imminent, when Jesus explicitly says "the end is not
yet" even as these things occur. Every generation since the ascension has
lived amid genuine birth pains; none of them has been given grounds to
calculate a due date from the contractions alone.

## Prayer

Lord, keep me neither indifferent to the signs of a groaning world nor
falsely certain about what they mean for timing. Let them turn me toward
readiness, not speculation. Amen.

## Reflection Question

When you hear news of war, disaster, or upheaval, does it tend to make
you more watchful and prayerful, or more prone to anxious speculation
about timing?
