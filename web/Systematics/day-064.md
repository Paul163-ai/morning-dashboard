## Verse

"As he who called you is holy, you also be holy in all your conduct,
since it is written, 'You shall be holy, for I am holy.'" (1 Peter 1:15-16)

## Explanation

Yesterday we saw holiness expose Isaiah before it could restore him. But
Peter takes the same attribute in a different, equally important
direction: holiness as invitation, not just verdict. God's holiness isn't
only a fact to tremble before — it's a character to be shaped by. Peter
roots the command directly in God's own nature: be holy *because* he is
holy. Not to earn his acceptance, but because being united to a holy God
inevitably reshapes what we love and how we live.

This is a crucial corrective to two opposite errors. One treats holiness
as an unreachable standard that only produces guilt, so it's quietly
avoided as a topic. The other treats holiness as a performance we
generate to prove our devotion. Peter's logic is neither: holiness is
the natural shape of a life that has actually been brought near to a holy
God. It grows out of relationship, not in place of it.

Practically, this means growth in holiness isn't primarily about
willpower crackdowns on individual sins, though that has its place. It's
about increasingly living in a way that fits who you now belong to —
letting the character of the God who called you shape the conduct of the
life you now live.

## Prayer

Holy Father, you have called me to be holy because you are holy. Shape my
conduct today not out of fear, but because I belong to you and want my
life to fit that reality. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is your pursuit of holiness currently driven more by guilt, performance,
or genuine relationship with God?
