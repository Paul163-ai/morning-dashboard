## Verse

"For in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body — Jews or Greeks,
slaves or free — and all were made to drink of one Spirit." —
1 Corinthians 12:13

## Explanation

In this section we turn to the Holy Spirit's ongoing role in the Christian
life, beginning with a phrase that's caused real confusion across church
history: baptism in the Holy Spirit. Paul's own usage here is
straightforward, even if the phrase has come to carry different meanings
in different traditions: at conversion, every believer, without
exception, is baptized by the Spirit into the one body of Christ — united
to Christ and to every other believer, regardless of background, in a
single, spiritual reality.

This is different from water baptism (an outward sign of an inward
reality) and different from later, repeated experiences of the Spirit's
filling, which we'll look at tomorrow. Paul describes it as something
already true of "all" believers — not a special second experience some
Christians have and others lack, but the universal, one-time reality of
every genuine believer's union with Christ and his church, accomplished
by the Spirit at conversion.

This should offer real reassurance if you've ever wondered whether you're
"missing" some special spiritual experience other Christians seem to
have. If you belong to Christ, you have already been baptized by the
Spirit into his body — fully united, fully included, with nothing further
required to belong.

## Prayer

Holy Spirit, thank you for baptizing me into Christ's body the moment I
believed — fully united, fully included. Let me rest in that completed
reality rather than searching anxiously for something more. Amen.
