## Verse

"Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign. Behold, the virgin
shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." —
Isaiah 7:14

## Explanation

Isaiah's prophecy, written some seven centuries before Christ's birth,
shows the virgin birth was never a later addition to make Jesus' story
more impressive — it was anticipated, as a specific sign, long in
advance. Matthew quotes this very verse when recounting the events of
Jesus' birth (Matthew 1:22-23), presenting it as prophecy genuinely
fulfilled, not retrofitted.

Beyond its predictive fulfillment, the virgin birth matters theologically
in at least two ways worth holding onto. First, it marks Christ's
entrance into the world as a genuine, supernatural act of God — not
merely another notable human birth, but the initiating miracle of the
incarnation itself, fitting for the arrival of God in flesh. Second, it
underlines that salvation, from its very starting point, was God's
initiative and God's doing, not a human accomplishment. Even the way the
Saviour entered the world testifies to the same truth this whole month
will keep returning to: rescue that comes from outside ourselves,
entirely by God's own action.

Far from being an awkward detail to explain away, the virgin birth is a
fitting, deliberate sign — pointing, from the very first moment of
Christ's earthly life, to exactly who he was and what kind of salvation
he came to bring.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that even Christ's entrance into the world testifies to
salvation being your work, not mine. Let that same truth shape how I
receive the gospel today. Amen.

## Reflection Question

How does seeing the virgin birth as a deliberate sign, fulfilled from
centuries-old prophecy, strengthen your confidence in the rest of
Scripture's claims about Christ?
