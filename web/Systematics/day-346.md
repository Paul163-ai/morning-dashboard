## Verse

"And do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul.
Rather fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell." — Matthew
10:28

## Explanation

Of all the New Testament's speakers, Jesus himself speaks about hell more
often, and more directly, than anyone else. This is worth stating plainly
before these readings go further, because hell is a doctrine
believers are often tempted to soften or quietly set aside as an
embarrassment — yet it comes to us largely from the same lips that spoke
the Sermon on the Mount and welcomed children into his arms. If we take
Christ's words on any other subject seriously, honesty requires taking
his words here seriously too, rather than treating them as an
uncomfortable exception to an otherwise gentler teacher.

This verse also reframes what should command our fear. Christ isn't
minimising physical death's seriousness; he's placing it beside something
he considers far weightier — a judgment that reaches soul and body both,
carried out by the one with actual authority over eternal outcomes, not
merely temporal ones. The point isn't to induce panic, but perspective:
the things this world can threaten are real but limited, while the reality
Christ warns of here is not. He asks his followers to fear rightly, not
to fear nothing.

## Prayer

Lord, give me the courage to take seriously what you took seriously, even
where it's uncomfortable. Let a right fear of you replace every lesser
fear this world tries to put in its place. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you tended to treat Jesus's own words about hell differently than
his other teachings? What would it mean to take them with the same
seriousness?
