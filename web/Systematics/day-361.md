## Verse

"No longer will there be anything accursed... They will see his face, and
his name will be on their foreheads." (Revelation 22:3–4)

## Explanation

Across the whole Old Testament, seeing God's face directly was
understood as fatal — even Moses, closer to God than almost anyone, was
told "you cannot see my face, for man shall not see me and live" (Exodus
33:20), and was shown only God's back as he passed by. This wasn't
arbitrary; it reflected a real barrier, sin's incompatibility with
unmediated exposure to God's holiness. John's vision announces that this
ancient barrier is finally, permanently gone: not glimpsed from behind,
not veiled through a mediator, but seen, face to face, by God's own
redeemed people.

The mark on their foreheads — God's own name, replacing whatever mark
sin had left — pictures the completeness of this restored relationship:
fully known as belonging to him, fully able to look on him without the
fear or danger that made such sight impossible before. This is the final
resolution to something the whole year has been building toward: the
God who was once too holy to look upon has made a way, through Christ,
for his own people to see him directly, marked as unmistakably his,
forever.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for making a way for me to one day see your face without
fear. Until that day, let me long for that sight the way Moses once did.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

Moses asked to see God's glory and was given only a partial answer. What
would it mean to you to finally receive the full one?
