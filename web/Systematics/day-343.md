## Verse

"He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no
more, neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore, for
the former things have passed away." — Revelation 21:3–4

## Explanation

Notice the specific, tender action John records: God himself wiping away
every tear, not simply declaring an end to sorrow from a distance, but
personally, individually attending to grief that has accumulated across a
whole lifetime, or a whole history. This is not a heaven pictured as mere
absence — the absence of death, mourning, crying, pain — though it is
certainly that. It is a heaven pictured as presence: God near enough to
touch the specific tears of specific people, one by one.

This matters because grief in this life is rarely abstract; it has names
and faces and particular losses attached to it, each carrying its own
weight. The promise here isn't that grief will simply be forgotten or
declared irrelevant, but that God himself will personally address it, the
way a parent might wipe a child's face after real hurt, not brush past it
as though it never happened. Every tear anyone has ever cried in faith,
over loss, over suffering, over sin's damage to a life or a world, has an
end appointed for it — not just the ending of sadness, but the personal
attention of God to it.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that you will not just end my sorrow but personally
attend to it, tear by tear. Let that tenderness comfort my grief now,
even before that day comes. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What specific grief in your life would you most want God to personally
wipe away? Bring that particular tear to him in prayer today.
