## Verse

"For as in one body we have many members, and the members do not all
have the same function, so we, though many, are one body in Christ, and
individually members one of another." — Romans 12:4–5

## Explanation

Paul's most developed image for the church is the human body — a single
living organism made up of genuinely different, specialised parts,
each necessary, none redundant, all working together under one head.
This image does real theological work. It affirms both real unity (one
body) and real diversity (many members, many functions) at the same
time, without collapsing either into the other.

This means the church was never meant to be uniform — everyone with the
same gifts, the same role, the same way of serving. It was meant to be
unified precisely through its diversity, the way a body functions well
not despite having different organs, but because it does. An eye and a
hand serve the same body in completely different ways, and the body
needs both, functioning as they were each specifically designed to
function.

This should shape how you think about your own place in a local church.
You're not meant to be a copy of someone else's gifting or role, nor is
anyone else meant to replicate yours. You're one member of a body,
individually connected to every other member, contributing something
genuinely needed that no one else contributes in quite the same way.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for making me part of one body with genuinely different
members. Help me see my own gifts and role clearly, and value the
different gifts of others rather than wishing everyone were the same.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

What specific role or gift do you bring to the body of believers you're
part of? Have you been actively using it?
