## Verse

"For the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the
Lord as the waters cover the sea." — Habakkuk 2:14

## Explanation

Mission is sustained not only by looking back to Christ's love but by
looking forward to where history is headed. Habakkuk's promise, given
centuries before Christ, pictures a completeness no partial success could
satisfy — not a few nations reached, not scattered pockets of belief
amid general darkness, but the whole earth filled with the knowledge of
God's glory, as thoroughly as water covers the sea floor. John's vision
in Revelation shows this promise's fulfilment: "a great multitude that no
one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and
languages," gathered before the throne (Revelation 7:9).

That promised ending gives mission a hope no strategy session or funding
shortfall can undermine. The church's task is not to guess whether the
mission will ultimately succeed — Scripture has already answered that —
but to participate faithfully in a work whose completion is certain, in
God's timing, whether or not we live to see it finished. This is why
weariness in mission, real as it is, need never become despair: the
outcome was decided before the task began.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that the completion of your mission doesn't depend on my
success. Let that certainty renew my hope, even when the work feels slow
or unfinished in my lifetime. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does knowing the outcome is certain change how you feel about the parts
of mission that feel slow, discouraging, or unfinished right now?
