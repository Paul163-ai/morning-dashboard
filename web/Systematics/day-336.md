## Verse

"For God has not destined us for wrath, but to obtain salvation through
our Lord Jesus Christ." (1 Thessalonians 5:9)

## Explanation

Tribulation and wrath are not the same thing, and the distinction matters.
Tribulation, in Scripture's ordinary sense, refers to suffering and
hardship — the kind believers have always faced and Christ himself
promised (John 16:33, "in the world you will have tribulation"). Wrath
refers specifically to God's righteous judgment against sin, poured out
in its fullness on the unrepentant. Paul's promise here is precise: God
has not destined believers for that second thing. Christians may and do
suffer real tribulation, but the wrath that judgment ultimately brings has
already been absorbed by Christ on their behalf.

This is why believers facing even the darkest tribulation this world has
promised can hold real hope without denying real suffering. The hardship
is not evidence of God's anger; it is simply what a groaning, unredeemed
world produces, and Christ's own people are not exempt from it any more
than he was. But the wrath that hardship is sometimes confused with —
God's judicial anger against sin — has already been fully satisfied at
the cross for everyone in Christ. Tribulation may come; wrath, for the
believer, will not, because Christ has already borne it.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for bearing the wrath I deserved so completely that
none remains for me. Whatever tribulation this life still holds, let me
face it without confusing it for your anger. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you ever mistaken hardship in your life for a sign of God's
displeasure with you? How does this distinction between tribulation and
wrath correct that?
