## Verse

"No longer will there be anything accursed, but the throne of God and of
the Lamb will be in it, and his servants will worship him. They will see
his face... and they will reign forever and ever." — Revelation 22:3–5

## Explanation

Heaven, in Scripture's own picture, is not an eternity of passive rest —
harps, clouds, idle leisure. It is described here as active: worship, yes,
but also service, and even reigning, forever. Whatever exactly this
future reign involves, it pictures redeemed humanity restored to
something like its original calling in Genesis 1 — given dominion,
purposeful work, genuine responsibility — except now free from every
limitation sin introduced, exercised in unbroken fellowship with God
himself.

This should correct an instinct many Christians carry without examining
it: that heaven will eventually grow monotonous, an eternity of the same
static experience repeated without end. What John describes instead is
dynamic — worship that never grows stale because it's fed by seeing God's
face directly, and purposeful activity that never grows tiresome because
it's no longer weighed down by sin's frustrations (the "thorns and
thistles" of a cursed creation, gone). Eternity, on this picture, is not
an escape from meaningful activity into rest, but the full flourishing of
both together, forever.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for a future that is not empty rest but full, purposeful
life with you. Let me long for that fullness, not just an end to this
life's weariness. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has your own picture of heaven been more static than Scripture's? How
does "reigning forever" change what you're actually anticipating?
