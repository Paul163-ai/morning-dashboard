## Verse

"So whoever knows the right thing to do and fails to do it, for him it is
sin." (James 4:17)

## Explanation

Yesterday's list from Jesus focused on sins of commission: wrong things
actively done. James names an equally real but easier-to-overlook
category: sins of omission, right things left undone. Notice the
specific condition James attaches: this isn't about failing to do
something you never knew about. It's about knowing the right thing to
do, and simply not doing it.

This category is easy to underestimate precisely because it involves no
dramatic action to point to: no lie told, no theft committed, nothing
visibly wrong. But the absence of visible wrongdoing isn't the same as
righteousness. The person who knew a neighbour needed help and didn't
offer it, who knew reconciliation was owed and delayed it, who knew
generosity was called for and withheld it out of convenience: all of
this, James says plainly, is sin, not merely a missed opportunity for
extra credit.

This should prompt an honest, specific kind of self-examination,
different from checking a list of forbidden actions. It's the question:
what do I already know I should be doing, right now, that I'm simply not
doing? Omission rarely feels as urgent as commission, but James gives it
exactly the same weight.

## Prayer

Lord, show me the right things I already know to do but have been
neglecting. Forgive my omissions as seriously as my active wrongs, and
give me courage to act. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What is one thing you already know is right to do, that you have simply
not been doing?
