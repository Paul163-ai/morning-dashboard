## Verse

"And he took bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and gave it
to them, saying, 'This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in
remembrance of me.'" (Luke 22:19)

## Explanation

The second ordinance Christ gave his church, the Lord's Supper, was
instituted on the last night before his death, at a Passover meal thick
with meaning. For centuries the Passover had pointed backward, to a lamb's
blood on doorposts and deliverance from Egypt. Jesus took that meal and
turned it forward, transforming its bread and cup into a new sign pointing
to himself: the true Lamb, whose body and blood would accomplish a greater
deliverance than Egypt ever pictured.

"Do this in remembrance of me" makes the Supper, like baptism, a
Christ-appointed ordinance rather than a tradition the church invented for
itself. It is not a memorial we chose to observe out of sentiment, the way
a family might revisit an old photograph; it is a command, repeated by
Paul as something he "received from the Lord" and "delivered" to the
church (1 Corinthians 11:23). Where baptism marks the beginning of the
Christian life, the Supper marks its continuation: the ongoing meal of a
people who keep needing to remember what Christ has done for them.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for giving your church a meal that keeps drawing us
back to the cross. Let me never take this ordinance as routine, but as the
remembrance you commanded. Amen.

## Reflection Question

When did you last take the Lord's Supper, and did it feel like a genuine
act of remembering Christ, or more like a habit performed without
thought?
