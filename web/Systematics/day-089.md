## Verse

"Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and
let them pray over him... And the prayer of faith will save the one who is
sick, and the Lord will raise him up." (James 5:14-15)

## Explanation

A fair question follows naturally from yesterday: did miracles stop after
the apostolic age, or does God still work this way today? Faithful,
Bible-believing Christians genuinely disagree here, and this devotional
isn't the place to settle that debate — what's worth noting is what all
sides agree on. James, writing to ordinary churches rather than
apostles, instructs believers to call for prayer over the sick,
expecting God to act in response. Whatever view one holds on the
frequency or nature of miracles today, the practice of praying in faith
for God's intervention, trusting him to act however he chooses, is
squarely biblical and never suspended.

What Scripture consistently guards against is either extreme: treating
miracles as so ordinary that God's normal providence is looked down on
as second-best, or treating them as so finished that prayer for God's
active, unusual intervention becomes pointless. James assumes neither.
He simply assumes a God who is still near, still powerful, and still
worth asking.

Whatever your convictions on this question, the invitation is the same
one James gives: bring your need honestly to God in prayer, trusting him
to act in whatever way he sees fit.

## Prayer

Lord, whatever your purposes for miracles today, teach me to pray in
faith, trusting you to act however you choose, without demanding a
particular kind of answer. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a need in your life right now that you've stopped bringing to
God in expectant prayer?
