## Verse

"For our citizenship is in heaven, and from it we await a Savior, the
Lord Jesus Christ, who will transform our lowly body to be like his
glorious body." — Philippians 3:20–21

## Explanation

We turn now to glorification — the final step in the whole
sequence we've been tracing since July: election, calling, regeneration,
conversion, justification, adoption, sanctification, and now, the
completion of it all. Glorification is what happens at Christ's return:
believers receive new, resurrection bodies, fully freed from sin, decay,
and death, made like Christ's own glorious, resurrected body.

This is worth distinguishing carefully from what we studied earlier this
month. The intermediate state (immediate presence with Christ after
death) is real and good, but it isn't yet the final, complete state
Scripture promises. Glorification is that completion: not a disembodied,
purely spiritual existence forever, but full, physical, resurrection
life — the human body itself, redeemed and transformed, never again
subject to sin, sickness, or death.

This matters because it corrects a common but mistaken picture of the
Christian hope as merely "going to heaven when you die," as though
disembodied spiritual existence were the final goal. Scripture's actual
hope is bigger and more physical: a real body, a real, renewed creation
(which we'll touch on in the days ahead), a complete, whole existence,
finally matching what Christ himself already possesses in his own
resurrected body.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you that my hope isn't just disembodied existence, but
full resurrection like your own glorious body. Let that fuller hope shape
how I think about my future. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has your picture of the Christian hope been mostly "going to heaven," or
has it included the fuller promise of bodily resurrection? How does that
change things?
