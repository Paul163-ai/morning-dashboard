## Verse

"When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth,
for he will not speak on his own authority, but whatever he hears he
will speak, and he will declare to you the things that are to come."
(John 16:13)

## Explanation

We turn now to the final section of this month's study: the Holy
Spirit. It's worth establishing something basic but easily overlooked:
the Spirit is a person, with a mind, a will, the capacity to speak,
hear, and guide, not an impersonal force, energy, or influence, the
way electricity or gravity might be described. Jesus' own language here
is thoroughly personal: "he will guide," "he will speak," "he will
declare": the pronouns and verbs are exactly what we'd use to describe
a person acting, not a force operating.

This matters because it's easy, without meaning to, to think of the
Spirit in impersonal terms, as a kind of divine power we access or tap
into, rather than a person we relate to. Scripture consistently resists
that framing. The Spirit can be grieved (Ephesians 4:30), lied to (Acts
5:3-4, a passage we'll look at tomorrow), and blasphemed against
(Matthew 12:31); none of which makes sense of an impersonal force, but
all of which make complete sense of a genuine person, capable of being
personally wronged or personally honoured.

This should reshape how we think about the Spirit's presence in our
lives: not a resource to be used, but a person to be known, honoured,
and related to, exactly as we would relate to the Father and the Son.

## Prayer

Holy Spirit, you are a person, not a force I access. Help me relate to
you today: listening, honouring, and depending on you as I would the
Father and the Son. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you tended to think of the Holy Spirit more as an impersonal power
than as a person to know and relate to?
