## Verse

"One thing have I asked of the LORD, that will I seek after: that I may
dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to gaze upon the
beauty of the LORD." — Psalm 27:4

## Explanation

We close this short cluster with two closely linked attributes: God's
beauty — the sum of all his perfections considered as supremely
attractive and satisfying to behold — and his glory — the brightness and
splendour that surrounds his manifest presence. Together they answer a
question the previous days have been circling: why would anyone actually
want this perfect, blessed God, rather than merely respect him from a
safe distance?

David's request in Psalm 27:4 isn't for protection, or answers, or
provision, though he asks for those things elsewhere. Here his one desire
is simply to gaze — to behold God's beauty for its own sake, the way you
might stand before a masterpiece not to use it for anything but to be
satisfied by looking. This is a striking model for worship: not only
transactional (asking God for things) but contemplative, delighting in
who he is regardless of what he gives.

God's glory, meanwhile, is what breaks into visible display throughout
Scripture — the pillar of fire, the cloud filling the temple, the
transfigured face of Christ. It's the perceivable evidence that this
being we're describing in words is not merely conceptually perfect, but
gloriously, overwhelmingly real. Beauty draws us to gaze; glory leaves us
in no doubt what we're gazing at.

## Prayer

Beautiful and glorious God, give me a heart like David's — one that
wants simply to gaze upon you, not only to ask things of you. Amen.

## Reflection Question

When did you last approach God simply to delight in who he is, rather
than to ask him for something?
