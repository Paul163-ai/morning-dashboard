## Verse

"You who are of purer eyes than to see evil and cannot look at wrong, why
do you idly look at traitors and remain silent when the wicked swallows up
the man more righteous than he?" (Habakkuk 1:13)

## Explanation

Having spent this month on God's holiness, justice, wrath, and
sovereignty, the hardest question comes into full view: if God is
perfectly good and completely sovereign over all things, why does evil
exist at all? Habakkuk doesn't flinch from asking it directly, almost
accusingly — how can a God too pure to look at evil apparently stand by
while it flourishes?

It's worth being honest that Scripture never offers a tidy, fully
satisfying philosophical resolution to this question the way a
textbook might. What it does offer is a God who welcomes the question
rather than punishing it — Habakkuk's complaint stands, unedited, as
inspired Scripture — and who eventually answers, not with a neat
explanation, but with a call to trust his character even before every
piece makes sense (Habakkuk 2:4, "the righteous shall live by his
faith").

This is worth naming plainly: honest wrestling with the problem of evil
is not a failure of faith. Habakkuk models exactly the opposite — bring
your hardest questions directly to God, not away from him. The chapters
that follow this month won't fully resolve the mystery, but they will
show some of the purposes Scripture does reveal for why God permits what
he hates.

## Prayer

Lord, like Habakkuk, I bring my hardest questions about evil and
suffering directly to you rather than away from you. Teach me to trust
your character even where I don't yet have full answers. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What is the hardest question about evil or suffering you've been afraid
to bring honestly to God?
