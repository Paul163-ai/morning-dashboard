## Verse

"...darkened in their understanding, alienated from the life of God
because of the ignorance that is in them, due to their hardness of
heart." (Ephesians 4:18)

## Explanation

We've looked at sin's origin, definition, and specific expressions.
Today we consider its reach: not one faculty affected while others stay
untouched, but the whole person, mind, will, and emotions,
genuinely corrupted by sin. Paul's description here is comprehensive:
darkened understanding, hardened heart, alienation from the life of
God. This is sometimes called total depravity, a term easily
misunderstood: it doesn't mean every person is as evil as they could
possibly be. It means every part of a person (reasoning, desiring,
choosing, feeling) has been affected by sin, with no faculty left
entirely uncorrupted or able to fix the rest on its own.

This has an important implication: the fundamental human problem isn't
merely bad information (as though a clearer explanation would fix
things) or weak willpower (as though more effort would fix things). It's
a corruption running through the very faculties we'd use to reason our
way out, will our way out, or feel our way out. This is precisely why
Scripture never presents self-improvement, education, or willpower as
sufficient solutions to sin: the tools themselves are affected by the
problem they'd need to solve.

This should humble any confidence in self-directed moral repair, and
point, once again, toward a rescue that has to come from outside, a
theme this whole month has been building toward.

## Prayer

Lord, my mind, will, and emotions have all been affected by sin. I
cannot reason, will, or feel my way out of my need for you. Rescue me
from outside myself. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where have you been relying on willpower or self-improvement to fix
something only grace can actually address?
