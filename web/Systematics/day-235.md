## Verse

"Beloved, we are God's children now, and what we will be has not yet
appeared; but we know that when he appears we shall be like him, because
we shall see him as he is." (1 John 3:2)

## Explanation

Glorification isn't only physical transformation; it's the completion
of the character transformation sanctification has been slowly working
toward across a whole lifetime. Everything begun in definitive
sanctification at conversion, and pursued imperfectly through progressive
sanctification across every year since, reaches its finished state here:
full, complete likeness to Christ, not just in body, but in character,
finally, fully free from every remaining trace of sin.

John says this transformation happens specifically through seeing Christ
"as he is." There's something about final, unveiled, direct encounter
with Christ's own glory that completes the transformation no amount of
present effort, however sincere, could fully achieve on its own. This
should be genuinely freeing for anyone discouraged by the slow, uneven,
often frustrating pace of their own present sanctification. The goal was
never to arrive at perfect Christlikeness through your own effort before
glory. Christ himself completes what he began, at the moment you finally
see him fully.

This connects everything this whole month has traced: sanctification's
slow, present work, glorification's final completion: the same
Christlike goal Romans 8:29 named earlier, finally, fully realised.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you that the transformation you began in me will be
completed, fully, when I finally see you as you are. Encourage me in the
slow present work, knowing the finished work is certain. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does the certainty of final, complete transformation change how you feel
about the slow, imperfect progress of your present sanctification?
