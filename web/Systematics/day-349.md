## Verse

"Therefore, knowing the fear of the Lord, we persuade others." (2 Corinthians
5:11)

## Explanation

Paul doesn't let the reality of judgment sit as abstract doctrine; he
lets it move him toward people. Knowing the fear of the Lord — the same
sobering reality these last four days have traced — his response isn't
paralysis, or despair, or a smug sense of being on the safe side of a line
others have missed. It's persuasion: actively, urgently telling people
the truth that could change where they stand. This is the only right use
of the doctrine of hell for anyone who has genuinely understood it.

This is worth closing on plainly. Hell is not a doctrine meant to be
wielded as a threat to frighten people into compliance, nor a fact to be
privately relieved about from a safe distance, nor a subject too
uncomfortable to mention at all. It is meant to produce exactly what it
produced in Paul: compassion urgent enough to act, love serious enough to
say hard things, and a settled resolve to spend whatever time remains
telling as many people as possible about the Christ who has already borne
this judgment for anyone who will trust him.

## Prayer

Lord, let what I know about judgment move me toward people in love, not
away from them in either fear or false comfort. Give me Paul's urgency
and Paul's compassion together. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has this section's study of hell moved you toward greater compassion and
urgency for anyone specific in your life who doesn't yet know Christ?
