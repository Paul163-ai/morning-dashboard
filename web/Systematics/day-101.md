## Verse

"With it we bless our Lord and Father, and with it we curse people who
are made in the likeness of God." (James 3:9)

## Explanation

James is confronting the human tongue's capacity for contradiction: the
same mouth blessing God in worship and cursing a person made in his
image moments later. What makes this "inconsistent" rather than merely
unpleasant is precisely the doctrine we've spent two days establishing:
every person, without exception, is made in God's likeness. To curse
another person isn't simply rude or unloving in the abstract; it's an
assault, however small, on someone bearing the very image of the God
being worshipped in the same breath.

This is where the doctrine of the image of God stops being an
interesting theological idea and becomes a direct, daily ethical
demand. It grounds human dignity in something that cannot be
stripped away by poverty, disability, unpopularity, disagreement, or
even sin, because it was never grounded in those things to begin with.
The person who frustrates you today, the stranger you'll never speak to,
the person easiest to dismiss or mock: all of them bear God's image
exactly as fully as you do.

James's point cuts close because it's so specific to speech: not
abstract social policy, but the words that come out of an ordinary
believer's mouth in an ordinary day. How we speak about and to other
people is, quite directly, a statement about how seriously we take the
image of God.

## Prayer

Lord, forgive me for the times my words have dishonoured someone made in
your image. Help my speech today reflect the dignity you have given to
every person I encounter. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there someone you've spoken about (or to) in a way that doesn't
match the dignity of being made in God's image?
