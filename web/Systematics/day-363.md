## Verse

"And I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the
Almighty and the Lamb." (Revelation 21:22)

## Explanation

Every stage of redemptive history has needed some structure to mediate
access to God — the tabernacle in the wilderness, the temple in
Jerusalem, and, in a different sense, Christ himself, "the temple of his
body" (John 2:21), through whom believers now approach God. Each of
these was a genuine gift, making real access to a holy God possible for
sinful people who could not otherwise draw near. But each was also, by
necessity, a mediating structure — something standing between worshipper
and the fullness of God's presence.

John's vision of the new Jerusalem removes this mediating structure
entirely, not because it failed, but because it is no longer needed:
"its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" — God's own direct
presence replacing every structure that once stood in for it. This is
the final removal of every barrier this whole final section of the year
has been tracing the undoing of: no curse, no tears, no temple, because
nothing remains that needs to be kept at a mediated distance from a
holy God. What Christ's own body accomplished as the meeting place
between God and his people becomes, at last, simply how things are,
everywhere, permanently.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for being the temple that made my access to God
possible now. I long for the day when even that mediation gives way to
your direct, unbroken presence. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does it change your sense of prayer and worship now, knowing they are
currently mediated, but one day won't need to be?
