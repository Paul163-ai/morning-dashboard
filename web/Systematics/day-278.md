## Verse

"And when they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord snatched
Philip away... and the eunuch went on his way rejoicing." (Acts 8:39)

## Explanation

Christians have long disagreed over whether baptism should be by immersion
(going fully under the water), pouring, or sprinkling. The word Scripture
uses, *baptizo*, ordinarily means "to dip" or "immerse" in Greek usage
outside the New Testament. The narrative details fit that meaning too:
John baptized "because water was plentiful" at Aenon; Philip and the
eunuch both "went down into the water" and both "came up out of" it,
strange details to record if a handful of water poured over the head was
all that happened.

More importantly, immersion pictures the theology of Romans 6 with a
clarity no other mode matches: going fully under is a picture of burial,
coming up is a picture of resurrection. That is why many churches hold
immersion to be not merely permissible but the fullest, most faithful
picture of what baptism means, while recognising, charitably, that
believers who were poured or sprinkled in good conscience are not thereby
unbaptized in God's sight. The mode serves the meaning; it should never be
allowed to eclipse it.

## Prayer

Lord, whatever mode marked my own baptism, let its meaning, burial and
resurrection with Christ, be what actually shapes how I live. Amen.
