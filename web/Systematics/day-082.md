## Verse

"...he does according to his will among the host of heaven and among the
inhabitants of the earth; and none can stay his hand or say to him, 'What
have you done?'" — Daniel 4:35

## Explanation

Yesterday we saw providence sustaining creation's existence moment by
moment. Today's aspect of providence is broader still: God's active
governing of every event that takes place within that creation, directing
history toward his own purposes. Daniel 4:35 is spoken by
Nebuchadnezzar, of all people — a pagan king who had just been humbled
into recognising that even the mightiest human rulers operate entirely
within the bounds of God's governing will, unable to resist or question
it.

It's important not to flatten this into bare determinism that erases
real distinctions between events. Scripture describes God governing
different kinds of events differently — some he causes directly, some he
permits while still holding people responsible for them, some he directs
through ordinary means (weather, human decisions, natural processes)
rather than overriding them. What unites all of it is that nothing
happens outside his governance; there is no corner of history, however
small, running independently of him.

For Nebuchadnezzar, this realisation came through humbling. For us, it's
meant to be a settled confidence: the news cycle, the unpredictable
turns of your own life, the decisions of people who have power over you
— none of it is outside the governing hand of the God who "does according
to his will."

## Prayer

Sovereign God, you govern every event, even the ones that unsettle me.
Help me find rest in your active rule over history, rather than anxiety
over what I cannot control. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What current event, in the world or in your own life, do you need to
entrust to God's governing hand rather than your own anxious control?
