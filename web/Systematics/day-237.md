## Verse

"Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed
us in Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places." —
Ephesians 1:3

## Explanation

We close August with the doctrine that has, in truth, been quietly
underneath everything we've studied since July: union with Christ.
Election, calling, regeneration, justification, adoption, sanctification,
perseverance, glorification — every one of these blessings, Paul says
here, comes "in Christ." Not alongside him, not earned separately and
then added to a relationship with him, but located entirely within union
with him — as though every spiritual blessing exists inside Christ, and
you receive it precisely because you have been joined, personally and
really, to him.

This is easy to treat as background theological plumbing rather than the
central truth it actually is. But consider how often "in Christ,"
"in him," or equivalent phrases appear across Paul's letters — dozens of
times, describing nearly every aspect of the Christian life. Union with
Christ isn't one blessing among many others. It's the relationship
through which every other blessing becomes yours at all.

Over these final days of August, and into the first days of September,
we'll trace exactly what this union means — chosen in him, crucified with
him, raised with him, abiding in him — before this whole section closes
and we turn, next, to the doctrine of the church.

## Prayer

Father, thank you for uniting me to Christ — the relationship through
which every other blessing has become mine. Deepen my understanding of
what it means to actually be "in him" in the days ahead. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Before today, how much thought had you given to "union with Christ" as
its own distinct, central doctrine, rather than just a phrase in
Scripture?
