## Verse

"Since therefore the children share in flesh and blood, he himself
likewise partook of the same things, that through death he might destroy
the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil." (Hebrews 2:14)

## Explanation

Yesterday we marveled at the fact of the incarnation; today we ask why it
was necessary at all. Hebrews gives a direct answer: because the problem
to be solved (death, and the devil's hold through it) belonged to
flesh and blood, the remedy had to enter flesh and blood too. A rescue
performed entirely from a safe distance, without genuinely sharing our
condition, could not have addressed a problem rooted precisely in that
condition.

This connects directly to the doctrine of substitution we'll study
later this month: for Christ to stand in our place, he had to actually
occupy the place he was standing in: genuinely human, genuinely subject
to death, not simply appearing to be so. Hebrews goes on to say he had
to be "made like his brothers in every respect" so that he could become
"a merciful and faithful high priest" (Hebrews 2:17); representation
requires genuine likeness, not a costume.

This should deepen our gratitude for what the incarnation actually cost.
It wasn't a convenient visit from a safe remove. It was the eternal Son
entering fully into the very condition (flesh, blood, mortality) that
needed rescuing, so that the rescue could actually reach it.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for entering fully into flesh and blood to defeat
death from the inside, not from a safe distance. Let that cost deepen my
gratitude today. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does understanding the incarnation as necessary, not just remarkable,
change how you think about what it cost the Son to become human?
