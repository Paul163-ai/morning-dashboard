## Verse

"I write these things to you who believe in the name of the Son of God
that you may know that you have eternal life." — 1 John 5:13

## Explanation

In this section we turn to assurance: can a Christian genuinely know, with real
confidence, that they are saved — not merely hope so, but actually know
it? John states his purpose for writing an entire letter plainly: so that
believers "may know" they have eternal life. This wasn't meant to remain
an anxious, unresolved question hanging over the Christian life. Scripture
treats assurance as a genuinely knowable, ordinary feature of healthy
faith, not a rare experience reserved for spiritual elites.

This doesn't mean assurance is automatic or that doubt never touches
genuine believers — we'll look at how assurance actually grows over the
next two days. But it does mean the goal was never permanent uncertainty
about your standing with God. If you've spent years wondering whether
you're "really" saved, unable to settle the question, that ongoing
uncertainty isn't the normal Christian experience Scripture describes or
intends for you. God wants you to know, not just hope.

The rest of John's letter goes on to give specific, concrete tests for
this knowledge — belief about who Christ is, love for other believers,
and a pattern of obedience — which we'll explore in the days ahead as the actual
grounds assurance can be built on, rather than vague feeling alone.

## Prayer

Father, thank you that you want me to know I have eternal life, not just
hope uncertainly. Where doubt has lingered for a long time, help me find
real, settled assurance in the days ahead. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you settled into confident assurance of your salvation, or does
uncertainty linger? What would it mean for you to actually "know," as
John describes?
