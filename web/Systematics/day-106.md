## Verse

"...everyone who is called by my name, whom I created for my glory, whom
I formed and made." — Isaiah 43:7

## Explanation

We've looked at what humanity is — image-bearing, embodied and
ensouled — now we ask what humanity is *for*. Isaiah states it without
hedging: God's people were created for his glory. This is not a minor
side-purpose alongside more important human goals like happiness,
achievement, or self-fulfillment. It's the stated reason for our
existence in the first place — the purpose everything else in a human
life is meant to serve, rather than compete with.

This can sound, at first, like it diminishes us — as though we exist
merely as props for someone else's display. But recall what we learned
in March: God is supremely blessed, perfect, and lacking nothing; he
doesn't need our glory-giving to complete himself. Being created "for his
glory" isn't God using us for his own benefit the way a person might use
a tool. It's closer to an invitation into the single most fitting,
satisfying purpose available to any creature — reflecting, in creaturely
form, the glory of the very being who is himself infinitely glorious and
infinitely good.

Every other purpose we might chase — success, comfort, legacy,
recognition — will eventually prove too small to fill a life. Only this
purpose is actually large enough, and it was built into us from the very
beginning, not added on as a later religious obligation.

## Prayer

Lord, you made me for your glory. Free me from chasing purposes too
small to satisfy, and let glorifying you shape everything I do today.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

What smaller purpose have you been chasing that was never going to be
big enough to satisfy the "for his glory" you were actually made for?
