## Verse

"Do you think that I cannot appeal to my Father, and he will at once send
me more than twelve legions of angels?" (Matthew 26:53)

## Explanation

Jesus speaks these words in Gethsemane, moments after Peter has drawn a
sword to defend him, and moments before his arrest and crucifixion. It's
one of the most striking displays of omnipotence in Scripture — not
because power is unleashed, but because it isn't. Christ names the
staggering force available to him (a single Roman legion held roughly
5,000 soldiers; twelve legions of angels is beyond military
comprehension) and then deliberately doesn't call on it.

This matters for how we understand God's power. Omnipotence untethered
from wisdom and love would be terrifying — raw force with no
predictability, no restraint. But God's power is never separate from the
rest of his character; it's always exercised in perfect step with his
goodness, wisdom, and purposes. Jesus had all the power needed to
escape the cross in an instant. What he didn't have — and wouldn't
choose even if he did — was any inclination to use that power apart from
his Father's will and our salvation.

This should reshape how we relate to God's power in our own lives. We
don't just want a God who is powerful; we want a God whose power is
always governed by wisdom and love, exercised for good purposes rather
than displayed at random. Gethsemane shows us exactly that kind of power
— immense, and immensely restrained.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you that your power was never separate from your love
— even when you had every means to escape the cross. Help me trust that
your restraint toward me is never weakness, but wisdom. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you ever wished God would use his power more visibly in your
situation — and what would trusting his restraint look like instead?
