## Verse

"And the Lord my God will come, and all the holy ones with him... And the
Lord will be king over all the earth." (Zechariah 14:5,9)

## Explanation

Premillennialism holds that Christ returns first, and his thousand-year
reign follows — hence "pre," before the millennium. On this view,
Revelation 20 should be read in the straightforward chronological order
the text presents: Christ's visible return (described in Revelation 19),
then a literal reign of a thousand years on earth, centred on Jerusalem,
fulfilling Old Testament promises of a coming kingdom of peace and justice
(passages like Isaiah 11 and Zechariah 14, which speak of nations
streaming to Jerusalem and Christ ruling as king over the whole earth) in
a way many premillennialists believe requires an actual earthly, historical
reign, not merely a spiritual one.

Premillennialism's strength is its plain reading of Revelation 20's
sequence and its serious attention to Old Testament kingdom promises that
can feel under-realised if treated only symbolically. Its most common
form today, dispensational premillennialism, also holds a particular view
of Israel's future role during this period, distinct from the church —
though other premillennialists (historic premillennialism) hold the
thousand-year reign without that particular distinction. Whatever its
form, premillennialism's core conviction is that the "already" of Christ's
current reign in heaven still awaits a further "not yet": a literal,
earthly, thousand-year installment of his kingdom before the final,
eternal state begins.
