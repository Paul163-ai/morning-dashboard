## Verse

"And the angels who did not stay within their own position of authority,
but left their proper dwelling, he has kept in eternal chains under
gloomy darkness until the judgment of the great day." (Jude 1:6)

## Explanation

Having looked at angels who remained faithful in their service to God, we
turn to a much darker chapter: some angels, led by the one Scripture
calls Satan, rebelled against God and were cast down. Jude's language is
careful and important: these were angels who "did not stay within their
own position of authority" and "left their proper dwelling." Satan is not
God's equal or opposite; he is a created being who overstepped the
authority he was given, and who now exists under a judgment already
determined, awaiting its final execution.

This distinction matters more than it might seem. Some worldviews
picture good and evil as two roughly matched cosmic forces locked in an
uncertain struggle. Scripture never allows that picture. Satan is
powerful, but creaturely: finite, limited, and already defeated in
principle, his final judgment fixed and certain even while some of his
activity continues in the present age. He is dangerous, not because he
rivals God, but because he is a real, malicious enemy operating within
limits God alone sets.

Knowing this shapes how seriously, and how confidently, we should treat
spiritual opposition: seriously, because it's real; confidently, because
it's never an even match.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that Satan is not your equal, but a creature already
under your judgment. Help me take spiritual opposition seriously without
ever fearing it as though it could rival you. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you tend to underestimate spiritual opposition, or overestimate its
power compared to God's? What would a right-sized view look like?
