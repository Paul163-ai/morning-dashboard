## Verse

"You are a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek." (Hebrews
5:6)

## Explanation

We turn now to Christ's second office: Priest. A priest's fundamental
role, throughout Scripture, is to represent people before God: offering
sacrifices, mediating access, standing between sinful people and a holy
God. Old Testament priests came from the tribe of Levi, through Aaron's
line, and their priesthood was passed down by descent and eventually
ended by death. Hebrews points to something different: Christ's
priesthood belongs to an entirely different, older order, named after
the mysterious priest-king Melchizedek (Genesis 14:18-20), and it is,
unlike every Levitical priesthood, permanent: "forever."

This matters because a priesthood that could end was always going to be
an insufficient, temporary solution, precisely why the Old Testament
sacrificial system needed constant repetition, offering by offering,
generation by generation, always incomplete. Christ's priesthood, by
contrast, needs no successor and no repetition, because it was never
subject to death in the way every human priesthood was. We already saw
this connected to his session at the Father's right hand (day 159): he
sits, because his priestly work of sacrifice is genuinely finished.

This gives believers something no Old Testament worshipper ever fully
had: a permanent priest, always available, whose access to God on our
behalf will never lapse or need renewal.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you are a priest forever, never needing replacement or
repetition. Thank you for permanent, unbroken access to the Father
through you. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does knowing your access to God rests on a permanent priesthood, rather
than a temporary one, change how secure you feel in your standing before
him?
