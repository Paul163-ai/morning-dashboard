## Verse

"And let all God's angels worship him." (Hebrews 1:6)

## Explanation

Scripture is emphatic, elsewhere, that worship belongs to God alone: an
angel refuses it when John tries to offer it (Revelation 19:10), and the
first commandment forbids it toward anything else. Against that
backdrop, Hebrews 1:6 is a remarkable claim: not merely that Christ is
worthy of great honour, but that God himself commands his own angels to
worship the Son. If Christ were anything less than fully God, this
command would contradict everything else Scripture says about worship
belonging exclusively to him.

This closes out three days considering Christ's full deity from
different angles: his identity as "the Word who was God" (John 1:1),
"the whole fullness of deity" dwelling in him bodily (Colossians 2:9),
and now, the direct command that even angels worship him. Together they
form an airtight case that the New Testament writers were not confused
or exaggerating when they applied divine titles, divine authority, and
now divine worship to Jesus of Nazareth.

This should shape our own posture directly. If angels, who have seen
God's glory unveiled far more clearly than we have, are commanded to
worship Christ, our own worship of him is not optional devotion to an
impressive teacher. It's the appropriate response to God himself, made
known to us in the Son.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, angels themselves worship you as God. Receive my worship
today, not as devotion to a great teacher, but as what is owed to God
himself. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does your own worship of Jesus reflect the same reality that commands
even angels to worship him?
