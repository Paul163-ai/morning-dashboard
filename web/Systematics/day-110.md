## Verse

"Everyone who makes a practice of sinning also practices lawlessness;
sin is lawlessness." (1 John 3:4)

## Explanation

Having looked at sin's first historical entrance, we now ask a more
basic question: what actually counts as sin? John's definition is
deliberately broad and God-centred: sin is lawlessness, literally,
being without law, or acting against God's law. This isn't a narrow list
of specific forbidden acts; it's a description of any failure,
whether in action, attitude, or omission, to conform to God's revealed
moral character and will.

This definition cuts against two common but incomplete pictures of sin.
One treats sin merely as breaking arbitrary religious rules, disconnected
from any deeper reality, as though God's laws were simply obstacles to
comply with. The other treats sin as purely a matter of visible,
extreme wrongdoing (violence, theft, obvious cruelty), leaving quieter
sins (pride, envy, unkind thoughts, neglected duties) largely
unexamined. John's definition includes all of it: any lack of conformity
to God's law is sin, whatever form it takes and however respectable it
looks from the outside.

Understanding sin this way keeps the category appropriately wide. It
also grounds sin's seriousness correctly: not in a list of cultural
taboos, but in God's own perfect, unchanging moral character, which we
spent time tracing in March. Sin isn't merely bad behaviour; it's a
failure to reflect the holy God whose image we bear.

## Prayer

Lord, show me where I've narrowed sin down to only the obvious things,
while overlooking quieter failures to conform to your character. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What quieter, more respectable-looking sin have you been slow to name
as genuinely lawlessness against God?
