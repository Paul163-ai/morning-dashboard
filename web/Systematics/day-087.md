## Verse

"I know that you can do all things, and that no purpose of yours can be
thwarted... Therefore I have uttered what I did not understand, things too
wonderful for me, which I did not know." — Job 42:2-3

## Explanation

We close our brief look at the problem of evil where the book most
devoted to it ends: not with an explanation, but with a confession. Job
had lost everything and had spent chapters demanding answers from God.
When God finally responds (chapters 38-41), he never actually explains
why Job suffered — he answers with question after question about the
scope of creation, until Job's own answer, above, is essentially: I
spoke about things I didn't understand, and I was wrong to demand an
explanation as the price of my trust.

This is not God shaming Job for asking. Notably, at the end of the book,
God rebukes Job's friends for their confident, wrong explanations of
Job's suffering, while affirming that Job "has spoken of me what is
right" (Job 42:7) — his honest complaints were more acceptable to God
than his friends' tidy theological answers. What God corrects in Job is
not the asking, but the assumption that he was owed a full explanation
before he could trust.

That's the note to end this month's hardest theme on. We don't get every
answer. We do get a God vast enough to be trusted without one — the
same God whose power, wisdom, and character we've spent all of March
tracing.

## Prayer

Lord, like Job, I don't need every explanation to keep trusting you. You
are wise and good beyond what I can grasp — let that be enough for me
today. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What would it look like for you to keep trusting God's goodness in a
situation where you may never get a full explanation?
