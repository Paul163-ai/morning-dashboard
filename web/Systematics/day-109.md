## Verse

"And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, 'You may surely eat of every
tree of the garden, but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you
shall not eat...'" (Genesis 2:16-17)

## Explanation

Before sin actually entered the world, God had already built in the
possibility of it: a single prohibition, in an otherwise abundant
garden, that could be obeyed or disobeyed. It's worth pausing on why. A
world with no possibility of disobedience would also have been a world
with no possibility of genuine, freely-given love and obedience, love
that isn't love at all if there was never any real alternative to it.
God's single command gave Adam and Eve exactly what was needed for real
relationship: abundant provision ("every tree"), together with one
genuine test of trust.

This doesn't fully answer every question the problem of evil raises
(we spent several days on that honestly in March), but it does clarify
something important: sin's possibility wasn't a design flaw God failed
to anticipate. A world capable of genuine love was always, necessarily, a
world where love could also be refused. The tragedy of Genesis 3 is not
that the test existed, but that it was failed.

It's also worth noting how generous the prohibition actually was:
one tree withheld, out of an entire garden freely given. The temptation
was never really about scarcity or unreasonable restriction; it was
about whether God's word, however small the ask, could be trusted over
one's own judgment.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for making genuine relationship with you possible, even
knowing it meant the possibility of refusal. Help me trust your word
rather than testing its limits. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where might God be asking for trust in something that feels small or
restrictive, the way the one tree did for Adam and Eve?
