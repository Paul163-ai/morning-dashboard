## Verse

"And let us consider how to stir up one another to love and good works,
not neglecting to meet together, as is the habit of some, but encouraging
one another." — Hebrews 10:24–25

## Explanation

Formal commitment to a specific local church isn't a burdensome
obligation Scripture reluctantly requires — it's the context in which
some of the deepest goods of the Christian life actually become
possible. The author of Hebrews connects "stirring up love and good
works" directly to not neglecting to meet together — the ordinary,
sustained, un-glamorous rhythm of showing up, again and again, with the
same specific people over years, is precisely what makes real, deep
encouragement and growth possible.

Consider what genuine church membership actually enables that occasional,
uncommitted attendance cannot: being known well enough for someone to
notice when you're struggling; having leaders who actually know your
specific circumstances well enough to shepherd you meaningfully;
developing relationships deep enough to bear real weight in genuine
crisis, not just surface-level friendliness; having your own gifts known
and actually used, rather than remaining anonymous in a crowd.

None of this happens automatically simply by attending a service
occasionally. It requires the kind of sustained, known, mutual commitment
membership is meant to formalise. If you've been holding church at
arm's length, these next few days are worth using to ask honestly what you might be
missing by keeping that distance.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the deep goods that come from real, known belonging
to your people. Give me courage to move from distant attendance to real,
committed membership, if I haven't already. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What specific good — being known, being shepherded, using your gifts —
might you be missing out on by not being fully committed to a local
church?
