## Verse

"Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his
glory!" (Isaiah 6:3)

## Explanation

Holiness is the attribute that made Isaiah, a righteous prophet, cry out
"Woe is me! For I am lost" the moment he glimpsed it (Isaiah 6:5). That
reaction tells us something the word "holy" can lose in casual use.
Holiness has two threads woven together: separation and purity. God is
*set apart* from creation — utterly distinct, in a category of his own,
not simply the biggest or best version of something we already know —
and he is *set apart from evil* — untouched by sin, incapable of moral
compromise, unable to look on evil with anything but opposition.

The seraphim's threefold repetition, "Holy, holy, holy," is the only
attribute in Scripture emphasised this way — not "loving, loving,
loving" or "powerful, powerful, powerful." Ancient Hebrew used repetition
for emphasis, and Isaiah pushes it to its absolute limit for exactly one
quality. Whatever else is true of God, his holiness is not one attribute
among equals — it's the lens the rest are seen through.

This is why an encounter with real holiness is never comfortable. It
exposes rather than flatters. But notice what happens next in Isaiah's
vision: the seraph doesn't leave him ruined, but touches his lips with a
coal and declares his guilt taken away (Isaiah 6:6-7). Holiness that
exposes sin is also the same holiness that, in Christ, makes a way to
deal with it.

## Prayer

Holy God, before you I have no case to make for myself. Thank you that
your holiness didn't stay at a distance from me, but made a way, in
Christ, to draw me near. Amen.

## Reflection Question

When did you last feel the weight of God's holiness rather than just
assent to it as a doctrine?
