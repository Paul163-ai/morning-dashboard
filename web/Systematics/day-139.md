## Verse

"Indeed, under the law almost everything is purified with blood, and
without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins."
(Hebrews 9:22)

## Explanation

We turn now to the atonement itself: what Christ's death actually
accomplished. Before examining its nature, it's worth asking a more
basic question: was the cross actually necessary, or could God have
simply forgiven sin some other way? Hebrews states a consistent
biblical principle running from the Old Testament sacrificial system
straight through to Christ: sin's forgiveness requires the shedding of
blood, the giving up of a life in the place of the one who sinned.
This wasn't an arbitrary rule God could have waived at will; it reflects
something true about the seriousness of sin and the justice of God we
studied back in March: sin carries a real penalty, death, that justice
requires be paid.

This rules out a common but mistaken assumption: that God could have
simply decided to overlook sin out of love, without the cross being
strictly necessary. If he could have, the cross becomes an
unnecessarily brutal and confusing detour rather than the fitting,
required solution to a genuine problem. Instead, Scripture presents the
cross as necessary precisely because God is both perfectly loving and
perfectly just; mercy could not come at the expense of justice being
ignored.

This is why the atonement is not one option among several equally valid
paths to forgiveness, but the fitting, necessary answer to exactly the
problem sin creates.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that the cross was necessary, not merely one option
among many. Let that necessity deepen my gratitude for what it cost you.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you ever assumed God could have simply forgiven sin without the
cross? How does that assumption change in light of his justice?
