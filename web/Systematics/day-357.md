## Verse

"But in fact Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of
those who have fallen asleep... For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ
shall all be made alive." (1 Corinthians 15:20,22)

## Explanation

"Firstfruits" was an agricultural term with a specific, guaranteed
meaning: the first portion of a harvest, offered to God as certain proof
the rest of the crop was coming. It wasn't a hopeful sample that might or
might not be followed by more — it was, by definition, the first
instalment of a harvest already underway. Paul's claim is that Christ's
own resurrection functions exactly this way for every believer's future
resurrection: not a unique, isolated event unrelated to us, but the
first, guaranteeing portion of the very same harvest we are promised to
share in.

This is why the resurrection of believers isn't merely plausible or
hoped-for, but certain, resting on an event that has already happened.
Paul draws the comparison to Adam deliberately: just as Adam's sin
guaranteed death for everyone joined to him by nature, Christ's
resurrection guarantees life for everyone joined to him by faith. The
empty tomb, already an established fact of history, is not simply
evidence for Christian belief in general — it is the actual, concrete
guarantee of the specific resurrection every believer in Christ is
promised to share.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that your resurrection isn't just proof I can believe in
you, but the actual guarantee of my own resurrection to come. Let that
certainty anchor my hope. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does thinking of Christ's resurrection as "firstfruits" — a guarantee,
not just an inspiring example — change how certain your own hope of
resurrection feels?
