## Verse

"And all these, though commended through their faith, did not receive
what was promised, since God had provided something better for us, that
apart from us they should not be made perfect." — Hebrews 11:39–40

## Explanation

Hebrews 11's long list of faithful believers, from Abel to the prophets,
closes with a striking admission: none of them, even after death, had yet
received the fullness of what was promised. Their faith was commended,
genuine, and rewarded with God's presence — but "made perfect," in the
sense of receiving every promise in its completed form, waits on
something communal, tied to us, the whole company of God's people across
history being brought to completion together rather than individually,
piecemeal, as each one dies.

This closes out what we can say about the intermediate state: it is real,
conscious, and near to God — genuinely better than life on earth, as
Paul insisted — but it is not the finish line. Every believer who has
ever died, however long ago, is still waiting alongside every believer
alive today for the same completing event: the resurrection of the body
and the fullness of what God has promised, arriving not for individuals
one at a time but for the whole gathered people of God together. That
shared, still-future completion is exactly where the readings ahead turn
next — to Christ's own return, the event every departed and living
believer alike is waiting for.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that I'm not waiting alone — every believer who has ever
lived is waiting with me for the same completion. Keep my hope fixed on
that shared future. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does it change your sense of hope to know that even the great believers
who died long ago are still waiting, with you, for the same final
promise?
