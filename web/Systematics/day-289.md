## Verse

"Not neglecting to meet together, as is the habit of some, but
encouraging one another." — Hebrews 10:25

## Explanation

Baptism and the Lord's Supper are both, by their nature, corporate acts —
which points us toward a wider question: why does the church
gather at all? In an age when sermons, songs, and Bible teaching are all
available instantly from a phone, physically assembling with other
believers can seem almost optional, a preference rather than a necessity.
Hebrews addresses believers tempted to think exactly that, and its
counsel is blunt: don't neglect it, however habitual the temptation
becomes.

The reason gathering matters isn't primarily informational — you could, in
theory, learn the same content alone. It's relational and covenantal.
Corporate worship is where the church visibly becomes what it invisibly
is: one body, made up of members who need each other, encouraging,
correcting, and being genuinely present to one another in a way no
broadcast, however excellent, can replicate. A church is not an audience
that happens to watch the same content; it is a gathered people, and the
gathering itself is part of what God has ordained for our growth.

## Prayer

Lord, forgive me for the times I've treated gathering with your people as
optional. Give me a hunger not just for content about you, but for the
actual presence of your people. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What has made you tempted, if ever, to treat gathering with your church as
optional? What would Hebrews 10:25 say to that temptation?
