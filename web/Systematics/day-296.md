## Verse

"For as in one body we have many members, and the members do not all have
the same function, so we, though many, are one body in Christ, and
individually members one of another." — Romans 12:4–5

## Explanation

Scripture gives us not one tidy list of spiritual gifts but several
overlapping ones — Romans 12 names prophecy, service, teaching,
exhortation, giving, leadership, and mercy; 1 Corinthians 12 adds wisdom,
knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, tongues, and their interpretation;
Ephesians 4 names apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds, and
teachers. No single list is exhaustive, and none of the biblical authors
seems concerned to produce a complete inventory. What they agree on is the
principle behind the lists: the body needs diversity to function, and no
single gift, however impressive, is sufficient on its own.

This is worth sitting with, because it cuts against a common instinct to
rank gifts by drama — assuming the more visible or supernatural-seeming
gifts (prophecy, healing, tongues) must matter more than the quieter ones
(administration, mercy, giving). Paul's body metaphor rejects that ranking
outright: an eye cannot say to a hand, "I have no need of you." A church
that only values its most visible gifts will starve for the ordinary ones
it also needs to function — the same way a body missing its less
glamorous organs would not survive long on eyes and hands alone.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the diversity of gifts you've placed in your church.
Help me value the quiet, ordinary gifts around me as much as the visible
ones. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Which gifts in your own church do you tend to notice and celebrate, and
which quieter ones might be going unnoticed and unthanked?
