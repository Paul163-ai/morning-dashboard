## Verse

"For then there will be great tribulation, such as has not been from the
beginning of the world until now, no, and never will be." — Matthew
24:21

## Explanation

We open December, and this year's final month, with a hard subject:
Scripture's promise that the time leading up to Christ's return includes
real, intense suffering — persecution, upheaval, and hardship unlike
anything before it. Christians have long debated exactly how this
tribulation relates to Christ's return chronologically, and this
devotional won't try to settle that timing question. What every reading
of these passages agrees on is that Jesus did not promise his church an
easy road to the end; he promised the opposite; and he told his disciples
this plainly, in advance, precisely so they would not be shaken when it
came.

That advance warning is itself a kind of mercy. A trial anticipated is
easier to endure than one that arrives as pure surprise, betraying every
assumption a person held about how life with God was supposed to go.
Jesus did not want his church caught off guard by suffering, as though
hardship meant he had failed to keep his promises. The tribulation he
describes, whatever its exact scope and timing, was written into his own
teaching from the start — not a sign that God has lost control, but a
sign that history is unfolding exactly as he said it would.

## Prayer

Lord, when tribulation comes — mine or the world's — let it not shake my
faith in you, since you told me plainly it would come. Keep me steady in
what you have already promised. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has any hardship in your life ever felt like proof that God had failed
you, rather than something he had already told you to expect?
