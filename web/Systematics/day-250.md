## Verse

"You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for
his own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who
called you out of darkness into his marvelous light." (1 Peter 2:9)

## Explanation

Having spent several days on what the church is, we turn now to why it exists —
its purpose. The first and most fundamental purpose is worship: gathering
to glorify and enjoy God together. Peter's language is priestly and
directional — a people called specifically to proclaim, to declare
God's excellencies, existing for the very purpose of magnifying who he is.

This is worth stating plainly because church purpose is easily reduced
to more immediately practical goals — community, teaching, service, even
evangelism, all genuinely important, as we'll see over the next two
days. But underneath and prior to all of them sits worship: the church
exists, first, because God is worthy of glory, and gathering to declare
that worth is not a means to some other end, but a purpose in itself.

This should shape what you expect and look for when you gather with
other believers. Not primarily entertainment, not primarily personal
benefit (though real benefit does flow from worship), but genuine
declaration of God's worth — songs, prayers, preaching, all oriented
first toward him, before anything else. A church that has lost sight of
worship as its primary purpose has lost sight of why it exists at all.

## Prayer

Lord, you alone are worthy of worship. Let my gathering with your people
be genuinely oriented toward declaring your excellencies, not just
toward what I personally get out of it. Amen.

## Reflection Question

When you gather with other believers, is your primary posture "what will
I get from this" or "how can I help declare God's worth together"?
