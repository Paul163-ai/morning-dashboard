## Verse

"The Rock, his work is perfect, for all his ways are justice. A God of
faithfulness and without iniquity, just and upright is he." (Deuteronomy 32:4)

## Explanation

Righteousness means God always acts in accordance with what is right —
and crucially, he himself is the standard by which rightness is measured.
This isn't circular the way it would be if a human declared themselves
the standard of right and wrong. God's own unchanging, perfectly good
character simply *is* what "right" means; there's no higher standard
outside of him to measure him against, because every other standard we'd
appeal to (justice, fairness, goodness) only makes sense because he
defined and embodies it first.

Moses' song, from which this verse comes, was written to a people who
had just spent forty years watching God's dealings with them up close —
his discipline, his provision, his patience with their repeated failures.
"All his ways are justice" isn't an abstract claim; it's a verdict handed
down after decades of firsthand observation. Every action of God,
examined closely enough, turns out to be right.

This matters enormously when God's actions in Scripture — or in your own
life — seem hard to understand. The pattern set by Deuteronomy 32 isn't
to assume God has gotten something wrong, but to trust that closer
inspection, or more time, will vindicate what currently looks
inexplicable.

## Prayer

Righteous God, all your ways are justice, even the ones I don't yet
understand. Give me patience to trust your rightness rather than rushing
to question it. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a situation where you've quietly assumed God got something
wrong, rather than trusting his righteousness?
