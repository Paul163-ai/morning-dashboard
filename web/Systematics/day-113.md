## Verse

"Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother
conceive me." (Psalm 51:5)

## Explanation

David writes this in the middle of his anguished confession after his
sin with Bathsheba, and strikingly, he doesn't trace his sin only to
that one moment of failure. He traces it all the way back to his own
conception, describing himself as sinful from the very start of his
existence, not merely once he was old enough to choose wrongly. This is
what theologians call inherited corruption: alongside the guilt
inherited from Adam we looked at yesterday, we also inherit a genuinely
sinful nature, a real bent toward sin present from birth, not acquired
gradually through bad influences or choices alone.

This matters because it explains something experience confirms but
polite optimism often denies: children don't need to be taught to lie,
to grab, to demand their own way: those instincts appear on their own,
long before any "bad example" could account for them. David isn't
making an excuse for his adultery and murder by blaming his nature; if
anything, the confession deepens his sense of need, tracing the root of
his specific sin to something more fundamental than a single bad
decision.

This is sobering, but it's also clarifying. If the problem runs this
deep (present from our very beginning, not just accumulated through
poor choices), then no amount of better decisions alone was ever going
to be the solution. Only a rescue that reaches the root will do.

## Prayer

Lord, like David, I confess a sinfulness that runs deeper than my
individual choices. Thank you that your rescue reaches the root, not just
the symptoms. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has recognising sin as a deep-rooted nature, not just individual bad
choices, changed how you understand your own need for grace?
