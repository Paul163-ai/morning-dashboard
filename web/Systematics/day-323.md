## Verse

"This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same
way as you saw him go into heaven." — Acts 1:11

## Explanation

The angels' words to the disciples, staring up at the sky after Christ's
ascension, are worth reading slowly. Not a vague spiritual return, not a
metaphor for his ongoing presence by the Spirit, but this Jesus — the
same one they had walked with, eaten with, watched crucified and then
seen risen — returning the same way he left: visibly, bodily, in person.
The doctrine of Christ's return, historically called the second coming,
is not an invention of later Christian imagination; it is the church's
inheritance from the very moment of Christ's ascension.

That "this same Jesus" matters more than it might first appear. It rules
out any account of the return that reduces it to something purely
internal or already accomplished — Christ's return is a real, future,
public event, involving the same physical person who walked out of an
empty tomb. Whatever mystery remains about its timing, which we'll turn
to in the coming days, the fact of it is not in question for anyone
taking the New Testament at its word: the one who ascended is coming
back.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, I believe you are coming back — the same Jesus who walked on
earth, died, and rose. Give me a heart genuinely watching for that day,
not merely assenting to the doctrine of it. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does Christ's return feel like a living hope you actually anticipate, or
more like a doctrine you affirm without much felt expectation?
