## Verse

"Then I saw thrones, and seated on them were those to whom the authority
to judge was committed... they came to life and reigned with Christ for a
thousand years." (Revelation 20:4)

## Explanation

In this section we turn to one of the most disputed passages in Scripture:
Revelation 20's vision of a thousand-year reign, traditionally called the
millennium. Faithful, careful readers of the same Bible have reached
genuinely different conclusions about what this passage describes — a
literal future era of Christ's earthly reign before the final judgment,
the present reign of Christ and his saints happening now in some form, or
a symbolic picture of the whole church age. This is a question where
humility is especially warranted, since the very book it comes from is
saturated with symbolic numbers and images throughout.

Before choosing sides, it's worth simply reading the text on its own
terms: a binding of Satan, a resurrection of martyrs to reign with
Christ, a thousand years, then a final release of Satan, a final battle,
and the last judgment. Whatever else remains disputed, this much is
shared common ground across the different views: Christ's victory is
total, Satan's opposition is time-limited and ultimately crushed, and the
saints who have suffered for Christ are vindicated and reign with him.
The disagreement is over the timing and nature of the "thousand years,"
not over whether Christ wins.
