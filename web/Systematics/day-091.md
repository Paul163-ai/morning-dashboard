## Verse

"Your Father knows what you need before you ask him. Pray then like
this..." (Matthew 6:8-9)

## Explanation

We open April by tying together two themes from March: God's sovereign
providence and the practice of prayer. A fair question follows naturally
from everything we studied last month: if God already governs all
things according to his will, and already knows what we need before we
ask, why pray at all? Jesus doesn't treat this as a contradiction. In the
very breath after saying the Father already knows our needs, he says
"pray then like this" — and teaches the disciples exactly how.

The answer Scripture gives isn't that prayer changes God's mind or
supplies him with information he lacked. It's that God has ordained
prayer itself as one of the real means by which his purposes come to
pass — not a formality performed alongside an already-settled outcome,
but a genuine part of how that outcome is brought about. Just as God
ordains the harvest and also ordains the sowing of seed as the means to
it, he ordains the ends he wills and also ordains our prayers as part of
the means to those ends.

This should embolden rather than discourage prayer. Far from making
prayer pointless, God's sovereignty is exactly what makes it worth doing
— we're not petitioning a God who might be unable to help, but one who
has built our asking into how he works.

## Prayer

Father, you already know what I need, and yet you invite me to ask. Teach
me to pray with confidence, not because you need reminding, but because
you have made my prayers part of your purposes. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has uncertainty about "why pray if God already knows" ever made your own
prayer life feel half-hearted? What might change if you saw prayer as a
genuine means God uses?
