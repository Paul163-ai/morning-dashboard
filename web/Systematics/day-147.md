## Verse

"He is the propitiation for our sins, and not for ours only but also for
the sins of the whole world." — 1 John 2:2

## Explanation

We turn now to a question faithful Christians have long discussed and
answered differently: the extent of the atonement — did Christ die with
the intention of actually securing salvation for every single person
without exception, or specifically and effectively for those the Father
gave him, who would come to genuine faith? John's language here leans
toward the widest possible scope: propitiation "for the sins of the
whole world," not merely a limited group.

What all sides of this discussion agree on, and what matters most for
daily faith, is this: Christ's death is fully sufficient to save any
person who comes to him, without exception or limit on his side. No one
who genuinely turns to Christ in faith will ever be told the atonement
wasn't enough for them, or that their particular sins fell outside its
reach. The invitation of the gospel is genuinely, sincerely open to
"whoever believes" (John 3:16), and 1 John 2:2 underlines just how far
that sufficiency reaches — potentially, to the whole world.

Tomorrow we'll look at a companion passage that emphasizes the other
side of this same question — Christ laying down his life specifically
for his own. Held together, both truths matter more than choosing a
side: the cross is sufficient for anyone, and effective for everyone who
believes.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, your atonement is sufficient for the whole world, and freely
offered to anyone who comes to you. Thank you that no sin of mine falls
outside its reach. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has uncertainty about the extent of the atonement ever made you doubt
whether Christ's death was truly sufficient for your own sin?
