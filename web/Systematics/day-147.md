## Verse

"He is the propitiation for our sins, and not for ours only but also for
the sins of the whole world." — 1 John 2:2

## Explanation

We turn now to a question the church has long reflected on carefully:
the extent of the atonement — for whom, specifically, did Christ die?
John's language here can sound, on first reading, like a claim that
every single individual who has ever lived is actually, effectively
saved by the cross. But John is writing to a small circle of Jewish
Christian readers, and his point is one of scope, not universal
application: Christ's propitiation is not a tribal or merely local
provision for "our" group alone, but sufficient for sinners of every
nation, "the whole world" in that sense — a truth Revelation later
confirms, describing the redeemed as a genuine multitude "from every
tribe and language and people and nation" (Revelation 5:9).

This is the classic distinction the Reformed tradition has held for
centuries: Christ's death is sufficient in value to atone for every sin
of every person who has ever lived — nothing about its worth limits who
could be saved by it — while being effective, by God's own design, only
for those the Father gave the Son (a theme we'll trace directly
tomorrow through Christ's own words about his sheep). The cross was
never a provision merely rendering salvation possible, awaiting
completion by human choice; it was the actual, accomplished securing of
salvation for those it was intended to save.

This should deepen rather than narrow your confidence. No sin is too
large or too foreign for the cross's sufficiency to reach — and if you
have come to Christ in genuine faith, that is itself the evidence his
atonement was intended, effectively, for you.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, your death is sufficient for sinners of every nation, and
effective for those you came specifically to save. Thank you that my own
faith is the evidence your atonement was always intended for me. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does distinguishing the atonement's infinite sufficiency from its
effective, particular application change how you read a verse like "the
whole world"?
