## Verse

"And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your
hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus." (Philippians 4:7)

## Explanation

We often speak of peace as a feeling we hope to have — calm nerves, a
settled mind. Scripture starts somewhere prior to that: peace as
something God *is* before it's something we *feel*. Philippians 4:7
doesn't promise a feeling that makes sense to you; it promises "the peace
of God," a peace rooted in his own unshakeable character, standing guard
over your heart even when your circumstances offer no rational basis for
calm. That's why it "surpasses understanding" — it isn't produced by
understanding your situation better. It's produced by the settled,
ordered nature of the God you're trusting.

This connects directly to yesterday's theme. A God who is not confused,
whose purposes are never in disarray, is able to be a genuine source of
peace rather than just a wish for one. If God himself were anxious,
reactive, or uncertain about outcomes, there would be nothing stable to
draw peace from. But because his character doesn't waver with
circumstances, the peace he offers doesn't have to wait for
circumstances to improve first.

Paul frames access to this peace as something practiced, not just
received — through prayer and thanksgiving (Philippians 4:6), we bring
our anxieties to the God whose own peace then stands guard over ours.

## Prayer

God of peace, thank you that your peace doesn't depend on my
circumstances making sense. Guard my heart and mind today with a peace
that comes from you, not from having all the answers. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What anxiety do you need to bring to God in prayer today rather than
carry alone?
