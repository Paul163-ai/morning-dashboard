## Verse

"'Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and hold fast to his
wife, and the two shall become one flesh.' This mystery is profound, and
I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church." (Ephesians 5:31–32)

## Explanation

We close this two-month sequence on union with Christ with the image
Paul reaches for as its deepest analogy: marriage. Just as a husband and
wife become, in Scripture's language, "one flesh" — two genuinely
distinct persons united in the closest possible earthly bond — so
Christ and his people are united, even more profoundly, in a bond Paul
calls a "mystery," language he's already used twice this letter for
truths hidden until revealed in the gospel.

This image captures something the legal and positional language of
union with Christ can sometimes miss: warmth, intimacy, delight. Christ
isn't merely your representative head or the legal ground of your
righteousness, true as both of those are. He is, in the deepest biblical
picture available, your bridegroom — committed, delighted in you,
pursuing you, giving himself for you (v.25), exactly as a husband gives
himself for his wife.

This is also the perfect bridge into what we turn to tomorrow: if Christ
is united to his people this intimately, then his people, united to him,
are also united to one another — one body, one bride, together. Everything
we've studied about your individual salvation across July and August has
always been, quietly, pointing toward this: not just you and Christ in
isolation, but you, Christ, and his whole gathered people — the church,
where we turn next.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for the profound mystery of your union with your
people — not distant, but as intimate as marriage. As I turn to think
about the church tomorrow, remind me that union with you always means
union with your people too. Amen.
