## Verse

"Shepherd the flock of God that is among you, exercising oversight, not
under compulsion, but willingly, as God would have you." (1 Peter 5:2)

## Explanation

In this section we look specifically at the two church offices Scripture most
clearly identifies: elders and deacons. Elders (also called overseers
or pastors, the New Testament uses these terms interchangeably for the
same office, see Acts 20:17, 28 and Titus 1:5, 7) carry primary
responsibility for teaching, spiritual oversight, and shepherding care
of the congregation. Peter's language is deliberately pastoral: elders
aren't primarily administrators or managers, but shepherds, tending
God's flock with the same kind of watchful, sacrificial care a shepherd
gives actual sheep.

Paul lists specific, detailed qualifications for this office in 1
Timothy 3 and Titus 1: qualifications weighted heavily toward proven
character, family life, and reputation, more than toward talent or
charisma: above reproach, self-controlled, hospitable, able to teach, not
a recent convert, well thought of by outsiders. This should reshape how
churches (and church members evaluating potential leaders) think about
what actually qualifies someone for this role, not primarily
impressiveness, but tested, consistent godly character over time.

Elders exist to serve the congregation, not to be served by it: Peter's
warning against exercising this office "for shameful gain" (v.2)
reflects Christ's own model of leadership as service (Mark 10:42–45),
the pattern every elder is meant to follow.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the shepherds you've given your church. Help me pray
for and support the elders over me, and help churches everywhere
prioritise character over charisma in choosing them. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you actively pray for the elders or pastors who shepherd you? What
would it look like to support them more intentionally?
