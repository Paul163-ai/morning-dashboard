## Verse

"We were buried therefore with him by baptism into death, in order that,
just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we too
might walk in newness of life." — Romans 6:4

## Explanation

Paul's argument in Romans 6 is the deepest well for understanding what
baptism pictures. Going under the water pictures burial — the old self,
condemned with Christ, going down into death. Coming up out of the water
pictures resurrection — rising to walk in a genuinely new life, no longer
enslaved to sin. Baptism, in other words, is a dramatization of the gospel
itself: not just forgiveness, but death and new life, the old self gone and
a new self raised.

This is why the New Testament treats baptism as inseparable, in ordinary
experience, from becoming a Christian — not because water saves, but
because baptism is the appointed moment where a believer's union with
Christ's death and resurrection is publicly enacted. It is one thing to
believe intellectually that Christ died and rose for you; it is another to
be lowered into water as though buried, and lifted out as though raised.
The ordinance presses the truth of the gospel from the head toward the
whole person.

## Prayer

Father, I was united to Christ's death and resurrection when I first
trusted him. Let that reality shape how I live today — dead to sin's claim
on me, alive to walk in the newness Christ purchased. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Paul says baptism pictures not just forgiveness but a decisive break with
the old self. Where in your life is that break still incomplete in
practice, even though it's already true in Christ?
