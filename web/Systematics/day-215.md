## Verse

"To put off your old self... and to be renewed in the spirit of your
minds, and to put on the new self, created after the likeness of God in
true righteousness and holiness." — Ephesians 4:22–24

## Explanation

Progressive sanctification is the slow, ongoing, often gradual work
Paul describes here: an active, daily process, not a passive waiting for
holiness to happen to you. He uses the image of clothing — putting off
one set of garments, putting on another. This is deliberate, repeated,
practical work: identifying specific old patterns (Paul goes on to name
lying, anger, stealing, corrupt speech in the verses that follow) and
actually replacing them, piece by piece, with their opposite.

This is worth emphasizing because progressive sanctification is easy to
either over-spiritualize into vague hopes for change, or reduce to sheer
willpower divorced from grace. Paul does neither. He calls for real,
specific, active effort — "put off," "put on" — while grounding the whole
process in being "renewed in the spirit of your minds," a renewal that is
itself the Spirit's ongoing work, not unaided self-improvement.

Growth in holiness, then, looks less like waiting passively for a
sudden transformation and more like daily, deliberate choices — noticing
a specific old pattern and replacing it with a specific new one,
repeated over years, empowered by the Spirit renewing your mind along
the way.

## Prayer

Lord, show me a specific old pattern I need to put off today, and give
me grace to put on its opposite in its place. Renew my mind by your
Spirit as I do the practical work of change. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Name one specific "old self" pattern you're currently working to put
off, and the specific "new self" pattern you're putting on in its place.
