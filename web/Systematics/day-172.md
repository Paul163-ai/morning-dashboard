## Verse

"But when the Helper comes, whom I will send to you from the Father, the
Spirit of truth, who proceeds from the Father, he will bear witness
about me." (John 15:26)

## Explanation

We've established the Spirit's personhood and full deity; today we
consider how he relates, eternally, to the Father and the Son within the
Trinity we studied back in February. Jesus describes the Spirit as
"proceeding from the Father" and being sent by both the Father and the
Son, language the church has long used to describe the eternal
relationship distinguishing the Spirit's personal identity from the
Father's (who eternally begets the Son and from whom the Spirit
proceeds) and the Son's (who is eternally begotten). These are not
differences in essence or worth (Father, Son, and Spirit remain equally,
fully God), but real, eternal distinctions in how the three persons
relate to one another.

This is admittedly one of the harder edges of Trinitarian doctrine to
hold precisely, and Christian traditions have discussed the exact
wording of "proceeds from" at length across history. What matters most
for ordinary faith is simpler: the Spirit is not an independent, freely
acting third party alongside the Father and Son, but is sent by them,
in eternal relationship with them, and his mission is specifically to
"bear witness" about the Son: his work is never self-focused or
independent of the Father and Son's own purposes.

This should shape our expectations of the Spirit's activity: always in
harmony with, never competing against, what the Father and Son have
already revealed.

## Prayer

Holy Spirit, you proceed from the Father and are sent to bear witness to
the Son. Keep my understanding of your work always in harmony with what
the Father and Son have revealed. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has this doctrine (the Spirit's eternal relationship to the Father and
Son) ever come up for you before, and how does it shape your view of
the Trinity?
