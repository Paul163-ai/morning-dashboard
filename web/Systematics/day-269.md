## Verse

"Brothers, if anyone is caught in any transgression, you who are
spiritual should restore him in a spirit of gentleness." (Galatians 6:1)

## Explanation

In this section we turn to a subject many churches quietly avoid entirely:
church discipline, sometimes called "the keys of the kingdom" (Matthew
16:19), referring to the church's Christ-given authority to formally
address serious, unrepentant sin among its members. It's essential to
begin with its actual purpose, because church discipline is easily and
badly misunderstood as harsh punishment, when Scripture consistently
frames its goal as restoration: winning a struggling brother or sister
back, not driving them away or condemning them further.

Paul's instruction to the Galatians is telling in its tone: "a spirit of
gentleness," addressed to those who are "spiritual": mature, humble
believers, not self-righteous enforcers eager to catch and expose
others' failures. Genuine church discipline, rightly practiced, looks
far more like a family member pursuing a wandering relative with real
love and real cost, than an institution enforcing rules to protect its
own reputation.

This reframing matters enormously for how a congregation should feel
about discipline when it becomes necessary: not eager or self-righteous,
but grieved, gentle, and hopeful, always aiming, first and foremost, at
winning someone back, never merely at punishing or expelling them.

## Prayer

Lord, give your church a right heart toward discipline: gentle, humble,
aimed at restoration, never self-righteous or harsh. Help me approach a
struggling brother or sister the way you would. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you witnessed church discipline done poorly, harshly, or done well,
gently and restoratively? What made the difference?
