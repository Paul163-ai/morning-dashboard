## Verse

"Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name
of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit." — Matthew 28:19

## Explanation

Christ's own words of institution root baptism not in a formula to be
recited but in a relationship to be entered. To be baptized "into the
name" of the triune God is to be marked as belonging to Father, Son, and
Spirit — brought under his ownership, claimed as his, folded into the life
of the God who is three persons in one being. This is why baptism has
always been administered in this threefold name across the whole history
of the church, whatever other disagreements have divided Christians about
its mode or recipients.

Notice, too, where this command sits: at the very center of the Great
Commission, alongside making disciples and teaching obedience. Baptism was
never meant to be a private, optional add-on for especially devoted
believers — it is Christ's ordinary means of publicly marking someone as
his disciple. To trust Christ and never be baptized is, in the New
Testament's own logic, an unfinished response to the gospel, not a more
spiritual one.

## Prayer

Father, Son and Holy Spirit — one God, three persons — thank you for
claiming me as your own. Let my life increasingly match the name I was
baptized into. Amen.

## Reflection Question

The Great Commission ties baptism to discipleship, not just decision. Does
your own sense of what it means to follow Christ include being marked
publicly as his, or has it stayed private?
