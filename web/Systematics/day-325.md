## Verse

"For you yourselves are fully aware that the day of the Lord will come
like a thief in the night." — 1 Thessalonians 5:2

## Explanation

A thief doesn't announce a schedule. That's the entire point of Paul's
comparison — Christ's return will arrive without advance warning,
catching the unprepared entirely off guard, "while people are saying,
'There is peace and security,' then sudden destruction will come upon
them" (5:3). This image sits alongside yesterday's — visible and
unmistakable once it happens, yet utterly unpredictable in its timing
beforehand. Both things are true at once, and the tension between them is
deliberate.

Paul's application, though, is not fear but readiness: "you are not in
darkness, brothers, for that day to surprise you like a thief" (5:4).
Believers aren't meant to live anxiously calculating dates, nor to be
caught unprepared the way those without hope will be; they're meant to
live already awake, already sober, already dressed for the day whenever
it comes, the way someone expecting an important guest keeps the house in
order rather than scrambling only when the knock finally sounds. The
thief's unpredictability is frightening only to those living as though he
will never come.

## Prayer

Lord, keep me awake and ready, not anxious about timing but genuinely
prepared, so that your return finds me expecting you rather than caught
off guard. Amen.

## Reflection Question

If you knew Christ might return today, is there anything about how you're
currently living that you would want to change?
