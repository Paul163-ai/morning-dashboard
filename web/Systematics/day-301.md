## Verse

"Do not despise prophecies, but test everything; hold fast what is good."
— 1 Thessalonians 5:20–21

## Explanation

Whatever view one holds on whether prophecy continues, this instruction
applies: prophecy, if it occurs, is never to be received uncritically. That
alone should reframe how the gift is often imagined. New Testament
prophecy, on the continuationist reading many hold today, differs from Old
Testament prophecy and from the apostles' own Scripture-producing
authority — it functions less like infallible, canon-level revelation and
more like a Spirit-prompted impression, offered tentatively, always
subject to testing against Scripture and the discernment of the gathered
church, exactly as Paul instructs here.

This matters because both careless enthusiasm and careless dismissal miss
what Scripture actually asks of us. "Do not despise prophecies" rules out
treating any claim to Spirit-given insight with automatic suspicion or
mockery. "Test everything" rules out the opposite error, accepting
whatever is said in God's name without weighing it against his revealed
word. Whatever a church concludes about whether this gift continues, no
church is permitted the shortcut of skipping the testing — every claim to
speak for God, ancient or modern, must be measured against Scripture,
which alone carries final authority.

## Prayer

Lord, give your church discernment — neither despising what may be your
Spirit at work, nor accepting claims about your voice without testing them
against your word. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Which error do you find more tempting in your own heart: dismissing
Spirit-prompted impressions too quickly, or accepting them too
uncritically?
