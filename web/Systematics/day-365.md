## Verse

"So now faith, hope, and love abide, these three; but the greatest of
these is love." — 1 Corinthians 13:13

## Explanation

A year ago, on Day 1, we began with a single conviction: God has spoken,
and Scripture is trustworthy, sufficient, and alive. Everything since has
been the long, patient work of listening to what he said. We traced who
God is — three in one, unchanging, everywhere present, holy, just, and
overflowing with love proven at the cross. We considered what it means to
be human, made in his image and ruined by sin, and met Christ, fully God
and fully man, and the Spirit who applies his work to ordinary people
like us. We walked through how that redemption reaches a sinner — calling,
regeneration, faith, justification, adoption, sanctification, all of it
grace from beginning to end. We spent a season in the church — what it
is, how it gathers, the ordinary means by which Christ strengthens it,
and the mission it's sent on. And these last two months, we looked all
the way to the end: death defeated, Christ returning, judgment answered
by no condemnation, and an eternity of face-to-face fellowship with the
God this whole year has been about knowing.

Paul's verse is the fitting place to let all of it settle. Every doctrine
we've studied has been in service of these three things, and of the
greatest among them. Faith received what God revealed, all year, page by
page. Hope reached forward to everything we studied these last two
months — the resurrection, the wedding, the renewed earth, the face we
will finally see. But love is what all the rest was for. Not love as
sentiment, but love as the thing that outlasts even faith and hope
themselves, because in glory faith becomes sight and hope becomes
possession, while love simply continues, deepened, forever — the love
that moved God to speak in the first place, to send his Son, to make his
dwelling with his people at last. Whatever you take from this year of
study, let it be this: not merely more accurate ideas about God, but a
deeper love for the one all of it was always about.

## Prayer

Father, thank you for a year spent listening to your word and coming to
know you more truly. Whatever I've understood, and whatever I've missed,
let it end here — in love for you that outlasts every doctrine, every
question, and every day of this life. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Looking back over this whole year, has your knowledge of God grown into
greater love for him — and if not yet, what would it take for that to
happen this next year?
