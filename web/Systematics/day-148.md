## Verse

"I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the
sheep... I know my own and my own know me... you do not believe because
you are not among my sheep." — John 10:11, 14, 26

## Explanation

Yesterday we saw the atonement's infinite sufficiency; today we look at
its particular design and intent. Christ is careful here to specify
exactly whom his death is for: "the sheep" — not "the world" without
qualification, not "everyone without exception," but a particular flock,
known to him by name, given to him by the Father (John 10:29), who will
certainly hear his voice and follow him (John 10:27). Notice too the
striking reason Jesus gives some of his listeners for their unbelief in
verse 26: not merely that they had chosen wrongly, but that they were
"not among my sheep" — belonging is presented as prior to, and the
ground of, believing.

This is the language behind what is properly called particular
redemption, or definite atonement: Christ did not merely make salvation
possible for anyone who might, by their own will, eventually choose it.
He laid down his life to actually, effectively secure the salvation of
those the Father had already given him — a design that cannot fail,
since every sheep so given will certainly come and will never be
snatched away (John 10:28-29). Some faithful traditions read these
texts differently, and this devotional holds them charitably; but taken
on its own terms, John 10 describes an atonement with a particular,
named people in view from the start, not a general provision whose
success still hangs on an undetermined human response.

This is, if anything, more comforting than a merely general atonement
could be: your coming to Christ was never a matter of chance among an
undifferentiated crowd. You are known, by name, as one he specifically,
effectively died to save.

## Prayer

Good Shepherd, thank you that you laid down your life specifically for
your sheep, and that I did not merely stumble into salvation by chance.
Let me rest in being known, named, and effectually kept. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does it strengthen or unsettle you to think of Christ's death as
particularly, effectively intended for those the Father gave him, rather
than as a general offer awaiting your own decisive contribution?
