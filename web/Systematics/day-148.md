## Verse

"I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the
sheep... I know my own and my own know me." — John 10:11, 14

## Explanation

Alongside yesterday's emphasis on the atonement's wide sufficiency,
Scripture also speaks of Christ's death in more particular, personal
terms — laid down specifically "for the sheep," those the Father has
given him, whom he knows by name (John 10:14, 27-29) and who will
certainly come to him and never be lost. This is the language behind
what's sometimes called particular redemption: Christ's death not merely
making salvation possible in general, but actually, effectively securing
it for those he came specifically to save.

Faithful, careful readers of Scripture land in different places on how
exactly to hold this together with yesterday's "whole world" language —
whether the atonement's sufficiency is universal while its effective
application is particular, or whether other distinctions better capture
both emphases Scripture makes. This devotional isn't the place to settle
that discussion, and Christians who love Christ and take Scripture
seriously continue to disagree charitably about it.

What both emphases agree on, and what matters most practically, is this:
if you have come to Christ in genuine faith, you are not a marginal
afterthought caught up incidentally in a wide net. You are known,
personally and by name, as one of his own — a shepherd who lays down his
life specifically for sheep he calls his own.

## Prayer

Good Shepherd, thank you that you know me by name and laid down your life
for me personally, not as an afterthought. Let me rest in being truly
known and truly kept. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does it change anything for you to think of Christ's death not just as
"for the world" abstractly, but as personally, specifically for you?
