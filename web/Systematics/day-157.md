## Verse

"For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of
the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made
alive." — 1 Corinthians 15:21-22

## Explanation

We close this short look at the resurrection's meaning by returning to
the same "two Adams" logic we studied in April. Just as Adam's
representative headship brought death to everyone united to him by
ordinary human descent, Christ's representative headship brings
resurrection life to everyone united to him by faith. Paul isn't merely
saying Christ's resurrection is encouraging or symbolically hopeful for
believers — he's saying it is causally, representatively connected to
theirs, exactly as Adam's fall was connected to universal death.

This gives the earlier "firstfruits" picture (day 154) its full
theological weight. It's not simply that Christ rose first, chronologically,
among many unrelated resurrections. It's that his resurrection is the
guaranteed pattern and cause of every other resurrection that will
follow for those united to him — as certain as death itself has been
for those united to Adam.

This should settle, decisively, any anxiety about whether death is
truly the end. For someone in Christ, physical death is real, but it is
not final — exactly as certainly as Christ's own death was real but not
final. The empty tomb outside Jerusalem is the guarantee, not merely the
inspiration, for every believer's own future resurrection.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that my future resurrection is as certain as yours
already accomplished. Let that certainty steady me against every fear of
death. Amen.
