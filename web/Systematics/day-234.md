## Verse

"So is it with the resurrection of the dead. What is sown is perishable;
what is raised is imperishable... It is sown a natural body; it is raised
a spiritual body." (1 Corinthians 15:42, 44)

## Explanation

Paul describes the resurrection body using the image of a seed planted
in the ground: what's sown looks nothing like what eventually grows, yet
there's genuine continuity between them: the same seed, transformed
into something far greater. Your resurrection body will be genuinely
yours, continuous with your present body, and yet gloriously
transformed: imperishable rather than subject to decay, raised in glory
rather than in the weakness and limitation that mark life now, powerful
rather than frail.

Paul calls this a "spiritual body," not meaning immaterial or ghostly,
but a body fully suited to, and empowered by, the Spirit, no longer
limited by the constraints sin and the fall have placed on physical
existence. Every ache, every limitation, every weakness your body carries
now (the effects of illness, aging, disability, exhaustion) has no
place in this future body. What's sown in weakness will be raised in
power.

This offers real, concrete hope, not just vague comfort, especially for
anyone carrying chronic pain, disability, or the ordinary wear of aging.
The resurrection isn't a spiritual metaphor for something less tangible.
It's the promise of an actual, transformed, imperishable body: the full
undoing of everything sin and decay have done to physical existence.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the promise of an imperishable, powerful, glorious
body to come. Whatever weakness or pain my body carries now, let this
future hope encourage me today. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What specific weakness, pain, or limitation in your body right now would
you most long to see undone in the resurrection?
