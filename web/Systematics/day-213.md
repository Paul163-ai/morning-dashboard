## Verse

"For this is the will of God, your sanctification." — 1 Thessalonians 4:3

## Explanation

We left July with adoption — being welcomed, fully and permanently, into
God's family. That settled status raises a natural question: if you're
already declared righteous and already a beloved child, what happens next?
Sanctification is the answer: the lifelong process of actually becoming
holy in character and conduct, growing into the likeness of the family
you already, legally and relationally, belong to.

Theologians distinguish two aspects of this, which we'll take one at a
time in the days ahead. Definitive sanctification is a decisive, once-for-all
break with sin's dominating power, happening at conversion — you are no
longer a slave to sin, even though you still sin (we'll unpack this
tomorrow). Progressive sanctification is the slow, often uneven, lifelong
process of actually growing more holy in daily life — put more simply,
becoming in practice what you already are in status.

This is why sanctification always needs to be held after justification
and adoption, never before or instead of them. You don't pursue holiness
to become accepted by God. You pursue holiness because you already are
accepted, adopted, and secure — sanctification is the grateful, Spirit-
empowered process of growing into that identity, not the anxious process
of earning it.

## Prayer

Father, thank you that you've already made me your child. Now grow me
into someone who actually looks like your family — patient with the
process, but never satisfied to stay where I am. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you tend to pursue holiness out of gratitude for your secure standing,
or out of anxiety about whether you're accepted? What would shift that?
