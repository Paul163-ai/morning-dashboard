## Verse

"For Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the
unrighteous, that he might bring us to God." — 1 Peter 3:18

## Explanation

Peter states substitution with almost mathematical clarity: "the
righteous for the unrighteous." Notice both the precision and the
purpose. The precision: this isn't a vague exchange between roughly
similar parties, but the perfectly righteous one — the sinless Christ we
studied earlier this month — standing in for the genuinely unrighteous,
which is where every one of us belongs. The purpose: not merely
punishment absorbed for its own sake, but relationship restored — "that
he might bring us to God."

This second half is easy to overlook but essential. The atonement isn't
only about a penalty removed; it's about a relationship repaired. Sin
had separated humanity from God — a theme we studied at length in
April. The substitutionary death of the righteous for the unrighteous
doesn't just clear a debt; it opens a way back into God's presence,
which is exactly what "bring us to God" describes. Peter's phrase
"once for sins" also matters: unlike the repeated Old Testament
sacrifices, Christ's substitution was a single, sufficient, unrepeatable
act, needing no supplement.

Together, precision and purpose show what's at stake: not merely a
legal transaction, but the righteous one's death accomplishing what
nothing else could — bringing genuinely unrighteous people all the way
into God's presence.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, the righteous for the unrighteous — thank you for
accomplishing what I never could, and bringing me all the way to God.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you experience the cross mainly as debt cleared, or also as
relationship restored — being brought near to God himself?
