## Verse

"...in accordance with the gospel of the glory of the blessed God with
which I have been entrusted." (1 Timothy 1:11)

## Explanation

Blessedness is a striking, easily-missed attribute: God is not merely
worthy of blessing from us — he is, in himself, supremely happy. Paul
calls him "the blessed God," and elsewhere "the blessed and only
Sovereign" (1 Timothy 6:15), language that pictures a God who delights
fully in his own perfect being, his own works, and the perfect harmony of
the Trinity, without needing anything outside himself to complete that
joy.

This might seem like a minor theological footnote, but it quietly answers
a question many people carry without realising it: is God, deep down,
grim? Is devotion to him a solemn duty toward a serious, joyless being?
Scripture's answer is emphatically no. The God who commands our worship
is himself the happiest being in existence, entirely satisfied in
himself, and it's precisely that overflowing blessedness that the gospel
invites us into — not a joyless requirement, but a share in God's own
delight.

This also reframes what it means to be made in his image. If God is
supremely blessed, then part of reflecting him well is a genuine,
Spirit-produced joy in him — not manufactured cheerfulness, but the real
delight of a creature made to know a God who is himself, at the deepest
level, glad.

## Prayer

Blessed God, thank you that you are not grim or joyless, but supremely
happy in yourself. Let your joy become mine today, not as a performance,
but as a gift I'm invited to share. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you picture God, deep down, as joyful — or as solemn and hard to
please? Where might that picture need correcting?
