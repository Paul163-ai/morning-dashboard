## Verse

"In the same way also he took the cup, after supper, saying, 'This cup
that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.'" (Luke 22:20)

## Explanation

The bread and cup are not chosen at random; each signifies something
specific about what Christ accomplished. The bread pictures his body,
broken for us: the physical suffering and death of the incarnate Son, a
real body torn for real sin. The cup pictures his blood, poured out: the
life given as the payment sin demanded, since "without the shedding of
blood there is no forgiveness of sins" (Hebrews 9:22). Together they
proclaim a whole gospel in a single meal: a real death, paying a real
debt, for real people.

Notice, too, the phrase "new covenant in my blood." Jesus is deliberately
echoing Jeremiah's promise of a new covenant, unlike the one Israel broke,
written not on tablets of stone but on the heart (Jeremiah 31:31–34). The
old covenant was also sealed with blood, sprinkled on the people at
Sinai. Christ's blood seals a better covenant, one that actually secures
what it promises, because it rests on a perfect sacrifice rather than
Israel's fragile obedience.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the new covenant sealed in your blood, better than
anything I could keep by my own effort. Let the bread and cup remind me
each time of the real cost of my forgiveness. Amen.

## Reflection Question

How does remembering that the covenant is sealed in Christ's blood, not
your own performance, change how you approach your own failures in the
days ahead?
