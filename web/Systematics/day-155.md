## Verse

"God raised him up, loosening the pangs of death, because it was not
possible for him to be held by it." (Acts 2:24)

## Explanation

Having established the resurrection as historical fact, we now spend a
few days exploring what it actually means. The first meaning is
vindication. Christ had been condemned by religious authorities as a
blasphemer and executed by Rome as a criminal: the world's verdict on
him was guilty, shameful, finished. The resurrection is the Father's own
counter-verdict, overturning every human judgment against his Son.
Peter's phrase is striking: it was "not possible" for death to hold him,
not merely that God chose to raise him, but that death itself had no
rightful claim on the sinless one we studied last month.

This matters because the resurrection publicly settles a question the
crucifixion, on its own, left open: was Jesus telling the truth about
who he was? A dead man making divine claims could easily be dismissed as
mistaken or fraudulent. A man who rises from the dead exactly as he
predicted (Matthew 16:21) has his claims dramatically confirmed. The
resurrection is God's own signature on everything Christ said and did.

This should settle any lingering doubt about whether the shame of the
cross was the final word on Christ. It wasn't. The empty tomb is the
Father's decisive answer to the world's verdict.

## Prayer

Father, thank you for vindicating your Son, overturning every human
judgment against him. Let his vindication settle any doubts I carry
about who he truly is. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does seeing the resurrection as the Father's vindication of Christ's
claims strengthen your confidence in the rest of what Jesus said about
himself?
