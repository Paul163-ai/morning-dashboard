## Verse

"So then you are no longer strangers and aliens, but you are fellow
citizens with the saints and members of the household of God." —
Ephesians 2:19

## Explanation

Alongside the image of a body, Scripture describes the church as a
family — a household, with God himself as Father, and believers as
genuine, adopted brothers and sisters, not merely acquaintances who
happen to attend the same gatherings. Paul writes this to Gentile
believers who, by ethnic and religious background, would have been
considered complete outsiders to God's covenant people. The point is
deliberately inclusive: whatever divided you from other believers before —
background, ethnicity, social status — has been dissolved in this new
family, where the only qualification for belonging is being found in
Christ.

This matters because family language carries obligations family
metaphors alone don't fully capture through the "body" image. Families
don't just function together, like organs in a body; they belong to one
another, in ways that involve real commitment, real care, real presence
in each other's lives — not merely useful cooperation, but actual
love and loyalty, the kind Scripture repeatedly commands believers to
show one another (John 13:34–35, Romans 12:10).

This should shape how seriously you take the other believers around you,
whoever they are, wherever they come from. They aren't merely fellow
attendees at the same gatherings. In Christ, they are, genuinely, your
family.

## Prayer

Father, thank you for making me part of your household — not a stranger,
but a genuine member of your family, alongside every other believer.
Help me love the family I've been given, not just tolerate them. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a believer in your church you've related to more as an
acquaintance than as family? What would it look like to close that gap?
