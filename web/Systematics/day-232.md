## Verse

"'O death, where is your victory? O death, where is your sting?' The
sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law. But thanks be to
God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ."
(1 Corinthians 15:55–57)

## Explanation

We close this section where the whole Christian view of death ultimately
lands: not denial that death is real and painful, but confidence that its
power has been decisively broken. Paul personifies death and mocks it,
almost taunting a defeated enemy ("where is your sting?") because
Christ's own resurrection, which Paul has spent the entire chapter
defending and celebrating, has fundamentally altered what death actually
means and what it can ultimately do to a believer.

Paul identifies exactly what gave death its sting: sin, and the law's
just condemnation of sin. Remove that, as Christ has, through his own
death and resurrection, dealing fully with sin's guilt and penalty (truths
we studied back in July under justification), and death is left as a
defeated enemy, still real, still painful to walk through, but stripped
of its ultimate power to separate you from God or to have the final word
over your story.

This is why Christians can grieve death honestly and still hope
genuinely, at the very same time, not contradictory postures, but the
two halves of a single, coherent Christian response to mortality: real
loss, and real, settled victory, both true together, because of what
Christ has already done.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for stripping death of its sting through your own death
and resurrection. Let me grieve honestly when death touches my life, and
hope genuinely, both at once, because of what you have already won. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you allowed yourself to hold both honest grief and genuine hope
together when facing death, rather than feeling you must choose one?
