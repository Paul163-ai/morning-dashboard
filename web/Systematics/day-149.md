## Verse

"All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned—every one—to his own
way; and the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all." (Isaiah
53:6)

## Explanation

We come now to the sharpest, most direct statement of what happened at
the cross: penal substitution, Christ bearing the actual legal penalty
for sin, in the place of those who deserved it. Isaiah's structure here
is deliberate: first, the universal diagnosis ("all we like sheep have
gone astray," echoing the pervasiveness of sin we studied in April), and
then the solution in the very same breath: the LORD himself laying our
iniquity on him. This wasn't Rome's cruelty or Israel's rejection acting
alone; behind the human actors, this was God's own act, transferring
the legal weight of our sin onto his Son.

"Penal" is the key word tying this together with everything we studied
in March about God's justice and wrath. Sin incurs real guilt, deserving
real punishment. Penal substitution says Christ did not merely suffer
generally on our behalf, but specifically bore the punishment, the
penalty, the legal consequence, our sin deserved, so that justice was
genuinely satisfied, not set aside or ignored.

This is not one optional metaphor among many for what happened at the
cross; it's the legal, judicial core that gives the other pictures,
ransom, victory, exchange, their actual force and coherence.

## Prayer

Lord, you laid my iniquity on your Son, and he bore the punishment I
deserved. Let that legal, costly reality never become merely familiar to
me. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does "penal substitution" (Christ bearing your actual legal penalty)
feel more abstract or more personal to you, and why?
