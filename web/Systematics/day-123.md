## Verse

"'Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and they shall call
his name Immanuel' (which means, God with us)." (Matthew 1:23)

## Explanation

Matthew closes his account of Christ's birth by naming exactly what has
happened, translating the Hebrew title for his readers: Immanuel, God
with us. Not God observing us from a distance, not God occasionally
intervening in human affairs, but God himself, personally and bodily,
present among the people he came to save. This is the incarnation's
most personal implication: God did not merely send help. He came.

This should reshape how we picture God's posture toward human suffering,
weakness, and struggle. A God who stayed at a distance could be
suspected of not truly understanding what it costs to be human: hunger,
grief, temptation, exhaustion, betrayal. Immanuel rules that suspicion
out entirely. Whatever you face today, you do not face it with a God who
merely watches from outside human experience; you face it with a God
who has actually lived it, from the inside, as one of us.

"God with us" is also a fitting bridge into everything this month will
cover: his full deity and full humanity, his sinless obedience, his
atoning death. All of it flows from this single, foundational reality:
in Christ, God did not remain distant. He came near.

## Prayer

Immanuel, God with us, thank you that you have not remained distant from
my struggles, but entered fully into human experience. Be near to me
today as you promised. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where in your life right now do you most need to remember that God is
not distant, but "God with us"?
