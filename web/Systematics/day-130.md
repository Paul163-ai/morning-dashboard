## Verse

"For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our
weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet
without sin." (Hebrews 4:15)

## Explanation

Yesterday we established Christ's genuine humanity in principle; today's
verse makes it deeply personal. Christ was tempted "in every respect" as
we are, not a sanitized, token temptation easily brushed aside, but
real testing across the real range of human weakness: hunger in the
wilderness, the offer of power without the cross, the temptation to
abandon his mission in Gethsemane. His sinlessness (a theme we'll study
soon) didn't come from being insulated against temptation's real force;
it came from genuinely facing it and never yielding.

This is enormously significant for how we approach him in our own
weakness. A high priest untouched by real temptation might understand
sin only theoretically, the way a doctor who has never been sick might
understand illness only from textbooks. Christ's temptation was
experienced, not merely observed, which is precisely why Hebrews says
he "sympathizes," using a word that implies genuinely feeling with, not
just intellectually acknowledging, our struggle.

This should change how we approach him when tempted or struggling. Not
with the fear that he'll be distant or unable to relate, but with
confidence that he has faced exactly this kind of pressure before, and
can meet us in it as one who truly understands.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you that you were tempted in every respect as I am,
yet without sin. Meet me in my weakness today as one who truly
understands. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does knowing Christ was genuinely tempted, not just theoretically aware
of temptation, change how you bring your own struggles to him?
