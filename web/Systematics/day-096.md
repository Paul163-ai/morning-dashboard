## Verse

"Be sober-minded; be watchful. Your adversary the devil prowls around
like a roaring lion, seeking someone to devour." (1 Peter 5:8)

## Explanation

Peter doesn't write about Satan and demons in abstract, academic terms;
he writes with the urgency of warning fellow believers about a genuine,
active threat. The image is deliberately unsettling: a prowling lion,
not idle or distant, but hunting, patient, and opportunistic, looking for
whoever is unguarded or isolated enough to be caught.

This should correct two equally unhelpful instincts. One is to dismiss
demonic activity as primitive superstition, irrelevant to a modern
Christian's daily life, but Peter clearly doesn't share that view, and
neither does the rest of the New Testament, which regularly describes
demons as real, personal, malicious spiritual beings actively opposed to
God and his people. The other instinct swings the opposite way,
attributing every struggle, temptation, or hardship directly to demonic
attack, turning ordinary sin and suffering into constant spiritual
combat. Peter's actual instruction is neither denial nor obsession:
it's sober watchfulness: alert, but not anxious; aware, but not
preoccupied.

Notice too what Peter pairs this warning with just a few verses later:
casting all your anxieties on God, because he cares for you (1 Peter
5:7). Sober awareness of a real enemy is meant to drive us toward
dependence on God, not toward fear.

## Prayer

Lord, keep me sober-minded and watchful against real spiritual
opposition, without letting it become fear or obsession. Let it drive me
toward dependence on you instead. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does your own posture toward spiritual opposition lean more toward
dismissal or toward preoccupation, and what would sober watchfulness
look like instead?
