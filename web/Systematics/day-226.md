## Verse

"They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of
us, they would have continued with us." — 1 John 2:19

## Explanation

Perseverance also helps make sense of a hard reality every church
eventually encounters: people who seemed, for a time, to be genuine
believers, who later walk away entirely from the faith. John's
explanation isn't that they lost a salvation they truly had, but that
their departure reveals something about the nature of their original
profession: genuine faith, produced by the Spirit's regenerating work,
does continue — not perfectly, not without struggle, but genuinely, over
the course of a life. A departure that is final and complete suggests the
earlier profession, whatever it looked like at the time, wasn't the real
thing.

This is a difficult, sensitive truth to hold carefully. It's not meant
to be wielded as a tool for constantly second-guessing your own
salvation or anyone else's during ordinary seasons of doubt, struggle, or
even significant sin — every believer, as we saw earlier this month,
experiences real ongoing struggle, and struggle itself is not evidence of
false faith. It's meant, rather, to explain the specific and different
case of total, final apostasy: complete, unrepentant abandonment of
Christ, sustained over time.

Continuing in faith, even imperfectly, even with real struggle, is itself
part of the evidence that your faith was genuine to begin with — not a
requirement you're straining to meet, but a fruit that naturally grows
from a real root.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that ongoing struggle isn't evidence of false faith. Let
my continuing, however imperfect, be genuine fruit of your real work in
me, not something I'm anxiously trying to manufacture. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a difference in your mind between "struggling with sin" and
"walking away from Christ entirely"? Why does that distinction matter?
