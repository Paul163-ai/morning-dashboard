## Verse

"The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but
is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish, but that all
should reach repentance." — 2 Peter 3:9

## Explanation

Mercy and grace answer what God has already given; patience answers what
he withholds, and for how long. God's patience — sometimes called his
longsuffering — is his goodness displayed toward those who continue in
sin over an extended period of time, without his judgment falling
immediately. Peter is writing to believers wondering why Christ hasn't
yet returned to set everything right. His answer isn't that God has
forgotten his promise or lost interest. It's that the apparent delay is
itself an act of mercy, giving space for repentance that judgment would
foreclose.

This is worth sitting with, because patience can feel, from the inside,
indistinguishable from absence. When wrongs go unaddressed and prayers
seem to go unanswered, it's tempting to conclude God isn't paying
attention. Scripture insists the opposite: the waiting is deliberate, and
it is kind. Every day evil is not yet finally judged is a day someone,
somewhere, still has time to turn.

It's also a pattern worth imitating. The patience God extends toward the
world's slowness to repent is the same patience Paul asks believers to
show one another (Ephesians 4:2) — costly, deliberate, and not a
reluctant fallback but a genuine expression of love.

## Prayer

Father, thank you for your patience toward me and toward this world. Help
me not mistake your waiting for your absence, and give me grace to be as
patient with others as you have been with me. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where do you need to trust God's patience rather than demand an
immediate resolution?
