## Verse

"We know that our old self was crucified with him in order that the body
of sin might be brought to nothing, so that we would no longer be
enslaved to sin." (Romans 6:6)

## Explanation

Definitive sanctification is the decisive, once-for-all change that
happens the moment you're united to Christ: your old self, dominated and
enslaved by sin, was crucified with him. This isn't a claim that
believers stop sinning: Paul spends the rest of Romans 6 urging
Christians not to let sin reign in their bodies, which would be pointless
advice if sin were already impossible. It's a claim about power and
dominion: sin no longer rules you as your master, even though it still
tempts, still fights, still sometimes wins particular battles.

Think of the difference between a slave and a former slave who still
struggles with old habits and fears from their captivity. The legal and
relational reality has genuinely, decisively changed (freedom has been
won) even while the practical, lived reality of that freedom is still
being worked out. That's the picture Paul paints: you have already,
decisively, been freed from sin's mastery, even though the fight against
its remaining influence continues.

This distinction matters because it changes the posture of the fight.
You're not struggling against sin hoping to eventually win your freedom.
You're struggling against sin from a position of freedom already secured,
working out in daily practice what is already, decisively, true.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that sin no longer reigns over me as my master. Help me
fight remaining sin from a position of freedom already won, not as a
slave hoping to someday be free. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does your fight against a particular sin feel like fighting from freedom
already secured, or like fighting to become free? How might that
distinction change your approach?
