## Verse

"The flesh is no help at all. The words that I have spoken to you are
spirit and life." — John 6:63

## Explanation

"This is my body" has generated more disagreement among Christians than
almost any other phrase in the New Testament. The Roman Catholic doctrine
of transubstantiation holds that at the moment of consecration, the bread
and wine actually become Christ's body and blood in substance, though
retaining bread and wine's outward appearance. Martin Luther, while
breaking from Rome on much else, held a related view often called
consubstantiation: that Christ's body and blood are truly, physically
present "in, with, and under" the bread and wine, without the elements
ceasing to be bread and wine.

Both views take Jesus's words with maximal literalness. But John 6, where
Jesus speaks at length about eating his flesh and drinking his blood,
supplies its own interpretive key: after language that sounds shockingly
physical, Jesus clarifies that "the flesh is no help at all" and that his
words are to be understood spiritually. Read alongside his frequent use of
"I am" metaphors — the door, the vine, the bread of life — "this is my
body" reads most naturally as Christ speaking the way he characteristically
spoke: identifying a physical sign with the reality it represents, not
claiming a literal transformation of substance.

## Prayer

Lord, whatever debates believers have had over how you are present at your
table, let me approach it in faith, trusting that you meet your people
there in a real way, even if a mysterious one. Amen.
