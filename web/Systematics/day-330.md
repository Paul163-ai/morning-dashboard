## Verse

"Scoffers will come in the last days with scoffing... The Lord is not slow
to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you,
not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach
repentance." — 2 Peter 3:3,9

## Explanation

Peter anticipated that the delay itself would become an object of
mockery — enough time would pass that some would begin treating Christ's
promised return as a broken promise rather than a postponed one. His
answer reframes what looks like delay from God's own perspective: not
slowness, but patience, and specifically patience aimed at repentance —
every additional day before Christ's return is a day someone else has to
respond to the gospel, not evidence the promise has failed.

This closes out what we can responsibly say about timing: the signs are
real but not a countdown, the day is unknown even to the Son in his
earthly ministry, the mission is tied to the end without giving us a
calculable trigger, and what looks like delay is in fact God's patience,
not his forgetfulness. The right posture, then as now, is neither
frantic calculation nor weary cynicism, but genuine watchfulness matched
to genuine trust — living as people who take Christ's promise seriously
enough to be ready, and take his patience seriously enough not to demand
he hurry.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for your patience — with the world, and with me. Keep me
watching for your return without growing cynical about the wait. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has the length of the church's wait for Christ's return ever tempted you
toward cynicism rather than patience? How does 2 Peter 3:9 answer that
temptation?
