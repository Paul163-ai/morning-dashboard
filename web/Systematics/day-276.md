## Verse

"For Christ did not send me to baptize but to preach the gospel, and not
with words of eloquent wisdom, lest the cross of Christ be emptied of its
power." — 1 Corinthians 1:17

## Explanation

If baptism pictures union with Christ so vividly, why does Paul seem almost
dismissive of it here, thankful he baptized so few of the Corinthians
himself? Because the Corinthian church had begun treating baptism as a
badge of allegiance to whichever leader baptized them — turning a sign of
union with Christ into a source of division between Christians. Paul's
point is not that baptism doesn't matter, but that it must never be
allowed to eclipse the gospel it signifies.

This is the danger on the other side of yesterday's reading. Because
baptism so powerfully pictures death and resurrection with Christ, it is
tempting to collapse the sign into the reality — to assume that because
someone has been baptized, they must therefore be saved. Scripture never
permits that shortcut. Simon the sorcerer was baptized in Acts 8 and still
described as "in the gall of bitterness." The water is a sign appointed by
Christ, administered by his church, pointing to a reality that only faith
receives. Where there is no faith, the sign is administered honestly but
signifies nothing yet received.

## Prayer

Lord, keep me from resting in the sign instead of the reality it points
to. Let my confidence be in Christ himself, crucified and risen for me,
not in the ordinance alone. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there anything in your life — baptism, church membership, religious
upbringing — that you've been tempted to treat as proof of salvation
rather than as a sign pointing to Christ?
