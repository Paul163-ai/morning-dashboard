## Verse

"Now there are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit... To each is given
the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good." — 1 Corinthians
12:4,7

## Explanation

A spiritual gift, in the New Testament's sense, is any ability given by the
Holy Spirit and used in the service of the church. That definition is
deliberately broad — Paul's lists include dramatic gifts like prophecy and
healing alongside apparently unspectacular ones like service,
administration, and giving. What makes any of these a "spiritual" gift
isn't its visibility or intensity but its source (given by the Spirit) and
its purpose (used for the church's good, not for self-promotion).

This is a genuinely different category from natural talent, though the two
can overlap. A believer with a natural gift for organisation may find the
Spirit takes that same capacity and uses it, empowered and directed, for
building up the church — the natural ability becomes a spiritual gift when
the Spirit takes hold of it for that purpose. No Christian is left without
some gift; Paul assumes every believer has received one, which means the
church's health depends not on a few especially gifted members but on the
whole body actually exercising what each has been given.

## Prayer

Spirit of God, thank you for equipping your church with gifts, not leaving
any believer without a way to serve. Show me clearly what you've given me,
and help me use it for others, not for myself. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you ever thought of your own natural abilities as potentially
Spirit-given gifts for the church, or have you kept those two categories
separate? What might change if you didn't?
