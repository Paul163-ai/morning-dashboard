## Verse

"For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your
own doing; it is the gift of God." (Ephesians 2:8)

## Explanation

If mercy is God withholding the punishment we deserve, grace is God
giving the blessing we don't. The two attributes are close cousins, but
worth distinguishing: mercy answers misery, grace answers unworthiness.
You could imagine, in theory, a being merciful enough to spare you but
still unwilling to bless you. God does both — not only does he not give
us what our sin deserves, he goes on to give us what only his own
generosity could supply: adoption, righteousness, the Spirit, eternal
life.

Paul is emphatic that none of this is "your own doing." Grace, properly
understood, cannot be topped up by our contribution without ceasing to be
grace. The moment salvation becomes partly wage and partly gift, it stops
being grace in the biblical sense — Paul makes this exact argument in
Romans 11:6, that grace and works-based earning are mutually exclusive
categories. Grace is total or it isn't grace.

This is remarkably freeing news for anyone who has spent years quietly
trying to stay one good deed ahead of their guilt. You were never meant
to carry that ledger. The gift has already been given, in full, apart
from your doing.

## Prayer

Father, thank you for the gift I could never earn. Free me today from
trying to top up your grace with my own performance — let me simply
receive what you have freely given. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there an area of your walk with God where you're quietly treating
grace as a wage rather than a gift?
