## Verse

"And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his
glory." (John 1:14)

## Explanation

On Christmas Day, this whole section's study of resurrection hope finds its
starting point. Everything we've traced — Christ's risen body as the
pattern for our own, his resurrection as the firstfruits guaranteeing the
harvest, death's sting drawn out and disposed of — depends entirely on
this: the eternal Word, through whom all things were made, taking on
genuine human flesh, becoming exactly the kind of physical, mortal
creature whose body could then die, and rise, and become the guarantee
of everyone else's rising. There is no resurrection hope without a
manger first.

This is why Christmas has never been a separate, self-contained story
from Easter, or from the resurrection hope this whole final month has
traced. The child born in Bethlehem is the same body later crucified, the
same body raised imperishable, the same body ascended and coming again,
the same body every believer's own resurrection will one day be
patterned after. God did not merely visit humanity for a season; he
joined himself to human flesh permanently, so that flesh itself — yours,
mine, every believer's — might share in what his own flesh has already
accomplished. Today we celebrate not just his arrival, but the whole
story his arrival made possible.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for taking on flesh so that my flesh might one day
be raised like yours. As I celebrate your birth today, let me see it
joined to everything you accomplished afterward. Amen.

## Reflection Question

How does connecting Christmas to the resurrection hope of this whole
month change how you'll celebrate today?
