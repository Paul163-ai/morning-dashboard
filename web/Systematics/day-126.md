## Verse

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word
was God." — John 1:1

## Explanation

We turn now to a claim at the very centre of Christian faith: Christ's
full deity. John doesn't ease into this claim; he opens his entire
Gospel with it. Notice the careful precision — "the Word was with God"
(distinct from the Father, a real person in relationship) and "the Word
was God" (fully divine, sharing the very same nature). John isn't
describing a lesser, godlike being, or a created intermediary. He's
identifying Jesus, the Word made flesh (John 1:14), as God himself.

This isn't an isolated verse taken out of context. Thomas, after seeing
the risen Christ, calls him outright "my Lord and my God" (John 20:28),
and Jesus doesn't correct him. Paul calls him "our great God and Savior"
(Titus 2:13). The New Testament writers, all devout Jewish monotheists
for whom calling anyone but God "God" would have been unthinkable
blasphemy, repeatedly, deliberately apply exactly that title to Jesus.

This claim is the non-negotiable centre of who Christ is. Everything
else this month covers — his humanity, the union of his two natures, his
atoning death — only carries the weight Scripture gives it because the
one doing it is not merely a great teacher or a uniquely anointed man,
but God himself, become flesh.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you are fully God, not merely a messenger from God. Let that
truth anchor everything I believe about who you are and what you have
done. Amen.
