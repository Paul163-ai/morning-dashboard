## Verse

"Even when we were dead in our trespasses, [God] made us alive together
with Christ... and raised us up with him and seated us with him in the
heavenly places in Christ Jesus." — Ephesians 2:5–6

## Explanation

If union with Christ means sharing in his death, it equally means
sharing in his resurrection — and Paul's language here is again
strikingly personal and already accomplished: made alive together with
him, raised up with him, even seated with him, all in the past tense,
already true, not merely a future hope still awaited. Your union with
Christ means his resurrection victory is already, in a real spiritual
sense, your own present reality, not just a future promise.

This is where union with Christ becomes the engine behind Paul's
repeated calls, throughout his letters, to actually live differently:
"if then you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are
above" (Colossians 3:1). The command to live a new, resurrection-shaped
life isn't an arbitrary demand — it's an appeal to become, in daily
practice, what union with Christ has already, definitively made true.
You're not being told to somehow achieve resurrection life through
effort; you're being told to live consistently with a resurrection you
already, really share in.

This gives progressive sanctification, which we studied earlier this
month, its actual foundation: not striving to become alive, but living
out of a life already, genuinely given through union with the risen
Christ.

## Prayer

Father, thank you that in Christ, I am already raised, already alive,
already seated with him. Help me live today consistently with that
reality, not as something I'm still trying to achieve. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where in your life are you currently striving to "become" something
Scripture says union with Christ has already made true of you?
