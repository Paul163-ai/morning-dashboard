## Verse

"We are of good courage, and we would rather be away from the body and at
home with the Lord." — 2 Corinthians 5:8

## Explanation

In this section we turn to death itself — a subject Christians are sometimes
tempted to avoid, but one Scripture addresses directly and, remarkably,
with courage rather than dread. Paul doesn't describe physical death for
the believer as annihilation, unconscious sleep, or terrifying uncertainty.
He describes it in the most intimate, immediate terms available: being
"at home with the Lord" — not a distant future hope only, but the very
next conscious experience awaiting a believer the moment physical life
ends.

This is a striking claim, and worth sitting with plainly: for a
Christian, death is not primarily loss, but transition — leaving one home
(the body) for another, better one (immediate presence with Christ). This
doesn't mean death is trivial or that grief over it is somehow wrong;
Scripture elsewhere treats death as a genuine, painful enemy, still to be
finally defeated (1 Corinthians 15:26). But for the believer specifically,
its sting has already been fundamentally altered: what waits on the other
side isn't darkness or uncertainty, but Christ himself.

This is why Paul can describe facing death with "good courage" rather
than dread. Whatever fear death still naturally provokes, for those in
Christ, the actual destination waiting on the other side removes its
power to have the final word.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that death, for those in you, is not the end but a
homecoming. Give me Paul's courage as I think about my own mortality —
not denial of its weight, but confidence in what waits beyond it. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does thinking about death typically produce more fear or more hope in
you? How does Paul's description of "home with the Lord" speak into that?
