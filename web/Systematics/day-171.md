## Verse

"But Peter said, 'Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the
Holy Spirit... You have not lied to man but to God.'" — Acts 5:3-4

## Explanation

Having established the Spirit's personhood, we turn to his deity —
established here about as directly as Scripture establishes anything.
Peter accuses Ananias of lying "to the Holy Spirit," and in the very
next breath restates the same accusation as lying "to God." The
equivalence is total and deliberate: the Holy Spirit simply is God, not
a lesser divine influence or a created spiritual force operating on
God's behalf. This is one of the clearest single statements of the
Spirit's full deity anywhere in Scripture.

This fits the pattern of everything the New Testament attributes to the
Spirit elsewhere: omniscience (1 Corinthians 2:10-11), omnipresence
(Psalm 139:7-8), the power to create and give life (Genesis 1:2, Job
33:4, themes we'll return to shortly), and inclusion alongside the
Father and the Son in the baptismal formula as one undivided name
(Matthew 28:19) — not three separate, ranked beings, but one God, three
persons, a doctrine we studied carefully back in February.

This is why the Spirit's work in the believer, which we'll study at the
end of this month, carries the weight it does. It is not a lesser,
secondary blessing compared to knowing the Father or the Son. It is God
himself, personally and fully present and at work within his people.

## Prayer

Holy Spirit, you are fully God, not a lesser blessing. Thank you for
being personally and fully present with me, exactly as the Father and
the Son are. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you tend to think of the Holy Spirit as fully equal to the Father and
the Son, or as somehow a lesser or secondary member of the Trinity?
