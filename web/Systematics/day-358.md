## Verse

"O death, where is your victory? O death, where is your sting? ... But
thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus
Christ." — 1 Corinthians 15:55,57

## Explanation

Paul reaches this taunt only after building his whole case across the
rest of the chapter — Christ's own resurrection as firstfruits, the
believer's body sown perishable and raised imperishable, transformed to
be like Christ's own glorious body. Only now, with all of that
established, can he turn and mock death itself, the same enemy we named
on the very first day of this section, and ask it where its power has
gone. The answer, of course, is that death's sting — sin, and the law's
condemnation of it — has already been drawn out and disposed of at the
cross.

It is fitting that this shout of victory falls on Christmas Eve, the
night before we remember why any of this became possible at all. Every
promise traced across this whole final section of the year — death
defeated, the intermediate state with Christ, his certain return, final
judgment answered by no condemnation, heaven's inheritance kept secure,
a renewed creation, a resurrected body like his own — rests on one fact:
God himself stepping into human history as a child, so that this same
human flesh could one day be raised imperishable. Tomorrow, we remember
how that story began.

## Prayer

Father, thank you for the victory over death that Christ has already won.
As I remember tomorrow how he first came, let my heart be full of the
same triumph Paul shouts here. Amen.
