## Verse

"But God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners,
Christ died for us." (Romans 5:8)

## Explanation

We close this short survey of the atonement's various pictures
(substitution, ransom, victory) by asking how they fit together. Each
captures something real and distinct: substitution names the exchange
of guilt and righteousness; ransom names the price paid for our release
from bondage; victory names the defeat of hostile spiritual powers. None
of them is a false or invented add-on; all are drawn directly from
Scripture's own language about the cross.

What unites them, and what Paul's summary here quietly assumes as their
common foundation, is substitution: "Christ died for us," in our
place, on our behalf. The ransom had to be paid by someone standing in
for the captives; the victory over hostile powers was secured precisely
through Christ bearing, in himself, what those powers held against us
(our sin and guilt). Substitution is the load-bearing picture the others
depend on, even while each adds real, distinct meaning of its own.

This should free us from treating these as competing "theories" to
choose only one of. Scripture doesn't ask us to pick. It gives us a
cross rich enough to be described as substitution, ransom, and victory
all at once: one event, several true and complementary facets, held
together by the same love Paul names here: shown to us "while we were
still sinners."

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for a cross rich enough to be substitution, ransom, and
victory all at once. Let the full richness of what you accomplished
shape my worship today. Amen.
