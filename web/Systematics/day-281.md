## Verse

"There is one body and one Spirit... one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one
God and Father of all." (Ephesians 4:4–6)

## Explanation

Having heard both cases in this section, where does that leave us? The New
Testament's explicit narrative pattern, faith, then baptism, is
consistent and clear enough that believer's baptism has the stronger claim
to being the church's ordinary, New Testament practice. But the covenantal
argument for infant baptism is not a careless one; it takes seriously that
God has always dealt with his people in households and generations, not
merely as isolated individuals. This is a matter on which godly,
Scripture-saturated churches remain divided, and it is worth naming
honestly rather than pretending the answer is obvious to everyone who
reads carefully.

What Paul insists on in Ephesians 4 is not uniformity of practice but unity
of what baptism, at bottom, signifies: one Lord, one faith, one baptism.
Whatever a church's convictions about mode or recipients, baptism marks
every Christian, everywhere, as belonging to Christ and to no one else:
united to him, to his death and resurrection, and to every other believer
who bears the same mark. That shared meaning is worth far more unity than
our disagreements over its administration should be allowed to cost us.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the one baptism that marks every believer as yours.
Where Christians disagree in good faith about how it should be practiced,
give me charity rather than contempt, and keep my eyes on the one Lord we
share. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Think of a Christian you respect who holds the opposite view from you on
baptism. What would it look like to disagree with them while still
treasuring the "one baptism" you actually share?
