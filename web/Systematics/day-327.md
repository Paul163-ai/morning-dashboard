## Verse

"But concerning that day and hour no one knows, not even the angels of
heaven, nor the Son, but the Father only." — Matthew 24:36

## Explanation

This is one of the most direct statements in Scripture on the timing of
Christ's return, and it is worth letting its plainness stand rather than
explaining it away: no one knows, not the angels, not even the Son in his
incarnate ministry, only the Father. Every generation that has confidently
announced a date for Christ's return has, without exception, been wrong —
and Matthew 24:36 explains why that outcome was never in doubt. If the
Son himself did not claim to know the day, no calculation performed by
anyone since has grounds to claim otherwise.

This should produce a settled humility whenever date-setting resurfaces,
as it periodically does. It isn't a sign of superior spiritual insight to
identify the year or the decade; it's a claim to knowledge Christ himself
disclaimed. The appropriate response to not knowing the day isn't
anxious guessing but the readiness we read about a few days ago — living
faithfully now, precisely because the timing has been deliberately
withheld from us, so that faithfulness, not calculation, is what fills
the waiting.

## Prayer

Father, only you know the day. Keep me from the temptation to guess, and
give me instead a heart faithfully occupied with what I know you've
called me to now. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you ever been drawn in by confident predictions about the timing of
Christ's return? What does Matthew 24:36 say to that kind of confidence?
