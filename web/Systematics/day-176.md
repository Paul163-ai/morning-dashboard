## Verse

"I have filled him with the Spirit of God, with ability and
intelligence, with knowledge and all craftsmanship." (Exodus 31:3)

## Explanation

We turn to another facet of the Spirit's work across Old Testament
history: not only creating and sustaining life in general, but
specifically empowering particular people for particular tasks. This
verse describes Bezalel, a craftsman given supernatural skill by the
Spirit to build the tabernacle's furnishings, a strikingly ordinary,
practical task compared to prophecy or leading armies, yet named
directly as the Spirit's own equipping.

It's easy to assume the Spirit's Old Testament work was limited to
dramatic moments: prophets speaking oracles, judges leading Israel into
battle, kings anointed for rule. Exodus 31 broadens that picture
considerably. Skilled craftsmanship, artistic ability, practical
competence: these too are named as gifts the Spirit supplies, not
merely natural talent operating independently of him. The Spirit's
Old Testament activity touched leadership and prophecy, yes, but also
the ordinary skill needed to build something well.

This should reshape how narrowly we define "spiritual" work. If the
Spirit specifically equipped a craftsman for skilled, practical labour
long before Pentecost, then whatever competence and skill you bring to
your own work, however unglamorous it seems, is not necessarily
outside the Spirit's gracious equipping, even now.

## Prayer

Spirit of God, you equipped Bezalel with skill for ordinary, practical
work. Help me see my own competence and skill as gifts from you, not
merely as things I've achieved on my own. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a skill or ability you possess that you've never considered
might be, in some sense, a gift from the Spirit?
