## Verse

"And do not get drunk with wine, for that is debauchery, but be filled
with the Spirit." — Ephesians 5:18

## Explanation

Where yesterday's baptism in the Spirit is a completed, once-for-all
reality at conversion, being filled with the Spirit is different: an
ongoing, repeated command, addressed to believers who already have the
Spirit but are called to keep yielding to his influence and control more
fully. The Greek verb Paul uses here is in a form suggesting continuous
action — literally closer to "keep on being filled" — not a one-time
event, but a repeated posture of ongoing dependence.

Paul's contrast with drunkenness is instructive. Being drunk means
letting alcohol control your speech, your judgment, your behaviour.
Being filled with the Spirit means the same kind of controlling influence,
but in the opposite, life-giving direction — the Spirit shaping your
speech (the very next verses talk about singing and giving thanks), your
relationships, your whole manner of life, more and more fully.

This means the filling of the Spirit isn't a rare, dramatic experience
reserved for special occasions, but something believers are called to
actively seek and yield to in ordinary, daily life — asking, moment by
moment, for the Spirit's control rather than relying on your own
strength, insight, or willpower to navigate the day.

## Prayer

Holy Spirit, fill me again today. I don't want to navigate this day in
my own strength — take fuller control of my speech, my choices, and my
heart. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What would it look like, practically, to actively seek the Spirit's
