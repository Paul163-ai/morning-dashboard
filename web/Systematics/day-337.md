## Verse

"Here is a call for the endurance of the saints, those who keep the
commandments of God and their faith in Jesus." (Revelation 14:12)

## Explanation

Revelation, written to churches under real and immediate persecution,
doesn't respond to coming tribulation with detailed predictions to satisfy
curiosity; it responds with a call to endurance. Whatever precise shape
the tribulation takes, and whenever exactly it falls relative to Christ's
return, John's pastoral aim throughout the whole book is the same:
sustain believers in patient, faithful obedience through hardship they
cannot avoid, rather than equip them to escape it through superior
knowledge of the timeline.

This is the note this short section should leave us on: not anxious
calculation about how bad things will get or when, but settled resolve to
keep the commandments of God and faith in Jesus regardless of what comes.
The saints Revelation calls blessed are not the ones who correctly
predicted the tribulation's arrival; they are the ones who endured it
faithfully, trusting a Lord who had already told them the outcome was
secure. That same endurance, not prediction, is what every generation of
Christians facing hardship — great or small — is actually called to.

## Prayer

Lord, give me endurance, not merely information. Whatever tribulation
comes in my lifetime or the world's, keep me faithful to your
commandments and to Jesus until the end. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What would it look like, practically, for you to build the kind of
patient endurance Revelation calls blessed, before hardship actually
arrives?
