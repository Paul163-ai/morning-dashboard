## Verse

"For though I am absent in body, yet I am with you in spirit, rejoicing
to see your good order and the firmness of your faith in Christ." —
Colossians 2:5

## Explanation

We close this section holding two truths about church discipline together,
both genuinely necessary. It is serious — Paul's instruction in
1 Corinthians 5 to remove an unrepentant, sexually immoral member from
fellowship is stark and weighty, treating the matter with real gravity,
not casual dismissal. Unrepentant, defiant sin, sustained despite every
loving appeal, is genuinely dangerous, both to the individual involved
and to the wider church's witness and health.

And yet it is also, always, full of hope. Even Paul's severe instruction
in 1 Corinthians 5 is followed, in his second letter to the same church,
by an appeal to restore and reaffirm love toward the same individual,
once repentance had genuinely occurred (2 Corinthians 2:6–8) — the goal
achieved, the wanderer won back, exactly as Galatians 6:1 described
earlier. Discipline, rightly practiced, always leaves the door open
for restoration, hoping and working for exactly that outcome, never
treating separation as a permanent, unconditional verdict.

This is why healthy discipline requires real wisdom and real love held
together — taking sin seriously enough to act, while never losing sight
of the restoration that remains the entire point of acting at all.

## Prayer

Lord, help your church hold discipline's seriousness and its hope
together — never minimising sin, never losing sight of restoration as
the goal. Give me this same balance in how I relate to those who
struggle. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you tend toward minimising sin's seriousness, or toward losing sight
of hope for restoration? Which balance do you need to grow in?
