## Verse

"For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain." — Philippians 1:21

## Explanation

Paul writes these words from prison, genuinely uncertain whether he will
live or be executed, and comes to a conclusion that only makes sense in
light of yesterday's reading held together with resurrection hope: death
is gain, not because life is worthless, but because what lies beyond death
is better still. He goes on to say that departing to be with Christ is
"far better" (1:23) than remaining — not because Paul despised his life
or ministry, which he calls genuinely fruitful, but because being with
Christ himself exceeds even fruitful, Christ-centred labour here.

This is a strange kind of math for a culture that treats death as
something to be avoided, denied, or postponed at nearly any cost, and
even for Christians who can quietly absorb that same instinct without
examining it. Paul's "gain" isn't relief from suffering, though there is
that too — it's positive gain, more of Christ, not less. That reframing
doesn't remove the sting of loss for those left behind, or make dying
easy, but it does mean a Christian can genuinely say, without denial or
bravado, that death delivers them somewhere better than anywhere life
could take them.

## Prayer

Lord, teach me Paul's math — that being with you is gain, not merely the
end of hardship. Whether I live long or die soon, let Christ be what my
life and death are both about. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does "to die is gain" feel true to you right now, or does it feel like a
truth you affirm but haven't yet let sink into how you actually feel about
death?
