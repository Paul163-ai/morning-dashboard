## Verse

"In your struggle against sin you have not yet resisted to the point of
shedding your blood." — Hebrews 12:4

## Explanation

We close this month's study of sin — and April itself — with a call to
match our resistance to sin's actual seriousness. Hebrews addresses
believers who felt they had already suffered greatly in their fight
against sin, and gently corrects the scale: compared to Christ, who
resisted sin all the way to bloodshed on the cross, their struggle,
however real, hadn't yet cost them that much. The verse isn't meant to
minimise genuine struggle, but to recalibrate how seriously we take
the fight.

Everything we've studied this month — sin's origin in a deliberate
choice to trust our own judgment, its definition as lawlessness and
falling short of God's glory, its inheritance from Adam reaching every
person, its expression in both action and omission, its corruption of
mind, will, and emotion, and its total pervasiveness across humanity —
adds up to a single, sobering conclusion: sin is not a minor
inconvenience to be casually managed. It's serious enough that dealing
with it required the bloodshed of the Son of God himself.

As we move into May and begin studying the person and work of Christ,
this is the necessary backdrop. The incarnation, the atonement, the
cross — all of it makes sense only against the seriousness of exactly
what we've spent this month examining. The next few months are the
answer to this one.

## Prayer

Lord, help me take sin as seriously as your own struggle against it, all
the way to bloodshed. As I move toward studying Christ's work, let this
month's honesty about sin deepen my gratitude for what it cost. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Looking back over this month's study of sin, what has most reshaped how
seriously you take your own ongoing struggle against it?
