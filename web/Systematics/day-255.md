## Verse

"If your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault, between you
and him alone." (Matthew 18:15)

## Explanation

The third traditional mark of a true church is the practice of church
discipline: a congregation that takes sin seriously enough to actually
address it, following the pattern Christ himself laid out, rather than
either ignoring persistent, unrepentant sin entirely or handling it
carelessly and without love. We'll spend several days on this later this
month, but it's worth naming now as a genuine mark of a healthy church,
alongside faithful preaching and rightly practiced sacraments.

A church without any meaningful practice of discipline tends, over time,
to lose its distinctiveness altogether, unable or unwilling to say that
any belief or behaviour actually falls outside what's acceptable for a
professing believer, until the line between the church and the
surrounding culture blurs beyond recognition. This isn't a mark most
churches emphasise or practice comfortably, and it's easy to understand
why: discipline is hard, often painful, easily done badly. But its
absence, over time, is itself a serious warning sign.

Together, these three marks (faithful preaching, right sacraments, and
genuine discipline) give you real, substantive criteria for recognising
a healthy, true church, rather than relying only on more superficial
measures like size, style, or personal comfort.

## Prayer

Lord, give your church courage to take sin seriously, in love, following
the pattern Christ gave. Help me support, rather than resist, genuine
accountability in my own church community. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Of the three marks in this section (preaching, sacraments, discipline), which
has your own church emphasised most, and which might need more
