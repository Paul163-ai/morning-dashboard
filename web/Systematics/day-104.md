## Verse

"And do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Rather
fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell." (Matthew 10:28)

## Explanation

We turn now to a different question about human nature: what are we
actually made of? Jesus' words assume something important: that a
person is not simply their physical body. Killing the body, however
final it looks, does not touch the soul; the two are distinguishable,
even if normally united. This is the basic claim behind what
theologians usually call dualism: a human being is both material (body)
and immaterial (soul or spirit), genuinely united in life, yet capable
of existing separately, as they do between death and the future
resurrection.

This matters enormously for how we think about death and suffering.
Persecution, illness, even death itself, has real but limited reach:
it can end physical life, but it cannot touch the deepest part of who
you are, the part that continues in God's presence when the body fails.
Jesus isn't minimising the reality or the grief of bodily death; he's
placing it within a larger truth about what a person actually is, so
that fear of it need not be ultimate.

Tomorrow we'll look at the other side of this truth: that the body
matters too, and isn't a disposable shell. Today, simply rest in the
comfort Jesus offers: whatever threatens your body, your soul remains
safely beyond its reach, held by God himself.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that my soul is beyond the reach of anything that can
harm my body. Free me today from fears that only have power over the
lesser, temporary part of who I am. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What fear about your body, health, or mortality do you need to hold
differently in light of the fact that your soul is beyond its reach?
