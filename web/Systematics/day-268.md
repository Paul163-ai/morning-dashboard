## Verse

"And when they had appointed elders for them in every church, with
prayer and fasting they committed them to the Lord in whom they had
believed." — Acts 14:23

## Explanation

Notice the consistent pattern across the New Testament: elders, plural,
in every church — never a single, unaccountable individual holding all
teaching and leadership authority alone. This plurality isn't incidental;
it's a deliberate, protective structure, built into how the earliest
churches were organised from the very beginning.

Shared leadership among multiple qualified elders provides real
safeguards a single leader, however gifted, cannot provide alone: mutual
accountability, so no one leader's blind spots, sin, or error goes
unchecked; shared wisdom, so major decisions benefit from multiple godly
perspectives rather than one person's judgment alone; and resilience,
so a congregation isn't left devastated or destabilised if a single
leader falls into serious sin or is otherwise unable to continue.

Church history is full of painful examples of what happens when a single
leader accumulates unchecked authority — spiritual abuse, doctrinal
drift, moral failure hidden or excused because no genuine peer
accountability existed to catch it. This is one of the strongest,
most consistent arguments for plural eldership found across nearly every
tradition of church government we surveyed earlier this month, whatever
their other differences: no single person, however gifted or trusted,
should hold unchecked spiritual authority alone.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the wisdom of shared leadership in your church.
Protect leaders everywhere from the isolation and temptation of
unaccountable authority, and give congregations discernment to expect
this pattern. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does your own church practice genuine plural leadership, with real
mutual accountability among its leaders? What signs would show this is
healthy, or that it's missing?
