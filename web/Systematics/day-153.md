## Verse

"'Why do you seek the living among the dead? He is not here, but has
risen.'" (Luke 24:5-6)

## Explanation

The women arrive at the tomb prepared for grief, carrying spices for a
body they expect to find. What they encounter instead is an empty tomb
and a question that reframes everything: why look for the living among
the dead? The resurrection isn't presented in Scripture as a beautiful
metaphor for hope or renewal: it's reported as a specific, jarring,
historical claim: the body that had been genuinely buried two days
before was genuinely, physically no longer there, because Christ had
genuinely, physically risen.

This claim rests on real, checkable evidence the Gospel writers were
willing to put forward, including some of its least convenient details:
women as the first witnesses, whose testimony carried little legal
weight in that culture, hardly the detail anyone inventing a
resurrection story would choose to include. Paul later lists named,
still-living witnesses who could be questioned directly (1 Corinthians
15:5-8), staking the entire Christian claim on events that had actually
happened, in public, and could in principle be investigated.

This should shape how we hold the resurrection: not as a comforting
symbol we choose to believe regardless of evidence, but as a claim about
what actually happened, which is exactly why it can bear the weight of
everything else we believe.

## Prayer

Risen Lord Jesus, you are not among the dead; you are alive. Let my
faith rest on the solid historical reality of your resurrection, not
merely on a comforting idea. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you ever treated the resurrection more as a symbol of hope than as
a historical event that actually happened? What difference does that
distinction make?
