## Verse

"Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for
us." — Galatians 3:13

## Explanation

We close this section on the nature of the atonement with Paul's own
striking summary: Christ became a curse for us. Paul is drawing on
Deuteronomy's language, where anyone hung on a tree was understood to be
under God's curse (Deuteronomy 21:23) — and applies it directly to the
crucifixion, but with a decisive twist. Christ was not cursed for his
own sin; he had none, as we saw earlier this month. He took on a curse
that belonged to us, so that the curse itself — the just penalty owed
for breaking God's law — would be exhausted in him rather than falling
on us.

This is substitution described in its most vivid, almost shocking terms.
It's not merely that Christ suffered a difficult death; it's that he
absorbed, in himself, the specific covenantal curse that Scripture says
falls on lawbreakers — becoming, legally and really, what we deserved
to be, so that we could become, legally and really, what only he
deserved to be. This is the same exchange described in different words
across Isaiah 53, 1 Peter 3, and here in Galatians: his place taken for
ours, our place opened to us through him.

Three days is not nearly enough to exhaust the nature of the atonement,
but the pattern across all of them is consistent and clear: a real
substitution, a real exchange, accomplished once, sufficient forever.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for becoming a curse for me, so the curse I
deserved would never fall on me. Let this exchange be the ground I stand
on today. Amen.
