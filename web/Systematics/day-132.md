## Verse

"Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God
a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a
servant, being born in the likeness of men." — Philippians 2:6-7

## Explanation

Having established Christ's full deity and full humanity separately, we
now turn to perhaps the deepest mystery of all: how the two are united in
a single person. Theologians call this the hypostatic union — one
person, Jesus Christ, possessing two complete and distinct natures,
fully divine and fully human, neither nature diminishing or being
absorbed into the other.

Paul's language here is careful. Christ did not stop being "in the form
of God" in order to take "the form of a servant" — he didn't trade one
nature for the other, or blend them into some hybrid third thing that
was neither fully God nor fully man. He added a genuine human nature to
his eternal divine nature, taking on humanity without in any way
setting aside his deity. "Emptied himself" (a phrase debated across
church history) is best understood not as Christ giving up his divine
attributes, but as his laying aside the outward display of divine glory
and privilege, taking instead the humble, obedient form of a servant.

This is genuinely difficult to fully picture, and church history is
littered with attempted explanations that overcorrected in one direction
or another. What Scripture insists on, without fully explaining the
mechanics, is this: one person, two complete natures, neither
compromised.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you are fully God and fully man, in a union I cannot fully
explain but can wholly trust. Thank you for not setting aside your deity
to take on my humanity, but adding one to the other for my sake. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Which is harder for you to hold onto without letting it slip — Christ's
full deity, or his full humanity — and why?
