## Verse

"Who will transform our lowly body to be like his glorious body, by the
power that enables him even to subject all things to himself." —
Philippians 3:21

## Explanation

Paul gives the resurrection body a specific pattern to be measured
against: Christ's own resurrected body. This is worth pausing on,
because it means the resurrection isn't a leap into the unknown; the
church already has a preview of exactly what a glorified human body
looks like, in the risen Christ himself — recognisable (his disciples
knew him), physical (he ate, he could be touched), yet transformed beyond
the limitations of ordinary human embodiment (he passed through locked
doors, appeared and vanished at will). Whatever mysteries remain about
resurrected bodies, this much is not speculative: they will be like his.

The power behind this transformation is the same power Paul names at the
verse's end — the power that subjects all things to Christ, the same
total, cosmic authority we've traced across this whole final section of
the year. This is not a modest hope, wistfully hoped for against long
odds; it rests on the same power already demonstrated in Christ's own
resurrection and already at work subjecting the whole created order to
him. The body you carry now, lowly and limited as it is, has that same
power committed to its future transformation.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you that my future body will be like your own glorious
one. By the same power that raised you, transform me — I trust the
pattern because I trust the power behind it. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does thinking of your future resurrection body as patterned specifically
on Christ's own risen body make the promise feel more concrete to you?
