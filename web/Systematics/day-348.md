## Verse

"He will render to each one according to his works... For God shows no
partiality." — Romans 2:6,11

## Explanation

It's tempting to think of hell as an excess — a punishment somehow
disproportionate to the offence, evidence of a harsher God than the one
who forgives so freely elsewhere in Scripture. Paul's language here
points the other direction: judgment is rendered "according to works,"
exactly proportioned, and administered by a God who "shows no
partiality" — no favouritism protecting some from a standard applied
strictly to others. Whatever hell is, Scripture insists it is not
arbitrary cruelty; it is justice, precisely calibrated, from a judge who
cannot be accused of unfairness.

This is cold comfort on its own, and Scripture doesn't leave it there.
The same God whose justice demands an accounting for sin is the God who,
in Christ, absorbed that very accounting on behalf of everyone who
trusts him — which is why these hard days on hell sit inside a whole
year spent tracing what Christ accomplished at the cross. Hell tells us
sin is more serious than we tend to feel it is; the gospel tells us
Christ's payment for it was sufficient and complete. Neither truth
should be allowed to eclipse the other.

## Prayer

Lord, your justice is real, and I don't want to soften it into something
less than it is. Thank you that Christ's payment for my sin was equally
real, and equally sufficient. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does thinking of hell as precise justice, rather than arbitrary cruelty,
change how you hold this doctrine — harder to dismiss, but perhaps also
easier to trust as coming from a God who is genuinely fair?
