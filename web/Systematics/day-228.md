## Verse

"The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of
God." — Romans 8:16

## Explanation

One source of assurance is internal: the Spirit's own testimony,
witnessing alongside your own spirit, confirming your identity as God's
child. This isn't primarily a dramatic, ecstatic experience, though it
can be felt deeply at times — it's often quieter, closer to a settled,
Spirit-given confidence that grows as you pray, worship, read Scripture,
and relate to God as Father, especially in the intimate, trusting "Abba"
language Paul describes in the verse just before this one.

This inward witness matters, but it's worth holding alongside the
external, more objective evidence assurance also rests on — the promises
of Scripture itself, and the visible fruit of a changed life, which we'll
look at tomorrow. Feelings alone can be unreliable, as we noted back in
January; they can be affected by circumstance, mood, or even physical
health, quite apart from your actual spiritual standing. But the Spirit's
inward witness isn't merely a feeling to be trusted uncritically — it's a
genuine work of God, confirming truth that's also grounded, objectively,
in what Scripture promises.

If you sense the Spirit's quiet confirmation in your own heart that
you belong to God, take it seriously as real evidence — while also
checking it, as we'll do in the days ahead, against Scripture's objective
promises and your own life's actual fruit.

## Prayer

Holy Spirit, thank you for bearing witness with my spirit that I am
God's child. Deepen that inward confidence, and help me hold it alongside
your objective promises, not apart from them. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you ever sensed the Spirit's quiet inward confirmation of belonging
to God? What did that feel like, and how did you respond to it?
