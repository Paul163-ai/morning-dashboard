## Verse

"I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but
Christ who lives in me." (Galatians 2:20)

## Explanation

If union with Christ means being genuinely, personally joined to him, it
means sharing in what happened to him, not merely benefiting from his
death from a safe distance, as an observer, but actually, in some real,
spiritual sense, having died with him. Paul's language is deliberately
personal and radical: "I have been crucified with Christ," not merely
"Christ died for me," true as that also is, but a claim about your own
identity being fundamentally altered through union with his death.

This connects directly to sanctification's definitive break with sin,
which we studied earlier this month (Romans 6:6, "our old self was
crucified with him"). That decisive death to sin's dominion isn't a
separate doctrine from union with Christ; it's one of its direct
outworkings. Because you are united to Christ, his death becomes,
genuinely, your death: your old, sin-enslaved identity ended at the
cross, together with him.

This is a strange, wonderful thing to actually believe about yourself.
The old "you," defined by sin's mastery, by guilt, by separation from
God, is not merely forgiven. In union with Christ, it has actually,
really died. What lives now is a genuinely new identity, Christ himself
living in and through you.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you that in union with you, my old self was truly
crucified. Help me live today from that new identity, not the old one
that has already died with you. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there an old pattern of identity (shame, sin's mastery, old labels)
you still relate to as though it were alive, when Scripture says it has
actually died with Christ?
