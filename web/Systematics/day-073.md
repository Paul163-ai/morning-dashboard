## Verse

"In him we have obtained an inheritance, having been predestined
according to the purpose of him who works all things according to the
counsel of his will." (Ephesians 1:11)

## Explanation

We come now to one of the harder attributes to hold together clearly:
God's will. Theologians have long found it useful to distinguish two
senses in which Scripture speaks of God "willing" something, and today we
look at the first: God's decreed will (sometimes called his secret or
sovereign will) — the will by which he ordains everything that actually
comes to pass, without exception. Ephesians 1:11 could hardly be more
sweeping: he "works all things according to the counsel of his will," not
most things, not the important things, but all things.

This is a claim about God's ultimate governance of history, not a denial
of real causes within it — tomorrow we'll look at how human choices and
responsibility fit inside this. But before we get there, it's worth
sitting with the comfort this attribute is meant to provide: nothing
that happens to you is falling outside the reach of a plan, or catching
God by surprise. His decreed will is settled and certain, precisely
because it flows from a counsel that is itself perfectly wise and good.

This isn't fatalism, which shrugs at outcomes as impersonal fate. It's
trust placed in a Person whose will, however hidden from us in the
moment, we have every reason from his character to trust.

## Prayer

Sovereign Lord, nothing that happens to me today falls outside your will.
Help me trust the wisdom behind your decree even when I cannot see its
purpose. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What current circumstance do you find hardest to believe is within God's
sovereign will — and why?
