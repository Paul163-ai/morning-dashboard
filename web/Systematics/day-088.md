## Verse

"This, the first of his signs, Jesus did at Cana in Galilee, and manifested
his glory. And his disciples believed in him." (John 2:11)

## Explanation

We close this month's study of God's character with a theme that flows
directly from providence: miracles. If providence is God's ordinary,
regular sustaining and governing of creation — the reliable patterns we
call the laws of nature — a miracle is best understood as a less common
kind of his providence, in which he acts to arouse people's awe and
wonder, and to bear witness to himself, often departing from the normal
patterns he otherwise upholds. John's Gospel favours the word "signs" for
exactly this reason: a miracle isn't merely a display of raw power, it
points beyond itself to something true about who Jesus is.

Notice the result at Cana: Jesus "manifested his glory," and the
disciples "believed in him." Miracles in Scripture are rarely presented
as ends in themselves — magic tricks meant only to impress. They serve
revelation, confirming a message and a person, inviting a response of
faith rather than mere astonishment.

This also clarifies what a miracle is not: it's not a violation of a
"broken" natural order, as though God were fighting against his own
laws. It's the same God who upholds nature every day, choosing on
occasion to act outside its normal patterns for his own good reasons —
the very same power at work, deployed in an unusual way.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the signs throughout Scripture that reveal your glory
and invite faith. Help me see your ordinary providence with as much awe
as I would a miracle. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where in your life might you be overlooking God's ordinary providence
because you're only watching for the extraordinary?
