## Verse

"For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the
image of his Son, in order that he might be the firstborn among many
brothers." — Romans 8:29

## Explanation

We close this section by asking what sanctification is actually aiming at.
It isn't a vague, generic sense of self-improvement or "being a good
person" by whatever cultural standard happens to be current. Paul is
specific: conformity to the image of Christ himself — becoming, in
character and conduct, increasingly like the Son, the pattern and goal
God has had in view from before you were even born, "predestined,"
alongside your election, back in eternity.

This gives sanctification a clear, concrete direction, not just an
open-ended sense of "getting better." Every specific area of growth —
patience, humility, generosity, self-control, love for enemies — is
measured against a single, concrete standard: does this look like Jesus?
This should be genuinely encouraging rather than intimidating, because
the goal was never left vague or up to your own imagination. You have an
actual person to look to, a life fully recorded in the Gospels, showing
exactly what the finished project looks like.

In this section we've traced sanctification from its decisive beginning
(definitive, at conversion) through its daily practice (progressive, put
off and put on) through its method (God's work and yours together) to its
goal (Christlikeness). Tomorrow we turn to something this section has
already implied: the ongoing, real fight against sin that continues even
as this growth unfolds.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for being the actual pattern I'm being shaped
toward, not a vague ideal I have to invent myself. Conform me more to
your image today, in specific, concrete ways. Amen.

## Reflection Question

If you picture the "finished project" of your own sanctification, does it
actually look like Jesus, or like some other standard you've absorbed
from elsewhere?
