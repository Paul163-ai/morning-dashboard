## Verse

"So when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a
delight to the eyes, and that the tree was to be desired to make one
wise, she took of its fruit and ate, and she also gave some to her
husband who was with her, and he ate." (Genesis 3:6)

## Explanation

Having spent a good stretch of days on what humanity was made to be, we now
turn to the sober reality of what went wrong. Genesis 3:6 describes the
first human sin with almost clinical detail: not a dramatic act of
open rebellion, but a quiet reasoning process: the fruit looked good,
looked desirable, looked wise to take. Sin's entry point wasn't
irrationality; it was a deliberate choice to trust one's own
assessment ("good for food... delight to the eyes... desired to make
one wise") over God's explicit word.

This is worth noticing because it's the same pattern sin still takes.
Temptation rarely presents itself as obviously ugly or foolish; it
presents itself, as it did to Eve, as reasonable, desirable, even wise.
The serpent's earlier lie ("you will not surely die," Genesis 3:4) had
already reframed God's clear command as an obstacle to something good,
rather than as protection from something harmful. Once God's word was
recategorised as restriction rather than gift, the fruit itself did the
rest of the persuading.

The tragedy of Genesis 3:6 isn't only that Adam and Eve disobeyed; it's
that they had every reason to trust God's word and chose their own
judgment instead, the same choice every subsequent sin, in some form,
repeats.

## Prayer

Lord, forgive me for the times I've trusted my own judgment over your
clear word because something looked good, desirable, or wise on its own
terms. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where has something recently looked "good, desirable, or wise" to you in
a way that quietly contradicted what God has clearly said?
