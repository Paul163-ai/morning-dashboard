## Verse

"But we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the
word." (Acts 6:4)

## Explanation

We close September with the two means of grace that stand, in a sense,
beneath and behind every other practice we've studied this month:
Scripture and prayer. When the apostles delegated practical service to
deacons (a decision we looked at earlier this month), the reason they
gave was precisely to protect their own devotion to these two
priorities, not because practical service was unimportant, but because
nothing could substitute for the church's continual saturation in God's
word and continual dependence on him in prayer.

Everything else we've studied this month (worship, edification,
mission, the marks of a true church, right government, healthy
membership, faithful discipline) ultimately depends on these two
ordinary practices remaining central. A church can organise itself
perfectly and still stagnate spiritually if Scripture and prayer are
crowded out by lesser priorities. Conversely, a church poor in resources
or polish but genuinely saturated in the word and given to prayer has
everything essential to actually flourish.

This whole month has traced what the church is and how it functions.
Next month, we turn to the specific practices that embody and enact all
of it: baptism, the Lord's Supper, and corporate worship, where word and
prayer, the church's whole life, and Christ's own presence among his
gathered people, all come together.

## Prayer

Lord, keep your church, and keep me, devoted to your word and to prayer
above all else. Let every other good thing about church life flow from
these two, not crowd them out. Amen.
