## Verse

"Ah, Lord GOD! It is you who have made the heavens and the earth by your
great power and by your outstretched arm! Nothing is too hard for you."
— Jeremiah 32:17

## Explanation

Omnipotence means God possesses all power, and can do anything
consistent with his own character. Jeremiah prays these words in a
strange moment — he's just been told to buy a field in a city about to
fall to the Babylonians, an act of obedience that made no earthly sense
given the coming catastrophe. His response isn't to question the
strangeness of the command, but to anchor himself in the God giving it:
the same God who made the heavens and the earth, who has never once
needed anything to accomplish his purposes. If he made the universe from
nothing, a besieged city's fate is not beyond his reach.

The qualifier "consistent with his own character" matters. God's
omnipotence doesn't mean he can lie, sin, or act against his own nature
(2 Timothy 2:13, Titus 1:2) — those aren't limitations on his power, but
the natural boundary of what "power" even means for a being who is also
perfectly good and true. True omnipotence isn't the ability to do
absolutely anything imaginable; it's having all the power needed to
accomplish everything he actually wills.

Jeremiah's prayer models exactly how this attribute should function for
us: not as abstract theology, but as the anchor we reach for precisely
when circumstances look impossible.

## Prayer

Almighty God, nothing is too hard for you — not my circumstances, not my
fears, not the situations that look impossible from where I stand. I
anchor my trust in your power today. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What situation in your life right now needs you to remember that nothing
is too hard for God?
