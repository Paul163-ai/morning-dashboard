## Verse

"But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and
you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to
the end of the earth." (Acts 1:8)

## Explanation

Luke records this as Christ's last word before his ascension, and its
geography is deliberate: Jerusalem (home), Judea and Samaria (the wider
region, including people the disciples would rather have avoided), and
the end of the earth (everywhere else). Mission, on this pattern, isn't
a choice between local ministry and global reach; it is concentric, both
at once, starting where you already stand and expanding outward without
ever leaving the first circle behind.

This matters because it's tempting to treat "the end of the earth" as the
only real mission field, as though ministry to neighbours and coworkers
were a lesser calling than crossing oceans. Acts 1:8 refuses that
hierarchy. The same Spirit-given power that sent the apostles to Rome
first sent them next door, to their own Jerusalem. A church faithful in
its own Jerusalem, genuinely present to its own city, its own
neighbours, is exactly the kind of church equipped to also send people
to the ends of the earth, because the same power and the same
Spirit-given boldness are required for both.

## Prayer

Holy Spirit, give me power to be your witness, starting exactly where I
am, in my own Jerusalem, without waiting for a more distant calling to feel
significant. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where is your own "Jerusalem," the place closest to you where you're
called to witness, and are you as faithful there as you'd like to imagine
being somewhere more dramatic?
