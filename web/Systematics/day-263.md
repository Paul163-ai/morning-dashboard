## Verse

"For this reason I left you in Crete, that you might amend what was
defective, and appoint elders in every town as I directed you."
(Titus 1:5, again)

## Explanation

The third major pattern is episcopal government (from episkopos,
"overseer" or "bishop"): leadership organised through a hierarchy of
bishops holding authority over multiple churches within a region,
tracing a historic line of ordained oversight. Proponents point to
figures like Timothy and Titus, who appear to have held authority over
multiple churches and their elders within a region, as a New Testament
pattern anticipating this kind of broader, hierarchical oversight, later
formalised more fully across church history.

Each of these three patterns (congregational, presbyterian, episcopal)
finds genuine support in different New Testament passages, and faithful,
serious Christians across history have disagreed, in good conscience,
about which best reflects Scripture's overall pattern. This is a matter
where real conviction is appropriate, but where the kind of division
that would fracture Christian fellowship entirely is not warranted,
exactly the balance of purity and unity we studied earlier this month.

Rather than requiring you to declare one model definitively correct,
these next few days are better used to notice something common to all three: shared,
qualified, accountable leadership, always under Christ's own headship,
never isolated, unaccountable individual power. Whatever specific
structure your own church holds, that underlying pattern is worth valuing
and protecting.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the different, faithful ways your church has
organised itself across history. Give me conviction without division,
and help me value accountable, qualified leadership wherever I find it.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

Rather than deciding which government model is "correct," what
underlying value (accountability, qualified leadership, connection
beyond isolation) matters most to you, and why?
