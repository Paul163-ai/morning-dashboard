## Verse

"We look to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith, who for the
joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and
is seated at the right hand of the throne of God." (Hebrews 12:2)

## Explanation

There's a difference between fighting sin with sheer willpower and
fighting sin by faith, and the difference matters enormously for how
sustainable and how gospel-shaped the fight actually is. Willpower alone
treats sin primarily as a discipline problem: try harder, resist more
forcefully, grit your teeth. This can produce short-term results, but it
tends to breed either pride (when it works) or despair (when it
inevitably fails), because it never addresses the deeper issue: what the
sin is actually offering you that faith in Christ is meant to supply
instead.

The author of Hebrews points to a different strategy: looking to Jesus.
Sin usually promises something: comfort, pleasure, control, escape,
significance, and the way to genuinely starve it isn't just refusing the
sin in isolation, but actively finding something better to look to
instead: the joy set before Christ, the reality of his finished work, the
promises that make the sin's promises look thin and cheap by comparison.

This is what makes the fight sustainable long-term. Willpower fights
against sin. Faith fights sin by being captivated by something better.
The question worth asking isn't only "how do I stop this?" but "what is
this promising me, and where does Christ actually offer something far
greater?"

## Prayer

Jesus, help me fight sin not just by gritting my teeth, but by looking to
you, trusting that what you offer is genuinely better than whatever sin
is promising me. Amen.

## Reflection Question

For a sin you're currently fighting, what is it actually promising you?
Where does Christ offer something better in that exact area?
