## Verse

"So is it with the resurrection of the dead. What is sown is perishable,
what is raised is imperishable." — 1 Corinthians 15:42

## Explanation

In this section we turn to the most concrete piece of the Christian hope: not
merely the soul's future with God, which we studied under the
intermediate state, but the body's own future — a real, physical
resurrection, the reuniting of body and soul in a form suited for
eternity. Paul pictures this with a seed: buried like something dead and
finished, yet emerging as something recognisably continuous with what
was planted, and yet utterly transformed — perishable becomes
imperishable, dishonour becomes glory, weakness becomes power (15:42–43).

This matters because Christian hope has never been for a disembodied
eternity, however comforting that alone might sound. The body sown in
weakness and decay is the same body raised in power and glory — not
discarded and replaced with something unrelated, but transformed the way
a seed becomes a plant unrecognisable in its final form yet genuinely
continuous with what was buried. Whatever your body's current
weaknesses, sicknesses, or eventual decay, Paul's image promises they are
not the final word on it; they are simply the seed stage of something
this life has not yet seen.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that my body's weakness and decay are not its final
chapter. I look forward to a resurrection as real as the seed's growth
into what it was always meant to become. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What physical weakness, illness, or limitation in your own body do you
most long to see transformed at the resurrection?
