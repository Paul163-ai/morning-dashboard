## Verse

"On his robe and on his thigh he has a name written, King of kings and
Lord of lords." — Revelation 19:16

## Explanation

We turn now to Christ's third office: King. Where the prophetic office
centres on speaking God's word, and the priestly office on mediating
access to God, the kingly office centres on ruling — exercising
authority over all things. Revelation's vision, deliberately set at the
end of history, leaves no ambiguity about the scope of that rule: not
merely king over Israel, or king of the church, but King of kings and
Lord of lords — supreme over every other authority, ruler, or power that
has ever claimed allegiance.

This connects directly to Christ's ascension and session, which we
studied earlier this month. His seating at the Father's right hand
wasn't only the completion of priestly work; it was, at the very same
time, his enthronement as king, exercising real authority now, not only
at some future point still to come. Paul makes this present-tense
reality explicit: God "put all things under his feet" (Ephesians 1:22)
— a completed action, not merely a promise awaiting fulfillment.

This should reshape how we read the news, weigh political power, and
face circumstances that seem, on the surface, to be ruled by forces
hostile to God. Every throne, every ruler, every claim to ultimate
authority in this world exists under, and will one day visibly bow to,
the authority of the true King of kings.

## Prayer

King of kings, every earthly power exists under your authority. Let that
truth steady me against whatever seems, right now, to be ruling the
world around me. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What earthly power or circumstance do you need to remember is genuinely
under Christ's kingly authority, even when it doesn't look that way?
