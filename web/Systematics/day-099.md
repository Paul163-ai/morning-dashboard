## Verse

"So God created man in his own image, in the image of God he created
him; male and female he created them." (Genesis 1:27)

## Explanation

Having spent nearly a full quarter tracing who God is, we now turn to a
question that flows directly from it: what is a human being? Genesis
gives its answer at the very peak of the creation account: after light,
sky, land, plants, sun, moon, fish, birds, and animals, humanity is
created last, and uniquely, "in the image of God." No other creature
receives that description. Whatever else is true of us, our fundamental
identity is not defined by our usefulness, our achievements, or our
resemblance to the animal kingdom around us, but by this: we bear God's
own image.

Being made in God's image means, at minimum, that human beings reflect
God in ways nothing else in creation does (morally, rationally,
relationally, spiritually), genuinely resembling their Creator, though
never equal to him. This truth doesn't depend on ability, achievement, or
circumstance. It's true of the newborn, the elderly, the disabled, the
overlooked, exactly as fully as it's true of anyone the world considers
significant.

Notice too the deliberate inclusion: "male and female he created them."
The image of God is not carried by one sex more fully than the other;
both together, equally, bear it: a theme we'll pick up directly in a
few days.

## Prayer

Creator God, thank you that I bear your image, not because I've earned
it, but because you made me this way. Help me see every person I meet
today the same way you see them. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a person (including yourself) whose worth you've been
measuring by ability or achievement, rather than by being made in God's
image?
