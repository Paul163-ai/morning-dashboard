## Verse

"Let a person examine himself, then, and so eat of the bread and drink of
the cup." — 1 Corinthians 11:28

## Explanation

Paul wrote these words to a church that had turned a meal meant to
proclaim Christ's death into an occasion for selfishness and division —
some Corinthians going hungry while others got drunk, the wealthy eating
without waiting for poorer believers who arrived later. That is the
context for his warning about eating and drinking judgment on oneself: not
a call to sinless perfection before approaching the table, but a call to
take the meal seriously enough to examine whether one's life and
relationships actually match what the bread and cup proclaim.

Self-examination before the Supper, then, is not primarily about probing
for hidden sin to feel appropriately guilty. It is about asking honest
questions: Am I trusting Christ, or resting on something else? Am I
holding a grudge I need to release before I come to this table? Am I
treating fellow believers — especially those different from me, or with
less than me — the way this meal itself declares we belong to one
another? The Supper isn't for the flawless; it is for believers willing to
be honest.

## Prayer

Lord, search me before I come to your table. Show me anything I'm holding
onto that doesn't belong there — unbelief, grudges, contempt for a brother
or sister — and give me grace to lay it down. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Next time you take the Lord's Supper, what specific thing would honest
self-examination bring to the surface?
