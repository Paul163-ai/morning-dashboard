## Verse

"For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, so that each
one may receive what is due for what he has done in the body, whether
good or evil." (2 Corinthians 5:10)

## Explanation

This verse is written to Christians, which is worth pausing on — Paul
doesn't reserve final judgment as a topic only for unbelievers to worry
about. Every person, believer included, will appear before Christ's
judgment seat, and this appearing is described as universal ("we must
all") and comprehensive ("what he has done in the body"). No life, and no
deed within it, escapes this final accounting. That should be enough to
strip away any casualness about how we spend the years we're given.

But this judgment for believers is not the same as the judgment
determining eternal destiny, which the next few days will address more
directly; Romans 8:1 already promises "no condemnation for those in
Christ Jesus." What Paul describes here is better understood as an
evaluation of a life already secured — a settling of accounts that
determines reward and loss (1 Corinthians 3:12–15), not a re-litigation
of whether that life belongs to Christ at all. Even so, it is a real
appearing, before a real judge, and Paul's own response to that prospect
was not indifference but a driving motivation to live now in a way that
will hold up then.

## Prayer

Lord, I will stand before you one day and give an account. Let that
truth shape how seriously I take today, not out of fear of condemnation,
but out of love for you and a desire to please you. Amen.

## Reflection Question

If you pictured giving an account of today specifically to Christ this

you'd want to change?
