## Verse

"And we know that for those who love God all things work together for
good, for those who are called according to his purpose." (Romans 8:28)

## Explanation

Yesterday we sat honestly with the difficulty of the question; today we
look at what Scripture does reveal. Romans 8:28 doesn't say all things
*are* good — Paul knew suffering intimately and never pretended
otherwise. It says God works all things, including painful and evil
things, *together* toward good, for those who love him. The claim isn't
that evil is secretly fine; it's that nothing, however dark, falls
outside God's ability to redirect toward a good purpose.

We've already seen one clear biblical example this month: Joseph's
brothers meant evil, God meant it for good (Genesis 50:20). Scripture
gives others — Job's suffering deepening his knowledge of God rather
than merely punishing him; the greatest evil in history, the crucifixion
of the innocent Son of God, becoming the very means of the world's
salvation. These aren't offered as a complete inventory of every reason
evil exists, but as real, textual evidence that God's purposes are not
defeated by evil — they work through and beyond it.

This doesn't remove present pain, and Scripture never asks it to. It
offers something different: confidence that the pain is not the final
word, and that a good purpose, even one currently invisible, is not
merely hoped for but promised.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that you work even painful and evil things toward good
purposes for those who love you. Where I cannot yet see the purpose, help
me trust the promise. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Can you identify a past hardship where you've since seen God work it
toward good — and does that shape how you view a current one?
