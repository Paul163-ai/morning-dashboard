## Verse

"To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good." (1 Corinthians 12:7)

## Explanation

The purpose clause in this verse is easy to skim past and impossible to
overstate: spiritual gifts are given for the common good, not for the
gifted individual's benefit, status, or self-expression. This single
phrase rules out two opposite errors the Corinthians themselves were
falling into: treating gifts as a private spiritual experience to be
enjoyed regardless of whether anyone else benefited, and ranking believers
by which gifts they displayed, as though some gifts made a Christian more
important than another.

Every gift, on this definition, exists to serve someone other than the
person who has it. A teaching gift exists to build up hearers, not to
showcase the teacher. A gift of mercy exists to comfort the suffering, not
to make the giver feel useful. This reframes the question every believer
should ask about their own gifts, not "what do I enjoy doing" or "what
makes me feel spiritually significant," but "how is this actually serving
the people around me." A gift that never reaches anyone else has, in an
important sense, missed its purpose.

## Prayer

Lord, keep me from using whatever gifts you've given me for my own
recognition. Let every one of them actually reach and serve the people
around me. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Think of a gift or ability you have. Who, specifically, is it currently
serving, and is there someone it should be serving that it currently
isn't?
