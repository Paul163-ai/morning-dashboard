## Verse

"In him you also are being built together into a dwelling place for God
by the Spirit." (Ephesians 2:22)

## Explanation

We close this opening section on the church's nature with a third image,
this one carrying deep Old Testament weight: temple. Under the old
covenant, God's presence dwelt in a specific, physical location — first
the tabernacle, then the temple in Jerusalem, a single sacred building
where the presence of God uniquely rested among his people. Paul's claim
here is remarkable: that location has shifted entirely. God's dwelling
place now is not a building, but a people — the church, "built together"
collectively, indwelt by the Spirit.

Notice the plural, corporate nature of this image. While individual
believers are, in a related sense, also called temples of the Spirit
(1 Corinthians 6:19), this passage describes something bigger: the whole
gathered church, together, functioning as the place where God's manifest
presence particularly dwells and is particularly experienced, in a way
individual believers scattered apart do not fully realise on their own.

This adds real weight to gathering with other believers, a theme we'll
return to later this month. If God's presence is especially found among
his gathered people, then isolating yourself from the church isn't
simply missing a helpful social activity — it's missing something of the
particular way God has chosen to make his presence known.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for making your church, gathered together, your
dwelling place by your Spirit. Help me value gathering with your people,
not as optional, but as where your presence is particularly found. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you experienced a distinct sense of God's presence specifically
when gathered with other believers, different from being alone?
