## Verse

"So those who received his word were baptized, and there were added that
day about three thousand souls." — Acts 2:41

## Explanation

Every baptism recorded in the New Testament follows the same order: a
person hears the gospel, believes it, and is then baptized. Peter's sermon
at Pentecost, Philip and the eunuch, Paul and the Philippian jailer — in
every case, faith comes first, baptism second. The pattern is consistent
enough that many churches conclude baptism belongs properly to those who
can themselves repent and believe: a conscious response to a gospel they
have understood and embraced, not a rite performed on someone too young to
respond at all.

On this view, baptizing a professing believer isn't merely a preference
but a matter of preserving what baptism actually signifies. If baptism is
a sign of someone's own union with Christ through their own faith, then
administering it before that faith exists risks detaching the sign from
the reality it is meant to seal — the very danger Paul warned the
Corinthians about. Tomorrow we'll look fairly at the strongest case many
faithful Christians make for the other position, because this is a
question on which sincere, careful readers of the same Bible have long
disagreed.

## Prayer

Lord, whether I was baptized as a believer or as a child later come to
faith, let my baptism point clearly to my own trust in Christ, not to
anyone else's. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Can you articulate, in your own words, why believer's baptism connects the
sign so directly to personal faith? What would be lost if that connection
were loosened?
