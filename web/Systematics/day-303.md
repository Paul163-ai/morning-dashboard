## Verse

"Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and
let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord."
— James 5:14

## Explanation

James assumes the church has ordinary recourse to pray for healing, and
the New Testament records real, sometimes dramatic, instances of it. But
it also records the opposite: Paul left Trophimus sick at Miletus (2
Timothy 4:20), asked three times for his own thorn in the flesh to be
removed and was told no (2 Corinthians 12:7–9), and instructed Timothy to
use a little wine for his stomach rather than expecting instant healing (1
Timothy 5:23). Whatever we conclude about the gift of healing, the New
Testament itself refuses the assumption that enough faith guarantees
recovery.

The reason is the same "already, not yet" shape that runs through so much
of New Testament theology. Christ's resurrection has already broken the
power of sickness and death, and healing, where it happens, is a genuine
foretaste of the wholeness coming at the resurrection of the body. But
full, guaranteed healing is not yet: it belongs to the age still to come,
when death itself is finally undone (1 Corinthians 15:26). Until then, the
church prays in faith for healing, genuinely expecting God to act, while
trusting him with the answer when, like Paul, we don't get the one we
asked for.

## Prayer

Lord, I bring my prayers for healing to you in faith, trusting your power
and also your wisdom when the answer isn't what I hoped. Complete your
healing work fully on the day you promise. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there someone you've stopped praying for healing because it hasn't
come? What would it look like to keep praying in faith while trusting
God's timing?
