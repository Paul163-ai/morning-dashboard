## Verse

"None is righteous, no, not one; no one understands; no one seeks for
God. All have turned aside; together they have become worthless; no one
does good, not even one." — Romans 3:10-12

## Explanation

Paul stacks up quotation after quotation from the Psalms here, building
toward a verdict that leaves no room for exceptions: not one person is
righteous, not one truly seeks God on their own, not one does
unqualified good. This is the pervasiveness of sin stated in its
starkest form — not confined to particularly wicked people while others
remain neutral or basically good, but reaching every single person
without exception, including the person reading this devotional.

It's worth being honest about how uncomfortable this is to sit with.
Most of us instinctively sort humanity into rough categories — clearly
bad people, and people like us, who are flawed but fundamentally decent.
Romans 3 refuses that sorting entirely. There is no third category of
"basically fine" standing outside the verdict. Every person, examined
by God's own perfect standard rather than by comparison to others, falls
under exactly the same indictment.

This is deliberately the darkest point in Paul's argument, because it's
about to turn — the very next verses in Romans 3 introduce the
righteousness of God given freely through faith in Christ (Romans
3:21-22). The bleakness of "none is righteous" is what makes the good
news that follows actually good news, rather than a minor correction.

## Prayer

Lord, I have no claim to being fundamentally decent apart from you. I am
included fully in "none is righteous" — and I need the righteousness
only you can give. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Have you been quietly placing yourself in a "basically decent" category
that Romans 3 doesn't actually allow for?
