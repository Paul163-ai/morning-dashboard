## Verse

"Therefore, we are ambassadors for Christ, God making his appeal through
us. We implore you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God." — 2
Corinthians 5:20

## Explanation

An ambassador doesn't speak on their own authority or advance their own
agenda; they represent a sovereign who sent them, carrying a message that
isn't theirs to alter. Paul's chosen image for the church's mission is
deliberately not "salesperson" or "recruiter" but ambassador — someone
entrusted with an appeal that belongs entirely to the one who sent them.
The message itself, reconciliation with God through Christ, is not the
church's to soften, edit, or replace with something more palatable; it is
delivered on behalf of another.

This should relieve two common anxieties about evangelism. First, the
pressure to be persuasive rests on the wrong shoulders — an ambassador's
job is faithful delivery of the message, not manufacturing its
acceptance; the appeal is God's, made through the messenger, and its
reception belongs to him. Second, it should raise the stakes on
faithfulness: an ambassador who distorts the message they were sent to
carry has failed regardless of how well received their distorted version
was. Mission means both freedom from having to close the deal and
responsibility to carry the actual message.

## Prayer

Lord, let me carry your appeal faithfully, without watering it down to
make it more comfortable and without carrying the weight of results that
belong to you alone. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where have you felt pressure to either soften the gospel to make it more
acceptable, or to feel personally responsible for someone's response to
it? How does "ambassador" reframe both?
