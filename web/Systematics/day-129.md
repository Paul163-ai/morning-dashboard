## Verse

"Therefore he had to be made like his brothers in every respect, so
that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest in the service
of God, to make propitiation for the sins of the people." (Hebrews
2:17)

## Explanation

Having spent three days establishing Christ's full deity, we now turn
to the equally essential truth on the other side: Christ's full
humanity. "Like his brothers in every respect" is a sweeping statement:
not partially human, not human in appearance only, but genuinely,
completely human: a real body, a real human mind, real human
development, real human experience of hunger, fatigue, sorrow, and joy.

Early in church history, some taught that Christ only appeared to be
human, or that his humanity was somehow diminished by his deity, errors
the church firmly rejected, precisely because of verses like this one.
Hebrews is explicit about why full humanity mattered: it was necessary
for Christ to become a "merciful and faithful high priest," making
atonement for sin. A high priest who didn't genuinely share the human
condition he represented could not have represented it faithfully, or
sympathized with it mercifully.

Holding both truths together, Christ fully God, and Christ fully human,
rather than letting one soften the other is essential. He is not part
God and part man, nor is he God merely disguised as a man. He is fully
both, at once, which is precisely what made him the one mediator capable
of representing both God and humanity.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for being made like me in every respect. Let that
truth deepen my confidence that you truly understand my human
experience. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a part of your human experience (weariness, grief, temptation)
you've struggled to believe Christ genuinely understands?
