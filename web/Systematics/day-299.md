## Verse

"So Peter and John and the rest of the apostles... these are the signs
that will accompany those who believe." — Mark 16:17 (see also 2
Corinthians 12:12)

## Explanation

The strongest case for cessationism runs roughly like this: the most
dramatic gifts — apostleship, prophecy that produced Scripture-level
authority, tongues as a sign to unbelieving Israel, and confirming
miracles — served a specific, time-bound purpose: authenticating the
apostles' unique authority while the New Testament was still being
written and the church's foundation still being laid (Ephesians 2:20 calls
the church "built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets"). Once
that foundation was complete and the canon of Scripture closed, the
argument goes, these sign-gifts had accomplished their purpose and
receded, much as scaffolding is removed once a building stands on its
own.

Cessationists also point to the historical pattern: reports of
apostolic-level miracles and authoritative prophecy become markedly rarer
after the apostolic generation, which they take as evidence confirming
what the theology predicts, not merely coincidence. On this view, gifts
like teaching, service, mercy, and administration continue without
controversy, while the sign-gifts that authenticated new revelation were
always meant to be exceptional, tied to a particular redemptive-historical
moment that has now passed.
