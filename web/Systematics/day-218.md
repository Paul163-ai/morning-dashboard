## Verse

"For I do not do the good I want, but the evil I do not want is what I
keep on doing... For I delight in the law of God, in my inner being, but
I see in my members another law waging war against the law of my mind."
(Romans 7:19, 22–23)

## Explanation

If our recent picture of sanctification sounded like steady, easy
progress, today's reality check is necessary. Even Paul, deep into
Christian maturity, describes real, ongoing internal warfare: genuinely
wanting to do good, and finding sin still fighting against that desire,
sometimes winning. This is a normal, expected feature of the Christian
life this side of glory, not a sign that something has gone badly wrong
or that your faith isn't genuine.

Galatians 5:17 describes the same reality from another angle: "the
desires of the flesh are against the Spirit, and the desires of the
Spirit are against the flesh, for these are opposed to each other, to
keep you from doing the things you want to do." The Christian life
involves real conflict, not because grace has failed, but because two
genuinely opposed forces (the remaining pull of sin and the Spirit's
renewing work) are both active in every believer until glory finally
resolves the tension.

This should offer real comfort if you've grown discouraged by ongoing
struggle with a particular sin, wondering if it means your faith is
somehow deficient. It doesn't. It means you're a normal believer,
described accurately by Paul's own experience, still being sanctified,
not yet glorified.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that ongoing struggle with sin isn't proof my faith is
false, but the normal experience of every believer this side of glory.
Keep me fighting, without letting discouragement convince me to give up.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has ongoing struggle with a particular sin ever made you doubt the
genuineness of your faith? How does Paul's own experience in Romans 7
speak to that doubt?
