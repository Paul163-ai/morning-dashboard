## Verse

"After this I looked, and behold, a great multitude that no one could
number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages,
standing before the throne and before the Lamb... crying out with a loud
voice, 'Salvation belongs to our God who sits on the throne, and to the
Lamb!'" — Revelation 7:9–10

## Explanation

Every time the church gathers to worship, it is doing in miniature, and
imperfectly, what the whole redeemed people of God will do forever: stand
before the throne, together, praising the Lamb. That is a striking way to
think about even an ordinary, unremarkable Sunday service — not merely a
weekly obligation or a helpful habit, but a rehearsal for the reality
every believer is heading toward, a foretaste offered now of the worship
that will one day need no interruption, no distraction, and no end.

This should reframe how we approach the parts of corporate worship that
feel dull or difficult on a given week — the unfamiliar hymn, the sermon
that doesn't land, the awkward greeting of someone we don't know well.
None of it is the final form of what we're practicing for. But it is
practice, genuinely connected to what it anticipates, the way a rehearsal
is genuinely connected to the performance it prepares for. We gather now,
however imperfectly, because we are being trained for a gathering that
will never need to end.

## Prayer

Lord, let me see my church's gathering as a rehearsal for the worship of
every nation before your throne. Even on ordinary weeks, train my heart
for what is coming. Amen.

## Reflection Question

If this Sunday's gathering really is practice for eternal worship, does
that change how you'll approach it — your attention, your expectations,
your posture?
