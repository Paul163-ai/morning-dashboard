## Verse

"This Jesus, delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge
of God, you crucified and killed by the hands of lawless men." (Acts 2:23)

## Explanation

Few verses hold sovereignty and human responsibility together as tightly
as this one. Peter tells the Pentecost crowd, in the same breath, that
Christ's death happened exactly according to God's definite plan — not
an unfortunate accident God merely responded to — and that the people
listening had genuinely, culpably crucified him by their own choices and
hands. Both are stated as fully true, with no hint that one cancels the
other out.

This is the tension we've been building toward across the last several
days: God's sovereign governance over "all things" doesn't erase the
genuine reality of human choice and accountability. The crucifixion is
the clearest test case in Scripture, because it's the one event where
getting this wrong would have catastrophic implications — either God
becomes the author of the worst sin in history with no human culpability,
or the cross becomes a tragic accident outside God's actual plan. Acts
2:23 refuses both errors at once.

Scripture doesn't fully explain how divine sovereignty and human
responsibility fit together logically; it simply insists both are true
and holds them without apology. That's not evasion — it's appropriate
humility before a mystery bigger than our categories, matched with
confidence in what's clearly revealed.

## Prayer

Sovereign Lord, you ordained the cross, and yet those who crucified Christ
were truly responsible for what they did. Help me hold both your
sovereignty and my own responsibility without collapsing either. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where in your own life do you tend to lean too hard on either "God is in
control" or "this is my choice" — at the expense of the other?
