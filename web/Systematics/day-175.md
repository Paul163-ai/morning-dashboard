## Verse

"The Spirit of God has made me, and the breath of the Almighty gives me
life." (Job 33:4)

## Explanation

Yesterday we saw the Spirit hovering over creation's formless beginning.
Today's verse names something more specific and personal: the Spirit as
the giver and sustainer of life itself, in every living creature,
continuously, not only at one historical moment in the distant past. Job
speaks of his own individual life ("the Spirit of God has made me"),
tying the Spirit's creative work directly to his own personal existence,
right now, breath by breath.

This connects to what we studied in March about God's providence:
his ongoing sustaining of creation, moment by moment, rather than merely
launching it and stepping back. The Spirit's role in that ongoing
sustaining is specifically highlighted here: every breath you take,
every living thing that exists, continues to exist because the life-
giving Spirit continues actively sustaining it. Psalm 104:29-30 makes
the same point about the entire created order: when God's Spirit is
taken away, creatures die; when he is sent, they are created and the
face of the ground is renewed.

This should deepen ordinary gratitude in an unexpected way. Something as
basic and unnoticed as your next breath is not a fact about biology
running independently of God; it is, in this moment, the Spirit's own
ongoing, life-giving work.

## Prayer

Spirit of God, you give and sustain my very breath, moment by moment.
Let even this ordinary, unnoticed gift deepen my gratitude to you today.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

What would change if you consciously recognised your next breath as the
Spirit's ongoing, life-giving work, rather than an unnoticed biological
fact?
