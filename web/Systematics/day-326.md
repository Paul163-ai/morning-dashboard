## Verse

"He said therefore, 'A nobleman went into a far country to receive for
himself a kingdom and then return... Engage in business until I come.'"
— Luke 19:12–13

## Explanation

Jesus told this parable specifically because his listeners supposed the
kingdom of God was about to appear immediately (19:11) — the same
temptation every generation of Christians has faced, sure that their own
moment is surely the last one. His answer is not a date, but a task: the
nobleman's servants are entrusted with resources and given clear
instruction while he is away — not to speculate about his return date,
but to put what they've been given to work, faithfully, for as long as
the waiting lasts.

That is the shape readiness for Christ's return actually takes in
practice: not calculation, but faithful stewardship of whatever we've
been entrusted with — time, gifts, relationships, work — for however long
the interval before his return turns out to be. The servants in the
parable who are commended are not the ones who guessed correctly when
their master would come back; they're the ones who kept faithfully
working in the meantime, regardless of how long that meantime lasted.
Readiness looks like ordinary faithfulness, sustained, not extraordinary
prediction.

## Prayer

Lord, whatever the length of the wait, keep me faithfully occupied with
what you've entrusted to me, rather than distracted by trying to guess
your timing. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What has God entrusted to you right now — time, gifts, responsibilities —
that faithful "occupying until he comes" would mean actually using well?
