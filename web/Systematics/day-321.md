## Verse

"I saw under the altar the souls of those who had been slain for the word
of God... they cried out with a loud voice, 'O Sovereign Lord, holy and
true, how long before you will judge and avenge our blood?'" (Revelation
6:9–10)

## Explanation

John's vision confirms and extends what we read yesterday: the souls of
believers who have died are not merely present with Christ, they are
aware, active, and capable of speech — crying out to God, remembering the
injustice done to them, longing for God's justice to be completed. This is
a vivid picture of the intermediate state as genuinely conscious
existence, not vague or dormant, even while these same souls are still
waiting for something not yet given.

That waiting is itself instructive. God's answer to their cry is not
immediate vindication but patience: they are told to rest a little longer,
until the full number of their fellow servants is complete (6:11). Even
in the immediate presence of God, the departed believers pictured here
are shown still oriented toward the future — toward a resurrection, a
judgment, a vindication that has not yet arrived even for those already
with Christ. The intermediate state, however good, is genuinely
intermediate: real, conscious, and near to God, but not yet the final
chapter.

## Prayer

Lord, even your saints already with you are still waiting for your
justice to be complete. Teach me patience for what is not yet finished,
trusting your timing as they do. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What injustice are you currently waiting for God to make right? How does
picturing even departed believers still waiting for that same justice
shape your own patience?
