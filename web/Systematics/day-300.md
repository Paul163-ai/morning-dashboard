## Verse

"For now we see in a mirror dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in
part; then I shall know fully." — 1 Corinthians 13:12

## Explanation

The strongest case for continuationism starts exactly where we left off
yesterday: Paul ties the cessation of prophecy and tongues not to the
closing of the canon, a category he never mentions here, but to "the
perfect" — most plausibly Christ's return, when partial knowledge gives
way to face-to-face sight. Until that day arrives, the argument runs,
there is no clear biblical statement that any gift has been withdrawn; we
are still in the "now we see in a mirror dimly" era the whole passage
describes, and Scripture nowhere flags apostleship's sign-gifts as
uniquely expiring while other gifts continue.

Continuationists also point out that Paul's instructions for orderly use
of prophecy and tongues in 1 Corinthians 14 read as standing instructions
for the ordinary life of the church, not temporary provisions for an
apostolic transition period — and that church history, while showing
uneven and disputed reports, has never been entirely silent about these
gifts appearing beyond the first generation. On this view, the gifts
described in the New Testament remain available to the church until
Christ returns, though wisdom, testing, and biblical accountability
(1 Thessalonians 5:19–21) remain essential for discerning genuine
exercise of them from counterfeit or misuse.

## Prayer

## Reflection Question

"Test everything; hold fast what is good" (1 Thessalonians 5:21) applies
regardless of which view you hold. What would faithful testing actually
look like in your own church?
