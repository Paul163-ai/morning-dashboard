## Verse

"It is not right that we should give up preaching the word of God to
serve tables... Pick out from among you seven men of good repute, full of
the Spirit and of wisdom, whom we will appoint to this duty." —
Acts 6:2–3

## Explanation

Alongside elders, Scripture identifies a second office: deacons (from
diakonos, "servant"), responsible for the practical, physical care of
the congregation, freeing elders to focus on teaching and spiritual
oversight without becoming overwhelmed by administrative and practical
needs. Acts 6 describes the origin of this pattern directly: a practical
problem (the neglect of certain widows in daily food distribution)
solved by appointing specifically qualified people to that practical
task, rather than requiring the apostles to divide their attention away
from prayer and the ministry of the word.

This division of labour matters. It protects elders from burnout and
distraction, while dignifying practical service as a genuine, vital
ministry in its own right — not a lesser or secondary role, but essential
to the church's health, requiring its own real qualifications (1 Timothy
3:8–13): dignified character, tested faithfulness, sound doctrine held
with a clear conscience.

This should reshape how practical service within a church is valued.
Setting up chairs, organising meals for the grieving, managing finances,
caring for practical needs — none of this is "lesser" ministry compared
to teaching or preaching. It's the very pattern the apostles themselves
established as essential to a healthy, functioning church.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for deacons and every form of practical service in your
church. Help me value and support practical ministry as genuine ministry,
not just an afterthought to teaching. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a practical need in your church you could serve in, even
quietly and without recognition?
