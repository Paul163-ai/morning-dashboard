## Verse

"Only, they asked us to remember the poor, the very thing I was eager to
do." (Galatians 2:10)

## Explanation

When the apostles in Jerusalem confirmed Paul's gospel to the Gentiles,
the one practical instruction they added was to remember the poor, and
Paul's response is telling: not grudging compliance, but eagerness. From
its earliest days, the church's mission has held word and deed together,
proclaiming the gospel and caring for physical need, without treating
either as optional or as a distraction from the other. James is blunter
still: faith that sees a brother without clothing or food and offers only
words is, on its own admission, dead (James 2:15–17).

This doesn't collapse evangelism and mercy into the same thing: caring
for the poor is not itself the gospel, and meeting physical needs cannot
substitute for the message of reconciliation with God. But a church that
proclaims Christ while ignoring real suffering in front of it preaches a
gospel its own actions contradict. Mercy ministry authenticates the
message it accompanies; it doesn't replace the message, but it makes the
message credible from a people who actually live what they proclaim.

## Prayer

Lord, keep my compassion and my witness together, not competing. Let my
care for the suffering around me make the gospel I proclaim more
credible, not less urgent. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a form of need (poverty, loneliness, sickness) near you that
your words about the gospel haven't yet been matched by any action?
