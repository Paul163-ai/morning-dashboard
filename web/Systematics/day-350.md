## Verse

"And he who was seated on the throne said, 'Behold, I am making all
things new.'" — Revelation 21:5

## Explanation

After the sobering weight of the last several days, John's vision turns
toward the note this whole doctrine has been building to: renewal, not
destruction. Notice the verb carefully — not "I am making all new
things," discarding creation for a replacement, but "I am making all
things new," renewing what already exists. This distinction shapes
everything the coming days will consider: the Christian hope is not
escape from a physical world God made and called good, but the
restoration of that world to everything it was always meant to be.

This corrects a picture many Christians carry without examining it —
eternity as a disembodied, spiritual existence somewhere else, far from
this created order, as though the physical world were merely a temporary
stage to be discarded. Scripture's actual picture is renewal on the same
scale as creation itself: a genuinely physical, genuinely renewed earth,
still God's handiwork, still called good, purged of everything sin
introduced but not erased from existence. The God who first said
creation was very good has not changed his mind about it.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that your plan isn't to discard what you made but to
renew it. Give me hope for a real, physical future, not just an
abstract, disembodied one. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Has your own picture of eternity been closer to escape from this world,
or renewal of it? How does "I am making all things new" correct that?
