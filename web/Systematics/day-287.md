## Verse

"The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a participation in the blood
of Christ? The bread that we break, is it not a participation in the body
of Christ?" (1 Corinthians 10:16)

## Explanation

Between the extreme of a physical transformation and the opposite extreme
(that the Supper is a bare memorial, no different in kind from any other
act of remembering, a view associated with the Reformer Ulrich Zwingli)
lies a third position, held by Calvin and much of the Reformed tradition:
that Christ is truly, spiritually present at his table, and that believers
who eat in faith genuinely feed on him, not merely recall him. The word
Paul chooses, "participation" (or communion), points to something more
than memory; it points to a real fellowship with the risen Christ,
mediated by the Holy Spirit rather than by a change in the elements
themselves.

On this view, the bread and cup remain ordinary bread and wine in
substance. What changes is not the elements but the believer's experience
of Christ through them: by faith, and through the Spirit who unites us to
the ascended Christ, the Supper becomes a real means of grace, not a
magic transaction, and not an empty ritual either, but a genuine encounter
with the living Lord who instituted it, nourishing faith the way a meal
nourishes a body.

## Prayer

Lord, by your Spirit, feed my faith at your table. Let me leave it having
truly met with you, not merely having performed a ritual. Amen.

## Reflection Question

"Spiritual" presence can sound like a lesser kind of presence to modern
ears. Why might it actually be a fuller way of describing how Christ meets
his people?
