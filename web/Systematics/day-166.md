## Verse

"Since then we have a great high priest who has passed through the
heavens, Jesus, the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession... let
us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace, that we may
receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need." — Hebrews
4:14-16

## Explanation

We close this brief study of Christ's priestly office with its
practical, daily payoff. Hebrews draws together everything we've
covered — a permanent priesthood, a completed sacrifice — and lands on a
direct instruction: draw near, with confidence, to the throne of grace.
Not hesitantly, not fearfully checking whether you're currently
qualified to approach, but confidently, because the priest who ushers
you in has already made permanent, sufficient provision for exactly
that access.

Notice too the reason given for this confidence: not merely his
authority, but his sympathy, the very quality we studied back in May
(Hebrews 4:15, immediately following this passage) — a high priest who
was genuinely tempted as we are, yet without sin, and who therefore
understands, from the inside, exactly what it costs to approach God in
weakness. Confidence and sympathy aren't in tension here; they reinforce
each other. You can draw near boldly precisely because the one you're
drawing near through actually understands what you're bringing to him.

This is the everyday application of everything this section has covered:
a permanent, sympathetic priest, whose finished work means the throne of
grace is never further away than your next honest prayer.

## Prayer

Great High Priest, thank you for making it possible for me to draw near
to the throne of grace with confidence, not fear. Meet me there today
with mercy and help. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you approach God with the confidence Hebrews describes, or do you
still hesitate as though your access depended on your own worthiness?
