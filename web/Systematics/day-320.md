## Verse

"We are of good courage, and we would rather be away from the body and at
home with the Lord." (2 Corinthians 5:8)

## Explanation

Between a believer's death and the future bodily resurrection we'll study
in the days ahead lies what theologians call the intermediate state —
the condition of the soul between death and resurrection. Some traditions
have taught "soul sleep," an unconscious waiting until Christ's return,
but Paul's language here rules that out: to be away from the body is,
immediately, to be at home with the Lord, not to enter unconscious
suspension. Whatever else remains mysterious about the intermediate
state, Scripture presents it as conscious, and as genuine nearness to
Christ.

This is why Paul can call departure "gain" and "far better," as we read a
few days ago — not because the intermediate state is the final goal
Scripture holds out (that remains the resurrection body, still to come),
but because even this in-between condition already means being at home
with the Lord, closer to Christ than embodied life on earth currently
allows. The dying believer moves not into darkness or waiting, but into
conscious nearness to Christ himself, a genuine and immediate improvement,
even while something greater still lies ahead.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that death for your people is not unconsciousness or
distance, but immediate nearness to you. Let that truth steady me as I
think about my own death and the deaths of those I love in Christ. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does picturing the intermediate state as conscious nearness to Christ,
rather than sleep or waiting, change how you think about a believer who
has recently died?
