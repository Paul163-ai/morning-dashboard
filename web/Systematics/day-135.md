## Verse

"For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we
might become the righteousness of God." (2 Corinthians 5:21)

## Explanation

Having established Christ's full humanity, an important question
follows: did that humanity include the corruption we studied at length
last month? Scripture's answer is unambiguous: Christ was genuinely
human in every respect except sin. He faced real temptation, real
weariness, real human limitation, but never once fell short of God's
perfect standard, never once sinned in thought, word, or deed.

This matters enormously for the exchange Paul describes in this verse.
For Christ to be "made sin" on our behalf (bearing our guilt as a
substitute), he first had to be someone who "knew no sin" of his own.
A saviour with his own sin to answer for would have needed rescuing
himself, with nothing left over for anyone else. Christ's sinlessness
isn't merely an impressive moral achievement; it's the necessary
qualification for everything the cross accomplishes. Only a sinless
substitute could bear sin that wasn't his own and offer, in exchange, a
righteousness that wasn't ours.

This should also deepen how we understand his temptation, which we
looked at earlier this month. His sinlessness wasn't the absence of real
testing; it was real testing met with perfect, unbroken faithfulness,
every single time, all the way to the cross.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for living a sinless life so that your
righteousness could become mine. Let this exchange shape how I see
myself before God today. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you sometimes relate to Christ's sinlessness as distant perfection
rather than as the necessary foundation of your own righteousness before
God?
