## Verse

"For if righteousness were through the law, then Christ died for no
purpose." — Galatians 2:21

## Explanation

Paul draws out a sharp implication from yesterday's theme: if there were
any other genuine path to righteousness before God — through law-keeping,
through moral effort, through anything humanity could supply — then
Christ's death would have been pointless, a needless sacrifice for a
problem that already had a solution. Paul states this almost as a test:
the very existence of the cross is proof that no other way was
available.

This should settle a question that quietly undermines confidence in the
gospel for many people: could I have somehow gotten right with God
through enough effort, enough sincerity, enough good deeds, if I'd just
tried hard enough? Galatians 2:21 answers plainly — no. If that had been
possible, Christ would not have needed to die. The cross is not one
generous option among several viable paths to God; it stands as the
necessary answer precisely because every other path had already, and
permanently, failed.

This should be liberating rather than discouraging. It means your
standing before God was never actually up to your own performance to
begin with — not because effort doesn't matter in the Christian life,
but because righteousness before God was never obtainable through it.
The cross was necessary because nothing else could have worked, and
because it worked, nothing else needs to.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that Christ's death proves no other way to righteousness
existed. Free me from any lingering hope in my own effort to earn what
only the cross provides. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a part of your life where you're still quietly trying to earn
what Christ's death already accomplished?
