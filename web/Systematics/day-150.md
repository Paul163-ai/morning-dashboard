## Verse

"When Jesus had received the sour wine, he said, 'It is finished,' and he
bowed his head and gave up his spirit." — John 19:30

## Explanation

Three words, in the original Greek a single word (*tetelestai*), spoken
at the very moment of Christ's death. It's worth being precise about
what this word actually meant in everyday first-century use: it was
stamped on paid bills and completed debts, meaning "paid in full." Jesus
does not say "I am finished," as though merely exhausted or defeated. He
says the work itself is finished — completed, accomplished, requiring
nothing further.

This single word settles something the rest of this month has been
building toward. The penalty Isaiah described being laid on him, the
curse Galatians described him becoming, the ransom paid for our
release, the victory over hostile powers — all of it was, at the cross,
fully and finally completed. Nothing remained to be added, by Christ or
by anyone else. There is no partial payment awaiting our contribution,
no unfinished balance we're still working off through good deeds or
religious effort.

This should settle any lingering anxiety about whether your standing
before God depends on you keeping the ledger balanced. It doesn't.
Christ himself declared the debt paid in full, with his final breath,
before he had even risen to prove it. The resurrection, which we look at
next, is not the completion of an unfinished work, but the vindication
of a work already, truly, finished.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, "it is finished" — paid in full, nothing left for me to add.
Let me rest today in a completed work, not an ongoing debt. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a part of your life where you're still quietly trying to add to
a debt Christ already declared finished?
