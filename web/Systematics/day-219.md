## Verse

"For if you live according to the flesh you will die, but if by the
Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live."
(Romans 8:13)

## Explanation

Given yesterday's real, ongoing struggle, what's the actual posture the
Christian life calls for: passive endurance, waiting for the fight to
end? Paul's word here is active and violent: put to death. Theologians
call this mortification: deliberately, decisively killing specific sinful
patterns rather than merely managing, tolerating, or negotiating with
them. Sin isn't something to slowly starve into submission while still
feeding it in smaller doses. It's something to actively execute.

This is deliberately strong language, and it's meant to be. A sin
pattern left half-alive tends to regain strength. Paul elsewhere uses
similarly stark imagery: "put to death therefore what is earthly in
you" (Colossians 3:5), naming specific sins directly, one by one, rather
than vaguely gesturing at "sin" in general. Real mortification usually
means naming the specific thing (a particular temptation, habit, or
pattern) and taking specific, decisive action against it, not just
feeling generally sorry about sin as an abstract category.

Notice too that this putting to death happens "by the Spirit": this
isn't self-generated willpower alone, but active cooperation with the
Spirit's own power at work in you, the same pairing of grace and effort
we saw in sanctification generally.

## Prayer

Holy Spirit, give me courage to actually put specific sin to death,
rather than merely managing or tolerating it. Empower my effort with your
strength, and don't let me settle for half-measures. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a specific sin pattern in your life you've been managing rather
than actually putting to death? What would decisive action look like?
