## Verse

"Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, for we all
partake of the one bread." (1 Corinthians 10:17)

## Explanation

Paul draws a conclusion from the Supper that is easy to miss: because
every believer eats from the same loaf, the meal doesn't just unite each
individual to Christ; it unites believers to one another. This is why the
Lord's Supper has almost never, across church history, been a private
devotional exercise. It is by nature a corporate meal, eaten together,
declaring that the people gathered around this table belong to each other
as much as they belong to Christ.

That is also why the Supper functions as a means of grace not only
vertically but horizontally. Coming to the table alongside believers we
find difficult, believers from different backgrounds, believers wealthier
or poorer than us, is itself a small enacted sermon: the ground at this
table is level, and the bread that unites us to Christ also binds us to
each other, whether we would have chosen these particular people or not.
Together, baptism and the Supper form the two ordinary, physical acts by
which Christ continually confirms his gospel to his gathered people,
which brings us, next, to that gathering itself: why the church
meets at all.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for a table that unites me not only to you but to
believers I might never otherwise have chosen as family. Teach me to love
them as you have loved me. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a believer at your church's table who is genuinely different from
you in background, age, wealth, or culture? What would it look like to
let the Supper actually draw you toward them?
