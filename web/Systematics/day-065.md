## Verse

"For God is not a God of confusion but of peace." (1 Corinthians 14:33)

## Explanation

Paul writes this in the middle of correcting chaos in Corinth's worship
gatherings — people talking over one another, no order, no one able to
follow what was happening. His argument isn't merely practical ("this is
inefficient"); it's theological. Worship gone chaotic misrepresents the
God being worshipped, because God himself is not a source of confusion.
His actions, his purposes, his self-revelation are ordered, coherent, and
consistent — not scattered or self-contradicting.

This attribute — sometimes named God's "order" — is easy to overlook
next to more dramatic ones like power or holiness, but it quietly
underwrites almost everything else we believe about him. It's why
Scripture can be trusted to say the same true things consistently rather
than contradicting itself. It's why creation displays intelligible laws
rather than random chaos. It's why a promise God makes in Genesis can
still be counted on in Revelation. A confused God could offer none of
this.

It also has a practical edge for us. If God is a God of peace and order,
then a life or a church marked by constant chaos, confusion, and
disorder isn't neutral — it's out of step with the very character it
claims to represent. Order isn't legalism; it's a reflection of whose we
are.

## Prayer

God of peace, thank you that you are never a source of confusion. Where
my life feels chaotic today, help me trust that your purposes for me
remain ordered and clear, even when I can't yet see them. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where in your life right now does chaos need to give way to God's order
and peace?
