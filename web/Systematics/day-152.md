## Verse

"And Joseph took the body and wrapped it in a clean linen shroud and
laid it in his own new tomb, which he had cut in the rock. And he
rolled a great stone to the entrance of the tomb and went away."
(Matthew 27:59-60)

## Explanation

We open June exactly where May left off, at the cross, and take the
next deliberate step: the burial. It would be easy to rush past this
detail on the way to the resurrection, but Scripture doesn't rush past
it, and neither should we. Matthew records it with careful, almost
legal precision: a real body, wrapped, placed in a specific, identifiable
tomb, sealed with a stone. This isn't incidental detail: it's evidence.
Christ's death was not a fainting spell or an appearance of death; he
was genuinely, verifiably dead, buried the way any dead body is buried.

This matters more than it might first appear. Without a real death,
there is no real resurrection to speak of, only recovery from
something that was never fatal to begin with. The burial anchors
everything that follows in solid historical ground: a known tomb,
known witnesses, a stone that would need to be explained if it were
later found rolled away. Paul lists the burial as one of the core facts
of the gospel itself, right alongside the death and resurrection (1
Corinthians 15:3-4), not a minor detail, but a necessary link in the
chain.

As we spend this month tracing Christ's resurrection, ascension, and
ongoing work, we start here: the tomb was real, the body was real, and
the death that made the resurrection possible was completely, verifiably
genuine.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for a death and burial as real and historical as my
own will be. Let the solid ground of what actually happened anchor my
faith today. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does thinking carefully about the historical reality of Christ's burial
strengthen your confidence in the resurrection that follows it?
