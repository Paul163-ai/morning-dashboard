## Verse

"What no eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor the heart of man imagined, what
God has prepared for those who love him." (1 Corinthians 2:9)

## Explanation

Every human attempt to picture heaven runs into the same limit: our
imagination can only rearrange things we've already experienced —
brighter versions of sunsets we've seen, deeper versions of joys we've
already tasted. Paul's point here is more radical than "heaven will be
very good." It's that heaven exceeds the category of things imagination
can extrapolate from at all — not because it's vague or undefined, but
because it's genuinely beyond what unaided human experience, however
extensive, could construct on its own.

This should temper both extremes that heaven-talk tends toward. It
shouldn't be reduced to sentimental clichés — golden streets and clouds,
imagined as slightly nicer versions of earthly luxury — because Paul is
explicit that our categories fall short of what's actually prepared. But
neither should it remain so abstract that it fails to stir real longing;
Paul mentions this precisely to those who love God, as a promise meant to
be anticipated, not merely acknowledged as doctrinally true. What we
cannot yet picture, we can still trust and long for, on the word of the
one who has prepared it.

## Prayer

Father, I can't picture what you've prepared, but I trust you and long
for it. Stir real anticipation in me for what exceeds anything I could
imagine. Amen.

## Reflection Question

When was the last time you actually let yourself long for heaven, rather
than just believing in it as a doctrine?
