## Verse

"Let us rejoice and exult and give him the glory, for the marriage of the
Lamb has come, and his Bride has made herself ready." (Revelation 19:7)

## Explanation

Scripture closes its account of eternal fellowship with God not with the
language of a courtroom or a kingdom alone, but with the language of a
wedding — the most intimate, joyful, permanent union human experience
offers as an image. Christ is the bridegroom; the church, gathered from
every nation across every age this whole year has traced, is his bride,
finally presented to him, ready, adorned, and joined to him forever. Every
other picture of eternal fellowship we've considered in this section — seeing
his face, dwelling with him, no mediating temple — is gathered up here
into the picture of a marriage actually consummated.

This is where the whole story ends, appropriately, at joy: not muted
relief that judgment has passed, not merely the absence of what made this
life hard, but active, exultant celebration — rejoicing, exulting, giving
glory, at a wedding that has finally arrived after every other wedding in
this life could only ever be a distant, imperfect echo of it. Tomorrow,
on the year's very last day, we'll look back over everything this whole
365-day study has traced and let it settle into the single word that
outlasts every doctrine: love.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for preparing your bride, and for the wedding day
that is coming. Let my heart rejoice now in anticipation of the joy that
day will finally bring. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Of every image of eternity this section has offered — seeing his face,
dwelling with him, no more mediation — does "wedding" capture something
the others don't? What does it add for you?
