## Verse

"But all things should be done decently and in order." — 1 Corinthians
14:40

## Explanation

Christians have long disagreed about how much detail Scripture actually
prescribes for a worship service. The regulative principle, associated
with much of the Reformed tradition, holds that a church should include
only what Scripture explicitly commands or clearly warrants by example —
word, prayer, song, ordinances — and be cautious about adding elements
merely because they seem helpful or moving. The normative principle,
associated with Lutheran and Anglican traditions among others, holds that
a church may include anything Scripture does not forbid, provided it
serves genuine worship and is done in good order.

In practice, the gap between these views is often narrower than the labels
suggest, since 1 Corinthians 14 itself shows Paul giving both freedom
(prophecy, tongues, hymns, teaching — a whole list of contributions from
the gathered congregation) and constraint (all of it done "decently and in
order," for building up the church, not for confusion or display). Whatever
principle a church holds, the New Testament's clearest instruction is not
a rigid liturgy but a purpose: everything that happens when the church
gathers should be ordered toward building up the body, not toward
spectacle or self-expression.

## Prayer

Lord, whatever form my church's gathering takes, let it genuinely build up
your people rather than merely fill time or entertain. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Think of your own church's typical service. Which elements most clearly
build up the body, and is there anything that feels more like filler?
