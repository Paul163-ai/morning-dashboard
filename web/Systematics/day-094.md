## Verse

"So Elisha prayed and said, 'O LORD, please open his eyes that he may
see.' So the LORD opened the eyes of the young man, and he saw, and
behold, the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire all around
Elisha." — 2 Kings 6:17

## Explanation

Elisha's servant wakes to find the city surrounded by an enemy army and
panics. Elisha's response isn't denial of the danger, but a claim about a
reality his servant simply couldn't see: "those who are with us are more
than those who are with them" (2 Kings 6:16). When Elisha prays for his
servant's eyes to be opened, what's revealed isn't a different set of
facts on the ground — the enemy army is still there — but an entirely
unseen dimension of the same situation, filled with God's own angelic
forces, vastly outnumbering the visible threat.

This story is a vivid picture of something true in every situation we
face, whether or not our eyes are ever opened to see it the way the
servant's were. Angels are not confined to biblical narrative as a
literary device; Scripture treats their activity as ongoing and real,
present in circumstances that, from our vantage point, look
overwhelming or hopeless. Psalm 91:11 promises the same truth in
different words: "he will command his angels concerning you to guard
you in all your ways."

We're not meant to go looking for angels, or to treat their presence as
a formula guaranteeing safety from every hardship. But Elisha's prayer
gives us a pattern: when circumstances look like they're surrounded, ask
God to help you see what's actually, truly there.

## Prayer

Lord, open my eyes, like Elisha's servant, to see that you surround me
with more than I can perceive. Where I feel overwhelmed today, remind me
the visible threat is never the whole picture. Amen.

## Reflection Question

What situation currently feels overwhelming to you, and how might
remembering the unseen reality around you change how you face it?
