## Verse

"And Mary said to the angel, 'How will this be, since I am a virgin?'
And the angel answered her, 'The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the
power of the Most High will overshadow you; therefore the child to be
born will be called holy—the Son of God.'" — Luke 1:34-35

## Explanation

The virgin birth is one of the most disputed claims in Christian
history, and one of the most clearly stated in Scripture. Luke, a
careful historian who tells us he investigated everything closely (Luke
1:1-4), records Mary's own honest question and the angel's direct
answer: this conception would happen entirely by the Holy Spirit's
power, without a human father. This is not a poetic flourish or a
legend accumulated over time — Luke presents it as a specific historical
claim, made to a specific, understandably confused young woman.

The virgin birth sits at the intersection of everything else we're
studying this month. It's the means by which the eternal Son could take
on a genuine human nature (born of Mary, truly human) while that nature
began entirely by the Spirit's miraculous act, not ordinary human descent
(avoiding, among other things, transmission of Adam's inherited guilt in
the ordinary way, though Scripture doesn't fully spell out every
mechanism here). It is, in short, the historical hinge on which full
deity and full humanity could be united in a single conception.

Mary's response to the angel is worth noting too: not demanding further
proof, but simple submission — "let it be to me according to your word"
(Luke 1:38). A remarkable model of faith facing something entirely
beyond ordinary explanation.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for the virgin birth, the historical means by which your
Son took on true humanity. Give me Mary's faith to trust what I cannot
fully explain. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does treating the virgin birth as a real historical event, rather than
symbolic language, change how you read the Christmas story?
