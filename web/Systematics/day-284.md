## Verse

"For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the
Lord's death until he comes." (1 Corinthians 11:26)

## Explanation

The Supper looks in three directions at once, and this verse captures all
three. It looks backward, remembering Christ's death as already
accomplished. It looks outward, in the present tense, as an act of
proclamation: every time a church eats this meal together, it is
preaching the gospel to itself and to any watching world, whether or not
a single word of explanation is spoken. And it looks forward, to Christ's
return, when the meal itself will no longer be necessary because the
Bridegroom it anticipates will finally be present.

That forward-looking edge matters as much as the backward-looking one. At
the Last Supper, Jesus told his disciples he would not drink of the fruit
of the vine again "until that day when I drink it new in the kingdom of
God" (Mark 14:25). Every observance of the Supper, then, is also a small
act of hope, a rehearsal dinner for a wedding feast still to come, eaten
by a people who know the meal is temporary because the reality it
anticipates is not.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, as I remember your death, give me hope for your return. Let
this meal keep me looking forward, not only backward, until the day you
drink it new with us. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you tend to experience the Lord's Supper mainly as remembrance, or also
as hope for Christ's return? What would change if you held both together?
