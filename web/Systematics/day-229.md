## Verse

"By this we know that we have come to know him, if we keep his
commandments." (1 John 2:3)

## Explanation

Alongside the Spirit's inward witness, John gives concrete, outward tests
throughout his letter: obedience to God's commands (2:3), love for other
believers (3:14), and belief in who Jesus truly is (5:1). These aren't
requirements you must perfectly meet to earn assurance, as though
salvation itself depended on passing a test; that would contradict
everything we established about justification by faith alone. They're
evidence, fruit that naturally grows from genuine faith, useful for
confirming, not creating, the reality already present.

This means assurance rests on three legs held together: the objective
promises of Scripture (God's word says whoever believes has eternal
life), the inward witness of the Spirit (a settled, Spirit-given
confidence), and the outward evidence of a changed life (growing, however
imperfectly, in obedience and love). Relying on only one of these can
create problems: feelings alone can waver, fruit alone can breed either
pride or despair depending on how it's measured, and Scripture's promises
alone, without any inward or outward confirmation, could theoretically be
claimed by someone with no actual faith at all.

Held together, though, these three provide sturdy, well-grounded
assurance: not perfect performance, but genuine evidence that the
Spirit's work described throughout this section (regeneration, sanctification,
perseverance) is actually, really present in you.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for giving me multiple, sturdy grounds for assurance:
your promises, your Spirit's witness, and the fruit growing in my life.
Help me rest confidently on all three together. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Which of the three grounds for assurance (Scripture's promises, the
Spirit's witness, or the fruit of your life) do you lean on most? Which
do you tend to neglect?
