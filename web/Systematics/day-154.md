## Verse

"But in fact Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of
those who have fallen asleep." (1 Corinthians 15:20)

## Explanation

Paul uses an agricultural image familiar to his readers: firstfruits,
the first portion of a harvest, gathered as a guarantee that the rest of
the crop would follow. Applied to Christ's resurrection, this is a
striking claim: his rising from the dead wasn't a unique, isolated
miracle unrelated to anyone else's future. It was the first installment
of a harvest still to come: the resurrection of everyone united to him.

This closes our short look at Christ's burial and resurrection with a
forward-looking note. What happened to Christ's body in that tomb
(genuinely dead, genuinely raised, genuinely transformed) is the pattern
and the guarantee of what will happen to every believer. This isn't
resurrection as a spiritual metaphor for a changed inner life; it's the
promise of an actual, future, physical resurrection, following the same
pattern as the firstfruits already gathered.

Paul makes the logic explicit a few verses earlier: if Christ has not
been raised, our faith is futile and we are still in our sins (1
Corinthians 15:17). But because he has been raised, our own future
resurrection isn't a hopeful guess: it's as certain as the fact that a
harvest follows its firstfruits.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, your resurrection guarantees my own. Let that certain future
hope shape how I face today, including whatever losses or fears about
death I carry. Amen.

## Reflection Question

How does thinking of Christ's resurrection as "firstfruits" (a
guarantee of your own future resurrection) change how you think about
death?
