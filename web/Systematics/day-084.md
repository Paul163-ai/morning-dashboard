## Verse

"As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to
bring it about that many people should be kept alive, as they are today."
— Genesis 50:20

## Explanation

Joseph speaks these words to the very brothers who sold him into slavery
— years of suffering, injustice, and separation from his family, now
seen in full light. His statement is a masterclass in holding two truths
at once, without either one softening the other: "you meant evil" (a
genuine moral verdict on his brothers' real, sinful choice) and "God
meant it for good" (the very same event, governed by God toward a
redemptive purpose). Same action, same history, two entirely truthful
descriptions operating at different levels.

Theologians sometimes describe this using the language of primary and
secondary causes: God's sovereign purpose is the primary cause working
through the real, free, and morally accountable choices of people as
secondary causes. The brothers weren't puppets acting out a script with
no will of their own — they freely chose evil, and are held fully
responsible for it. And yet their choice, without ceasing to be theirs,
served a purpose only God could have woven through it.

This gives us a model for our own suffering and others' wrongdoing
against us. We're never asked to pretend the wrong wasn't wrong. We're
invited to trust that even genuine evil, freely chosen by real people, is
never outside the reach of a God who can work it toward good (Romans
8:28).

## Prayer

Lord, like Joseph, help me name real wrong as wrong without excusing it,
while trusting that you can work even what was meant for evil toward
good I cannot yet see. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is there a wrong done to you that you need to both name honestly as evil
and entrust to God's redemptive purposes?
