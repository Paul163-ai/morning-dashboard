## Verse

"For the love of Christ controls us, because we have concluded this: that
one has died for all, therefore all have died." — 2 Corinthians 5:14

## Explanation

Mission run on the wrong fuel eventually stalls or curdles. Guilt burns
out — a mission driven by shame at not doing enough collapses the first
time it fails to produce results. Duty alone hardens into resentment.
Ambition for a church's own growth or reputation corrupts the very message
it claims to carry. Paul names the only fuel that actually sustains
mission across a lifetime: the love of Christ, specifically his love
demonstrated in dying for us, controlling and compelling everything that
follows.

Notice the logic Paul gives: Christ's death for all leads him to conclude
that "all have died" — the old self-interested way of measuring life is
over, replaced by a life lived, as the next verse says, "for him who for
their sake died and was raised" (5:15). Mission, on this account, isn't
an obligation added on top of the Christian life; it's the natural
overflow of a heart that has genuinely grasped what Christ's love cost
and can no longer live only for itself. Where mission feels like a duty
to grit through, that's usually a sign this fuel has run low, not that the
calling was wrong.

## Prayer

Lord, let your love, not guilt or duty, be what moves me toward mission.
When I feel depleted, draw me back to what your death actually cost and
accomplished. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Honestly, what has been fueling your own sense of mission lately — guilt,
duty, ambition, or genuinely the love of Christ? What would it take to
return to the right fuel?
