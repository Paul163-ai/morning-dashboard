## Verse

"And he who sent me is with me; he has not left me alone, for I always
do the things that are pleasing to him." (John 8:29)

## Explanation

Christ's sinlessness, which we studied over the last two days, wasn't
merely the absence of failure; it was the positive, continuous presence
of obedience, actively pleasing his Father in every circumstance, not
occasionally under ideal conditions. "I always do the things that are
pleasing to him" is a claim no other person in Scripture makes about
themselves, and it's made not as boasting but as simple, settled fact
about the shape of his whole life.

This obedience covered far more than avoiding obvious sin. It meant
active submission at every point: to his Father's timing (John 7:6), to
his Father's mission even when it meant rejection and hardship, to
ordinary human authorities where appropriate, to the specific path laid
out for him rather than an easier alternative he could have chosen.
Obedience, for Christ, was not a single decisive act but the continuous
texture of an entire life.

This has direct relevance for how we think about our own obedience.
Christ's example isn't only a moral standard to admire from a distance;
it's the obedience credited to us through union with him (a theme we
will return to later this year), and the pattern the Spirit is at work
forming in us, moment by moment, not merely in isolated big decisions.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you always did what pleased your Father, in every
circumstance. Shape my obedience today to be as continuous as yours, not
just occasional. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Is your own obedience to God more like a continuous pattern, or
something you turn on only for the "big" decisions?
