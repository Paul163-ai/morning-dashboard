## Verse

"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness,
goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control." — Galatians 5:22–23

## Explanation

If you want to know what the Spirit's filling actually looks like in
practice, Paul gives a concrete, visible list: fruit, not a single item,
but a connected cluster, growing together the way branches on a healthy
tree produce many kinds of fruit from the same root system. Notice too
the word "fruit," singular, despite the list of nine qualities — this
isn't nine separate gifts you might have some of and lack others, but one
unified, integrated character, growing together as the Spirit's ongoing
work.

Fruit, by its nature, grows slowly and organically, not through sheer
effort forcing it into existence overnight. You don't manufacture love,
joy, or self-control through willpower alone, the way you might force a
particular action. You cultivate the conditions — abiding in Christ,
walking in step with the Spirit (Galatians 5:25), staying connected to
the root — and the fruit grows, gradually, as evidence of that
connection, not as an achievement separate from it.

This gives a practical, ongoing way to check your own walk with the
Spirit: not by measuring dramatic spiritual experiences, but by asking
whether this fruit — however imperfectly, however slowly — is actually
growing in your ordinary, daily life and relationships.

## Prayer

Spirit of God, grow your fruit in me — not through my forced effort, but
through my staying connected to Christ. Show me where this fruit is
still thin in my life, and help me abide more closely in him. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Of the nine qualities Paul lists, which feels least developed in your
life right now? What might it look like to stay more closely connected
to Christ in that specific area?
