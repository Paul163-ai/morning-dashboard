## Verse

"Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints." — Psalm
116:15

## Explanation

This is a strange verse to sit with honestly. "Precious" is not the word
we would naturally reach for to describe death, even a believer's death —
we might expect "sad," or "necessary," or "the doorway to something
better." But the psalmist, writing centuries before the fuller
resurrection hope of the New Testament, already grasped something the
rest of Scripture confirms: when one of God's own people dies, God does
not look on with detached efficiency, processing an unavoidable
transition. He regards it as precious — valuable, tender, weighty to him.

Part of what makes a believer's death precious to the Lord, in context, is
what the psalm has already said: God has "loosed my bonds" (116:16) and
delivered the psalmist's soul from death itself in a fuller sense. The
death of a saint is precious partly because it is not the disaster it
appears to be from this side — it is a doorway into the very presence the
psalmist has spent the whole psalm praising God for, watched over by a
God who does not lose interest in his people the moment their life on
earth ends.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that my death, whenever it comes, will not be something
you regard carelessly. Let that tenderness comfort me now, and comfort me
when I grieve the deaths of those I love who trusted you. Amen.

## Reflection Question

How does it change your view of a loved one's death to think of God
regarding it as precious rather than merely sad or inevitable?
