## Verse

"Shall not the Judge of all the earth do what is just?" — Genesis 18:25

## Explanation

Abraham asks this question while bargaining with God over Sodom's fate —
pleading for the city to be spared if even a handful of righteous people
can be found in it. What's remarkable isn't just Abraham's boldness in
questioning God directly, but the assumption underneath the question:
that appealing to God's justice is a safe and reasonable thing to do,
because justice is exactly what God can be counted on to deliver. Human
courts can be bribed, intimidated, or swayed by status. God's justice
answers to none of that.

Justice is righteousness applied specifically to how God treats people —
rewarding good and punishing evil impartially, without favouritism toward
the powerful or prejudice against the poor (Deuteronomy 10:17). This is
part of why the biblical prophets could confront kings without fear:
their authority to speak truth to power rested on a Judge higher than any
throne, one whose verdicts couldn't be purchased.

It's also why the cross had to happen the way it did. A God who could
simply overlook sin without consequence wouldn't be just — and Abraham's
own question would have a troubling answer. Instead, God's justice was
satisfied at the cross (a theme we'll return to this month), so that
mercy toward us never had to come at the cost of justice being ignored.

## Prayer

Just Judge of all the earth, thank you that your justice can never be
bribed or overlooked. Help me trust your verdicts, even when I don't yet
see them carried out. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where do you need to trust God's justice rather than take matters into
your own hands?
