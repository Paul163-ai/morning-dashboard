## Verse

"As each has received a gift, use it to serve one another, as good
stewards of God's varied grace." (1 Peter 4:10)

## Explanation

Peter's phrasing assumes something worth stating plainly: every believer
has received a gift. Not most believers, not the especially devoted ones:
each. This means the question for any Christian is never really "do I
have a spiritual gift," as if that were in doubt, but "have I identified
it, and am I using it." Gifts are discovered, not manufactured, usually
through some combination of trying to serve, noticing where that service
actually helps others, and receiving honest feedback from people who know
us well.

Peter's language of "stewardship" is significant too. A steward manages
something that belongs to someone else, for that owner's purposes, and
will one day give an account of how it was used. Spiritual gifts, on this
picture, are never really possessions: they are entrusted, temporarily,
to be deployed on behalf of the true owner, the "varied grace" of God
himself. That should relieve any pressure to feel proud of a prominent
gift or ashamed of a modest one; the point was never ownership, only
faithful use of what was lent.

## Prayer

Father, thank you for entrusting me with gifts to steward, not to own.
Help me identify what you've given me and use it faithfully for others,
whether it feels prominent or ordinary. Amen.

## Reflection Question

If you're not sure what your spiritual gift is, who in your life could you
ask, someone who has watched you serve and could tell you honestly where
it actually helps?
