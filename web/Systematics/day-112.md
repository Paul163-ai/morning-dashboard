## Verse

"Therefore, just as sin came into the world through one man, and death
through sin, and so death spread to all men because all sinned."
(Romans 5:12)

## Explanation

We turn now to a harder, often misunderstood doctrine: original sin,
not merely that everyone happens to sin individually, but that Adam's
first sin had consequences reaching every one of his descendants. Paul's
logic here is careful: sin entered through "one man," and death (sin's
consequence) spread to all, "because all sinned." Adam wasn't simply
the first sinner in a long line of unrelated individual failures; he
was, in some real sense, humanity's representative head, whose sin
brought consequences to the whole race he represented.

This can sound, at first, deeply unfair: why should later generations
bear consequences for one ancestor's choice? Yet notice Paul is setting
up an argument he'll finish two verses later, contrasting Adam with
Christ (Romans 5:18-19, a theme we'll return to shortly): the very same
principle of representative headship that condemned us in Adam is what
saves us in Christ. If we reject the logic of one man's action
affecting many in Adam's case, we lose the very same logic that secures
our salvation through Christ's obedience. The doctrine that troubles us
in Romans 5:12 is the same doctrine that rescues us by verse 19.

## Prayer

Lord, I don't fully understand how one man's sin reached me, but I trust
that the very same principle secures my salvation in Christ. Thank you
for that greater rescue. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does understanding that Adam's headship and Christ's headship work by
the same principle change how troubling Romans 5:12 feels to you?
