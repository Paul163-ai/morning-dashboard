## Verse

"How then will they call on him in whom they have not believed? And how
are they to believe in him of whom they have never heard? And how are
they to hear without someone preaching?" — Romans 10:14

## Explanation

Paul's chain of questions makes a simple but easily overlooked point: no
step in the chain can be skipped. Calling on Christ requires believing;
believing requires hearing; hearing requires someone actually speaking.
God could, in theory, have chosen to save people apart from any human
messenger — but he has ordinarily chosen not to. The ordinary means by
which the gospel reaches anyone is another person, willing to open their
mouth and say something true about Christ out loud.

This is a sobering thought precisely because it removes the excuse that
someone else, more qualified or more gifted, will surely say it instead.
Paul calls the feet of those who bring good news "beautiful" (Romans
10:15, quoting Isaiah 52:7) — not because speaking the gospel is
naturally comfortable or graceful, but because God delights in the
ordinary, often awkward act of one believer telling another person the
truth about Christ. The chain from unbelief to faith runs, ordinarily,
through exactly that kind of unremarkable courage.

## Prayer

Lord, give me courage to actually speak, not just to hope someone else
will. Make my feet, however ordinary and awkward, beautiful in bringing
your good news. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Who is someone in your life who has likely never heard the gospel clearly
from anyone — and what's keeping you from being the one who tells them?
