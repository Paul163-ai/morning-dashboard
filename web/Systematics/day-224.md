## Verse

"My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me. I give them
eternal life, and they will never perish, and no one will snatch them out
of my hand." (John 10:27–28)

## Explanation

In this section, we turn to a doctrine that offers deep security to genuine
believers: the perseverance of the saints — the teaching that those who
are truly born again will be kept by God's power all the way to final
glory, never ultimately and finally falling away from salvation. This
isn't a claim about human strength or consistency; it's a claim about
God's own faithful preservation of what he has begun.

Jesus states it with striking confidence: eternal life, once genuinely
given, results in never perishing — not "probably," not "as long as you
maintain sufficient effort," but a settled promise, backed by the
security of being held in his own hand, from which no external force can
snatch you away. This isn't a passive doctrine that removes human
responsibility, as we'll see in the days ahead — genuine believers do actively
continue in faith and obedience. But the ultimate guarantee of that
continuing rests on Christ's own grip, not on the strength of your own
grip on him.

This should be a source of profound comfort, especially on days when your
own faith feels weak or your own faithfulness feels inconsistent. Your
security was never actually resting on the strength of your hold. It
rests on the strength of his.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you that my security rests in your hand, not in the
strength of my own grip on you. When my faith feels weak, let me rest in
your promise that none of your sheep will ever be snatched away. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does your sense of security in Christ tend to rest more on your own
consistency, or on his promise to hold you? What would it look like to
shift that?
