## Verse

"Jesus answered, 'Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born of
water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God.'" (John 3:5)

## Explanation

We turn now to our final theme this month, and this year's study of
Christ and the Spirit together: the Spirit's work specifically in the
believer, beginning with regeneration, the new birth. Jesus tells
Nicodemus, a respected religious teacher, that entry into God's kingdom
requires something no amount of moral effort or religious knowledge can
produce: being "born of the Spirit," a genuinely new spiritual life,
given by the Spirit himself, not achieved by human effort.

This connects directly to April's study of sin's total corruption of
mind, will, and emotions. If sin has left every human faculty affected,
then no self-generated effort, however sincere, could produce the
spiritual life needed to enter God's kingdom. Regeneration is the
Spirit's solution to precisely that problem: not helping an already-
living spiritual capacity improve itself, but giving genuinely new life
to what was, spiritually, dead (Ephesians 2:1). It is entirely his
gracious, sovereign act, "like the wind" Jesus describes moments later
(John 3:8): real, powerful, but not something we control or produce.

This should ground your confidence in a very particular place. If you
have genuine faith in Christ today, it is because the Spirit himself has
already done something in you that you could never have done for
yourself.

## Prayer

Holy Spirit, thank you for giving me new life I could never produce for
myself. Let my confidence rest in your gracious work, not my own effort.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you ever quietly credit your own spiritual growth or decision to
believe to your own effort, rather than to the Spirit's regenerating
work?
