## Verse

"I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever abides in me and I in him,
he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing."
(John 15:5)

## Explanation

Union with Christ isn't only a past-tense reality (chosen, crucified,
raised with him); it's also an ongoing, present, daily relationship,
which Jesus describes through the image of a vine and its branches. A
branch doesn't produce fruit by trying hard in isolation; it produces
fruit by remaining connected, drawing life continuously from the vine it
was never separate from in the first place. Cut off from that connection,
Jesus says plainly, a branch "can do nothing"; no amount of independent
effort substitutes for the ongoing, moment-by-moment dependence real
abiding requires.

This ties together everything we've traced about sanctification and the
fight against sin earlier this month. Growth, fruit, victory over sin:
none of it comes from sheer willpower operating apart from Christ. It
comes from continuously, actively abiding: staying connected through
prayer, through Scripture, through ongoing, conscious dependence on him,
the way a branch simply, continuously draws sap from the vine it's
attached to.

This is a helpful daily check. When effort toward holiness starts to
feel exhausting, isolated, and self-generated, it's often a sign that
abiding has quietly slipped, and independent striving has taken its
place. The invitation, always, is back to connection, not more effort in
isolation.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, keep me abiding in you today, not striving in isolation, but
drawing continuously on your life. Apart from you, I can do nothing;
connected to you, let fruit grow naturally. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does your current pursuit of growth feel more like abiding
(continuous connection) or striving (isolated effort)? What would it look
like to shift toward abiding today?
