## Verse

"And to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all
peoples, nations, and languages should serve him; his dominion is an
everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom one
that shall not be destroyed." — Daniel 7:13-14

## Explanation

Yesterday we saw Christ's kingship as a present, already-established
reality. Daniel's ancient vision, given centuries before Christ's
birth, adds an important companion truth: this everlasting dominion,
though real now, has not yet been made visible to every eye, or fully
acknowledged by every nation, language, and people. Christ reigns
truly, but the world at large has not yet universally recognised or
submitted to that reign the way Daniel's vision anticipates it one day
will.

This tension — a kingdom genuinely present, yet not yet fully and
visibly realised — is sometimes summarized as "already, but not yet."
Christ already reigns as king over all things, seated at the Father's
right hand; and yet a day is still coming when every rebellious power
will be visibly, finally subdued, and his kingdom made unmistakably
plain to every eye (a theme this year's final section, on the future,
will return to). Living faithfully in the meantime means neither
despairing as though Christ's reign were only a future hope with no
present reality, nor expecting every circumstance now to already
display his kingdom's full, visible triumph.

This is a necessary corrective for anyone discouraged by how little the
world currently seems to acknowledge Christ's rule. The kingdom is real
now. Its full, visible display is still to come.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, your kingdom is real now, even where it isn't yet visibly
acknowledged. Help me live faithfully in the "already, but not yet,"
without despair or false expectation. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where do you find it hardest to trust that Christ's kingdom is real,
given how little it's currently visible in that situation?
