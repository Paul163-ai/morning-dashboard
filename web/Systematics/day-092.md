## Verse

"You do not have, because you do not ask." — James 4:2

## Explanation

James states this almost bluntly: some blessing God intended to give was
never received, simply because it was never asked for. This is a
remarkable statement sitting right alongside everything Scripture says
about God's sovereign governance of all things. If God's will is settled
regardless of us, how could our failure to ask actually leave something
undelivered? James's answer, consistent with yesterday's theme, is that
asking is not an optional extra tacked onto God's plan — it's frequently
built into the plan itself as the means through which the intended
blessing arrives.

This has practical weight for how we approach the ordinary requests of
daily life — provision, guidance, healing, the wellbeing of people we
love. It would be easy to reason our way out of asking: "if God wants to
give it, he will, whether I ask or not." James cuts directly against
that passivity. Some things genuinely sit unreceived, not because God
was unwilling, but because his ordained means — our asking — never
happened.

This doesn't reduce God to a vending machine dispensing whatever we
request. James goes on immediately to address wrong motives in asking
(James 4:3). But the core instruction stands: bring your genuine needs
to God, expecting that your asking is not incidental to receiving, but
part of how it comes.

## Prayer

Father, forgive me for the times I've quietly assumed my asking doesn't
matter to what you're already going to do. Teach me to bring my genuine
needs to you, trusting that you have made my prayers part of your plan.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

What request have you been carrying silently, without actually bringing
it to God in prayer?
