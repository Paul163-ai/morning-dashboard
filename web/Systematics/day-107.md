## Verse

"So, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do all to the glory of
God." — 1 Corinthians 10:31

## Explanation

Yesterday's truth — that we exist for God's glory — could easily stay
abstract, reserved for obviously "spiritual" moments like worship or
prayer. Paul deliberately picks the most mundane example available:
eating and drinking. If glorifying God only applied to sacred moments,
Paul would never have chosen the dinner table to make his point. Instead,
he insists that literally everything — including the parts of life that
feel too small or routine to matter spiritually — falls under this same
purpose.

This is enormously freeing for anyone who has quietly assumed their
ordinary work, chores, or daily routines are spiritually irrelevant
compared to "real" ministry. Paul gives no such hierarchy. The context of
this verse is actually a question about eating meat sacrificed to
idols — about as unglamorous and specific a dilemma as Scripture
addresses — and Paul's answer zooms out to the widest possible
principle: whatever you're doing, right now, however small, can be done
for God's glory or not.

This reframes an ordinary day entirely. The commute, the meal, the
laundry, the meeting, the conversation with a difficult colleague — none
of it sits outside the purpose we were made for. The glory of God isn't
found only in extraordinary moments; it's available in literally
everything, simply by doing it as unto him.

## Prayer

Lord, help me see today's ordinary tasks — eating, working, resting — as
opportunities to glorify you, not as separate from my spiritual life.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

What ordinary task later today could you deliberately do "to the glory
of God" rather than on autopilot?
