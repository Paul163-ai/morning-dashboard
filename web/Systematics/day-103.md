## Verse

"Then the LORD God said, 'It is not good that the man should be alone; I
will make him a helper fit for him.'" — Genesis 2:18

## Explanation

Yesterday we established equal dignity between men and women; today we
look at real distinction, which Genesis presents as good, not as a
regrettable complication to equality. Before Eve is created, God
declares something "not good" for the first time in the creation
account — Adam's aloneness — and the solution isn't a duplicate of Adam,
but a "helper fit for him": someone corresponding to him, distinctly,
not identical to him.

The word "helper" deserves a moment, since in English it can sound
subordinate or menial. The same Hebrew word is used of God himself as
Israel's helper throughout the Old Testament (Psalm 121:1-2) — a
term describing a role, not a rank. Eve's distinctness from Adam isn't
a lesser version of him; it's precisely what makes her the answer to his
aloneness, someone genuinely other, genuinely corresponding, not a
copy.

This is a helpful pattern for thinking about male and female more
broadly: real, God-designed distinction, existing alongside — never in
competition with — equal dignity and equal image-bearing. Difference
isn't evidence of inequality, and equality doesn't require erasing
difference. Genesis holds both together from its very first description
of human relationship.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you for designing men and women with real, purposeful
distinction, not despite equal dignity but alongside it. Help me
value difference without turning it into inequality. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Where might you be tempted to treat difference between people —
whether of sex or otherwise — as a threat to equality, rather than as
part of God's good design?
