## Verse

"...concerning his Son, who was descended from David according to the
flesh and was declared to be the Son of God in power according to the
Spirit of holiness by his resurrection from the dead." (Romans 1:3-4)

## Explanation

We close this brief look at Christ's two natures by asking why any of it
matters practically. Paul's own summary here traces both lines at once:
descended from David "according to the flesh" (genuinely human, part of
a real human genealogy) and declared Son of God in power "according to
the Spirit of holiness" through the resurrection (genuinely, eternally
divine). Both were necessary for the salvation Paul is about to describe
in the rest of Romans.

Christ needed to be fully human to represent humanity: to obey where
Adam failed, to die a death that could actually stand in our place, to
rise as the first of a truly human resurrection. He needed to be fully
God for that obedience, death, and resurrection to carry infinite worth,
sufficient to atone for sin against an infinite God, and to guarantee
the resurrection he promises to those united to him. Take away either
nature, and the whole structure of salvation collapses: a merely human
saviour couldn't bear the weight; a merely divine one, without a genuine
human nature, couldn't represent us or genuinely die in our place.

This is why the church has always insisted, sometimes at real cost, on
holding both natures fully and simultaneously. It isn't abstract
precision for its own sake; it's the very thing that makes the gospel
actually work.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, your full humanity and full deity together are what make my
salvation possible. Thank you for being everything you needed to be to
rescue me. Amen.
