## Verse

"And when he had said these things, as they were looking on, he was
lifted up, and a cloud took him out of their sight." (Acts 1:9)

## Explanation

Forty days after the resurrection, the risen Christ ascends bodily into
heaven, an event easy to treat as a brief transitional scene between the
resurrection and Pentecost, but one Scripture treats as genuinely
significant in its own right. Notice the physical, witnessed nature of
it: the disciples watch it happen, in real time, and two angels
immediately promise he will return "in the same way you saw him go"
(Acts 1:11): a bodily departure implying a bodily return.

The ascension confirms something important about the nature of Christ's
resurrected body: not a spirit released from physical existence, but a
genuinely physical, transformed body, now taken up to a real place,
heaven, where it remains even now. This matters for how we picture the
risen Christ today: not a vague spiritual presence dissolved into the
universe, but a specific, glorified, embodied person, reigning from a
particular place, who will return, bodily, exactly as he left.

The ascension also marks the end of Christ's period of bodily,
localized presence on earth and the beginning of something new: his
reign from heaven and, soon, the sending of the Spirit to be with his
people in a way his earthly, localized body could not (John 16:7, a
theme we'll return to later this month).

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you were taken up bodily into heaven, and you will return
the same way. Let that certain hope shape how I wait for you today.
Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does picturing Christ as a specific, embodied person reigning from a
real place, rather than a vague spiritual presence, change how you
think about him?
