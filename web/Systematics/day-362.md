## Verse

"Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them,
and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their
God." — Revelation 21:3

## Explanation

This sentence is the oldest promise in Scripture reaching its final,
permanent fulfilment. "I will be their God, and they shall be my people"
runs like a thread through the whole Bible — spoken to Abraham, renewed
at Sinai, promised again through the prophets to a scattered and exiled
people, and here, at last, declared complete and unbreakable. Every
partial, interrupted experience of God's presence across redemptive
history — the garden lost, the tabernacle veiled, the temple destroyed,
even the incarnate Christ ascending out of sight — was a step toward
this: God dwelling with his people, permanently, without interruption or
mediation.

This is worth letting settle as the true centre of everything the
doctrine of the future has been describing. Not primarily a place, though
it is that; not primarily an absence of suffering, though that too is
promised; but this: God himself, present with his people, forever. Every
other promise in this final section of Scripture — no more tears, no
curse, a renewed creation, resurrected bodies — serves this one central
reality. The whole story of the Bible ends exactly where it should: with
God and his people, together, for good.

## Prayer

Father, thank you for keeping the promise you made from the very
beginning — to be my God, and to make me your people, forever. Let this
be the hope beneath every other hope I hold. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Of everything this year has taught you to long for in eternity, does
"God dwelling with his people" feel like the centre of it, or has
something else quietly taken that place in your imagination?
