## Verse

"Speaking the truth in love, we are to grow up in every way into him who
is the head, into Christ." — Ephesians 4:15

## Explanation

We close this section where purity and unity actually meet: truth held
together with love, neither sacrificed for the other. It's possible to
pursue purity in a way that fractures unity unnecessarily — treating
every secondary disagreement as a battle for the gospel itself, quick to
divide, slow to extend charity. It's equally possible to pursue unity in
a way that quietly compromises purity — avoiding necessary correction or
disagreement for the sake of surface-level peace, unwilling to say
anything might actually be wrong.

Paul's phrase, "speaking the truth in love," refuses both errors. It
assumes real truth exists and matters enough to be spoken, even when
inconvenient or uncomfortable — purity taken seriously. And it insists
that truth always be spoken within love, with genuine care for the
person and the relationship, not weaponised as a tool for winning
arguments or asserting superiority — unity taken seriously, too.

Practically, this means learning to distinguish core gospel truths,
worth real division over if genuinely compromised, from secondary
matters where believers can, and should, remain united despite real
disagreement. Getting this discernment right — what's worth dividing
over, and what isn't — is one of the most important, and most difficult,
skills any healthy church, and any healthy Christian, needs to develop.

## Prayer

Lord, teach me to speak truth in love — never sacrificing truth for
false peace, never sacrificing love for harsh correctness. Give me
wisdom to know what's actually worth dividing over. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Can you think of a disagreement you've had within the church where truth
and love were both genuinely present? What made that possible?
