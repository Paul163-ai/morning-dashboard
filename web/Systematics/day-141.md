## Verse

"But he was pierced for our transgressions; he was crushed for our
iniquities; upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace, and
with his wounds we are healed." — Isaiah 53:5

## Explanation

Having established that the cross was necessary, we now examine its
nature more closely: what actually happened there. Isaiah, writing
centuries before Christ, describes it with startling precision —
pierced, crushed, chastised — but notice the crucial detail woven
through every clause: "for our transgressions," "for our iniquities,"
the chastisement "that brought us peace." This is substitution: one
person bearing the penalty that belonged, by right, to someone else.

This is the heart of what happened at the cross. Christ did not merely
suffer alongside us in sympathy, or set a moral example of self-sacrifice
we should imitate, though both are true in a secondary sense. He
suffered instead of us, taking the punishment our sin deserved so that
it would not fall on us. The exchange is total: he receives what was
ours (guilt, punishment, death), and we receive what was his (peace,
healing, life) — an exchange the rest of this month will keep exploring
from different angles.

Isaiah 53 is worth returning to often, because it names both the cost
and the outcome in the same breath: real suffering, genuinely borne by
him, and real peace and healing, genuinely given to us. Neither half
makes sense without the other.

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you were pierced for my transgressions and crushed for my
iniquities. Thank you for bearing what I deserved so I could receive
what I never earned. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Do you tend to think of the cross more as Christ's example to follow, or
as Christ's substitution on your behalf? How does Isaiah 53 correct an
imbalance either way?
