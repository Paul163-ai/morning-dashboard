## Verse

"For no prophecy was ever produced by the will of man, but men spoke
from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit." (2 Peter
1:21)

## Explanation

We close this look at the Spirit's work across creation and history with
perhaps its most consequential effect on our own lives: the inspiration
of Scripture itself. Peter's description is careful: not that human
authors simply wrote their own religious reflections which happened to
be roughly accurate, but that they were genuinely "carried along" by the
Spirit as they wrote, producing words that were truly, fully their own
and, at the very same time, truly, fully God's own words.

This ties directly back to where this whole year of devotionals
began, the doctrine of Scripture we studied in January: its necessity,
authority, inerrancy, clarity, and sufficiency. All of that rests on
this claim about the Spirit's work: Scripture is not merely wise human
reflection about God that we happen to find helpful. It is the Spirit's
own superintended product, carrying his own authority into every word.

This should deepen our confidence every time we open the Bible. The same
Spirit who hovered over creation, sustains every breath, and equipped
Bezalel for skilled work is the very Spirit who carried the biblical
writers along, producing the words you read each morning as this
devotional draws from Scripture, day after day, month after month.

## Prayer

Holy Spirit, thank you for carrying along the writers of Scripture so
that their words are truly your own. Let that confidence deepen every
time I open your Word. Amen.

## Reflection Question

Does remembering the Spirit's role in Scripture's inspiration change how
you approach reading it day to day?
